# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

## Arc 8 — Crescendo

# Chapter Sixty-Seven — My Hero

*Bell's POV*

---

Things were not the same anymore.

Not necessarily because the world had changed, nor because I had suddenly developed a different perspective on everything. Sometimes, people simply changed at different speeds. What seemed perfectly ordinary to one person could become something entirely different to another.

Take Lefiya, for example.

I think she has a crush on me.

Just saying.

I'm not particularly unfamiliar with romance. I've had enough exposure to understand the general principles behind it, even if I wouldn't exactly consider myself an expert. It's not some mysterious disease that suddenly eats away at your brain. Well, depending on the person, perhaps it could.

At its simplest, attraction is a response to certain stimuli. Familiarity, admiration, affection, physical appearance, shared experiences, or perhaps the rather unfortunate combination of all five.

The important thing is that I am not obligated to solve that particular mystery.

If Lefiya wants to tell me something, she can approach me herself. I'm not going to interrogate her simply because she occasionally behaves strangely whenever we're together.

Besides, jumping to conclusions is a terrible habit.

And I have considerably more important things to worry about.

Like tomorrow's sparring session with Ais.

That was interesting.

She had approached me earlier with an unusually direct request. She wanted to spar. Not a demonstration, not a casual exchange of techniques, but an actual training session.

I could think of several reasons.

Perhaps she wanted to understand how I had become stronger. Perhaps she wanted to measure my current abilities, examine my fighting style, or determine how much I could adapt against someone of her caliber.

All perfectly reasonable explanations.

However, there was something else I had noticed.

Ais had been watching me rather closely.

Not necessarily in a suspicious manner. It was more like someone observing a puzzle they couldn't quite solve.

I understood that feeling.

After all, strength was not merely a matter of physical improvement. Training your body and learning to use your strength were two entirely different things.

There was also the matter of understanding yourself.

An adventurer could spend years improving their technique, refining their movements, and increasing their physical capabilities without ever understanding what truly motivated them to fight.

And that was a problem.

Especially for someone like Ais.

She was extraordinarily talented. Her swordsmanship was refined, her instincts were exceptional, and her experience was far beyond what most adventurers could hope to achieve.

But strength without understanding was a dangerous thing.

Sometimes, the most important lesson an adventurer could learn had absolutely nothing to do with fighting.

Perhaps I could help her with that.

Not by teaching her another sword technique.

But by helping her understand how to express herself.

That was tomorrow's problem.

For now, I wanted to sleep.

***

The morning arrived.

Or, at least, I assumed it was morning.

Apparently, the night had lasted approximately five hours.

Five.

I stared at the faintly glowing crystals embedded in the surrounding rock formations.

"...Seriously?"

The crystals illuminated the area with an intensity that made distinguishing between morning and evening rather difficult. Their light was consistent, and the surrounding environment offered very little indication of the passage of time.

I had almost forgotten how inconvenient underground environments could be.

The natural body clock was apparently not part of their design philosophy.

I stretched my arms and sat up.

Around the campsite, the expedition was beginning to stir. Several people were already preparing breakfast, while others were organizing equipment and checking their surroundings.

The usual morning routine.

Except something was different.

The training ground.

There was movement coming from that direction.

I rubbed my eyes.

Then I looked again.

Oh.

Right.

The sparring session.

And apparently, it had already started.

I slowly stood up, brushing the remaining traces of sleep from my clothes.

"Well, that's interesting."

Ais was already waiting.

And she was armed.

I looked toward her weapon.

Desperate.

A rather appropriate name, considering the circumstances.

She held the sword with both hands, her posture composed and her expression almost completely unreadable. Even from a distance, I could tell she was prepared to begin.

I glanced at myself.

Then at her.

Then back at myself.

"...I should probably get ready."

I reached into my spatial storage.

There were plenty of weapons available. Some were designed for monsters, others for armored opponents, and a few were simply experimental projects that I hadn't found a practical reason to use.

But there was one weapon I had in mind.

A rapier.

Its polished surface reflected the ambient crystal light, revealing a deep crimson hue beneath its metallic finish. The guard was relatively simple, though its construction was considerably more complicated than its appearance suggested.

Crimson Order.

I held it in front of me.

For a moment, my thoughts drifted elsewhere.

This weapon had been made for someone else.

A goddess.

Astrea.

I had forged it after learning about the tragedy that had befallen her Familia. An expedition, a terrible encounter, and a trap that had cost her nearly everything.

I wasn't particularly good at offering comforting words.

Nor was I particularly fond of giving people meaningless encouragement.

So I had decided to make something instead.

A weapon was not going to restore what she had lost. It wasn't going to bring back her children or undo the circumstances that had led to their deaths.

But perhaps it could serve as a reminder.

That losing something precious did not necessarily mean the end of one's journey.

That there was still a reason to stand again.

The rapier was primarily constructed from synthetic mithril, reinforced with orichalcum components. Producing it had been considerably more difficult than I initially anticipated.

The material composition required careful balancing. Too much mithril would compromise certain structural properties, while excessive orichalcum would make the weapon unnecessarily difficult to work with.

It had taken several attempts before I was satisfied.

Unfortunately, Astrea had rejected my offer.

Not rudely.

Not ungratefully.

She had simply refused to accept something that she believed she had no right to receive.

I hadn't pressed the matter.

The weapon had remained in my storage ever since.

And now?

Well.

There wasn't much point letting a perfectly good weapon collect dust.

Besides, my previous opponents had either possessed exceptionally heavy armor or simply hadn't warranted the use of something this refined.

A rapier was not particularly suitable for crushing heavily armored targets.

But against a swordswoman?

That was an entirely different matter.

I rotated the weapon in my hand.

The balance was excellent.

The point was sharp.

And the blade was more than capable of surviving the amount of force I intended to apply.

"Sorry, Astrea."

I glanced toward the training ground.

"I'll borrow it for a while."

I began walking.

Ais watched me approach.

Her gaze briefly shifted toward the weapon in my hand.

Then returned to my face.

"You're late."

I stopped a few steps away.

"Good morning to you too."

She blinked.

"...Good morning."

I smiled.

"Better."

For a moment, she simply stared.

Then her eyes returned to Crimson Order.

"That sword?"

"Rapier."

"Why?"

"Because you brought a sword."

She looked down at Desperate.

Then at my weapon.

"...They're different."

"Exactly."

Ais seemed to consider that answer.

I continued.

"You're already familiar with your weapon. You know its weight, its balance, and how it responds to your movements. There's no reason for me to make things easier by using something you're already accustomed to fighting against."

"You're using a disadvantage?"

"Not necessarily."

I raised Crimson Order slightly.

"Just a different set of advantages."

She nodded.

Then she adjusted her grip.

I noticed the subtle change in her posture.

Her shoulders relaxed.

Her knees bent slightly.

Her breathing became quieter.

The expression on her face remained almost unchanged, but her eyes had sharpened.

There she was.

The Sword Princess.

And I had a feeling she wasn't going to make this easy.

***

### The First Exchange

We stood approximately ten meters apart.

Neither of us moved.

Ais was studying my stance.

I could almost see the calculations happening behind those golden eyes. She was measuring the distance between us, observing my grip, examining the angle of my shoulders, and trying to determine which direction I would move first.

It was an excellent habit.

A swordsman who merely watched an opponent's weapon was already at a disadvantage.

The weapon could deceive.

The body was considerably more difficult to lie with.

I relaxed my shoulders.

My right foot shifted slightly backward.

Ais's eyes followed the movement.

There.

She had noticed.

I smiled.

"Ready?"

She nodded.

"Yes."

I took a breath.

And attacked.

There was no warning.

My foot struck the ground, and the distance between us disappeared almost immediately.

Crimson Order flashed.

Ais reacted.

Desperate rose diagonally, preparing to intercept my thrust. Her movements were clean, almost effortless, and her response was exactly what I would expect from someone with her experience.

Unfortunately for her—

She was a fraction too slow.

The tip of my rapier slipped past her guard.

A shallow red line appeared along her forearm.

I immediately withdrew.

A small drop of blood ran down her skin.

Ais looked at it.

Then at me.

"...Fast."

"Thank you."

She raised her sword again.

"Again."

I tilted my head.

"You're not going to complain about the wound?"

"No."

"Not even a little?"

She shook her head.

"Again."

I sighed.

"You're surprisingly straightforward."

I adjusted my grip.

"Fine."

This time, she moved first.

Desperate swept toward my shoulder.

I stepped outside its trajectory, turning my body just enough to allow the blade to pass. The weapon carried enough force to disturb the air beside my face.

I could feel the difference.

Ais wasn't simply swinging a heavy sword.

Every movement was connected to the next.

Her attacks flowed naturally, with almost no wasted motion. Even when her sword missed, the momentum was immediately redirected into another strike.

I retreated.

One step.

Two.

Three.

Desperate descended.

I raised Crimson Order and deflected the blade away from my torso.

CLANG!

The impact traveled through my wrist.

Strong.

Very strong.

I pivoted and attempted to thrust toward her exposed side.

She twisted.

My rapier grazed her clothing.

Ais immediately brought her sword around.

I ducked.

The blade passed above my head.

Then I stepped forward.

Our weapons crossed.

For an instant, we were close enough to see the details of each other's expressions.

Her eyes were focused.

Mine probably looked rather amused.

"You're holding back," she said.

I raised an eyebrow.

"Already?"

"Yes."

"That's a little unfair. We just started."

She pushed against my weapon.

I yielded.

Then stepped sideways.

"Besides, you're doing the same."

Ais didn't answer.

She attacked again.

This time, she was faster.

I deliberately allowed her to establish the rhythm.

One horizontal strike.

A diagonal cut.

A thrust.

A sudden reversal.

I parried each movement with the smallest possible adjustment, using Crimson Order's narrow profile to redirect her attacks rather than meeting them directly.

It was an important distinction.

A rapier was not designed to exchange blows with a heavier weapon.

Trying to overpower Desperate would be an unnecessarily expensive way of losing.

Instead, I needed to control the direction of her blade.

Every successful parry changed the angle of the next attack.

Every small adjustment reduced the number of options available to her.

It was a battle of positioning.

And Ais was beginning to notice.

Her next strike came faster.

I twisted my wrist.

CLANG!

The weapons separated.

She immediately followed with a thrust.

I moved my head.

The point passed beside my cheek.

I stepped back.

"You're learning quickly."

She looked at me.

"So are you."

"That's because I have an excellent teacher."

Ais blinked.

"...Who?"

I smiled.

"You're standing in front of me."

She stared.

Then, very faintly, her expression changed.

Was that confusion?

Amusement?

It was difficult to tell.

Either way, she attacked.

And this time, I had to take her seriously.

***

### A Familiar Sword

The rhythm changed.

Ais lowered her center of gravity.

Her right foot moved slightly forward, while her left shoulder turned inward. Desperate was no longer held in the same position as before.

I recognized the adjustment.

Her next movement was different.

The sword descended in a deceptively simple arc.

I raised Crimson Order to intercept.

But the strike changed direction halfway through.

Not because she had altered the trajectory with her arms.

Rather, her entire body had shifted.

Her lower half barely moved, yet her upper body seemed to possess an extraordinary degree of freedom.

I barely managed to avoid the blade.

The next attack came immediately.

Then another.

I retreated.

My eyes narrowed.

That movement.

I knew it.

Not from ordinary training.

Not from any of the swordsmanship manuals I had studied.

I had encountered it somewhere else.

A place where the passage of time had little resemblance to the world outside.

The Land of Dreams.

There, I had been exposed to techniques that were no longer practiced in the modern era. Among them was a particular style of swordsmanship that had once belonged to a swordsman whose name carried an extraordinary weight.

Waldstein.

I remembered the first time I had attempted to reproduce it.

It had been miserable.

Absolutely miserable.

The movements were deceptively simple when observed from the outside. The difficulty came from understanding how the body was supposed to behave.

The technique demanded extraordinary coordination between the upper and lower body.

The feet were economical.

The hips were controlled.

The shoulders moved with remarkable freedom.

And the sword followed a path that was difficult to predict because the movements preceding it revealed almost nothing about the actual attack.

It was beautiful.

And exceptionally dangerous.

I had spent an unreasonable amount of time trying to master it.

And now—

I was watching Ais use it.

How?

I parried another strike.

CLANG!

The impact forced my arm downward.

I stepped away.

My eyes moved toward her feet.

Then her shoulders.

Then the position of her sword.

There was no mistaking it.

She was using the Waldstein sword technique.

I almost laughed.

What an extraordinary coincidence.

Ais noticed my expression.

"What's wrong?"

"Nothing."

I rotated Crimson Order.

"Actually, I have a question."

She waited.

"Where did you learn that swordsmanship?"

Her eyes shifted slightly.

"...This?"

"Yes."

She looked at Desperate.

Then back at me.

"My father."

For a moment, I said nothing.

Ah.

That explained it.

Albert Waldstein.

The legendary swordsman.

The man whose technique had survived long after his death through the person who had inherited his movements.

I had heard enough about him to understand why the style was so extraordinary.

But seeing it firsthand was another matter.

I smiled.

"That makes sense."

Ais tilted her head.

"Why?"

"Because it's beautiful."

She blinked.

"...Beautiful?"

"Your swordsmanship."

I raised Crimson Order.

"There's very little unnecessary movement. Your lower body remains stable while your upper body adjusts independently. It makes your attacks difficult to read."

Ais looked at me.

Then she lowered her sword slightly.

"How do you know?"

I considered my answer.

"Experience."

"Where?"

"Somewhere."

She waited.

I waited.

Neither of us said anything.

Eventually, Ais blinked.

"...That's not an answer."

I laughed.

"You're right."

I lowered my weapon.

"But there's something I should tell you."

She looked at me.

"You're using it incorrectly."

The atmosphere changed.

Not dramatically.

Not enough for someone unfamiliar with Ais to notice.

But I saw it.

Her fingers tightened around the hilt.

"...Incorrectly?"

I nodded.

"Your understanding of the technique is good. Your execution is also impressive. But you're relying too heavily on the movements themselves."

She remained silent.

I continued.

"You're reproducing the motions without fully understanding why they were developed that way."

Her gaze sharpened.

I raised Crimson Order.

"Watch."

I adjusted my stance.

My right foot moved slightly backward.

My shoulders relaxed.

I lowered the point of Crimson Order.

Then I moved.

It was a simple attack.

A single forward step followed by a thrust.

But the moment I began, the structure of the movement changed.

My lower body remained stable.

My hips shifted only as much as necessary.

My shoulders rotated independently, allowing the rapier to travel along a narrow and unusually direct trajectory.

The result was a thrust that appeared to emerge from almost nowhere.

Ais's eyes widened.

She barely moved.

I stopped the tip of Crimson Order a finger's width from her chest.

Silence.

Then I withdrew.

"That's the difference."

Ais stared at the weapon.

I continued.

"You're thinking about how to perform the technique. You should be thinking about how to make the opponent respond to it."

I demonstrated again.

This time, I changed the angle.

The point moved toward her shoulder.

Then her throat.

Then her chest.

Three different trajectories, all originating from nearly identical movements.

Ais watched carefully.

I stopped.

"Your father understood something important. A sword technique isn't just a collection of movements. It's a method of controlling the opponent's perception."

I lowered Crimson Order.

"Every movement should make the next one more difficult to anticipate."

Ais looked at me for a long moment.

Then she raised Desperate.

"Again."

I smiled.

"Of course."

She attacked.

And I answered with her own swordsmanship.

Not a crude imitation.

Not an approximation.

The actual technique.

My movements were sharper, more economical, and considerably more refined than the version she had been using.

I slipped past her first attack.

Then redirected her second.

A third strike came toward my shoulder.

I stepped inside its trajectory and turned my wrist.

Crimson Order touched her guard.

Ais immediately attempted to withdraw.

Too late.

I followed.

A small movement of my hips redirected the rapier toward her exposed side.

She twisted away.

I continued.

Another thrust.

Another.

Then a cut across her sleeve.

The exchange lasted only a few seconds.

But for Ais, it must have felt considerably longer.

She had recognized her own technique.

And yet, she was watching someone use it better than she could.

Not because I possessed greater strength.

Not because I had a superior weapon.

But because I understood the underlying principles.

I stepped away.

"Sorry, Ais."

She stared at me.

I lowered Crimson Order.

"But you're doing it wrong."

For a moment, she didn't move.

Her eyes remained fixed on my stance.

Then something changed.

Her pupils widened.

Her breathing stopped for a brief instant.

And a single word escaped her lips.

"...Waldstein."

I smiled faintly.

"Yes."

She looked at me again.

"How?"

I tilted my head.

"That's a rather long story."

Ais didn't seem satisfied.

But before she could ask another question, she raised Desperate.

This time, her expression was different.

There was something behind her eyes.

Recognition.

Perhaps even longing.

She wasn't simply watching a sword technique.

She was looking at something that reminded her of someone she had lost.

I considered stopping.

Then Ais moved.

And I realized she didn't want me to.

### Ariel

The wind changed.

It began as a faint disturbance around Ais's shoulders.

Then it gathered.

The surrounding air trembled, and strands of wind began circling her body.

Ais raised Desperate.

"Ariel!"

The spell activated.

A surge of wind enveloped her.

Her hair moved with the current, and the air around her weapon became visibly distorted.

Ariel.

A wind enchantment that strengthened her physical abilities while enhancing her attacks and providing a protective layer around her body and weapon.

I had seen enough to understand why she was considered one of the most dangerous swordswomen in the city.

Her movements became faster.

Much faster.

The next instant, she disappeared from where she had been standing.

I moved.

CLANG!

Desperate struck Crimson Order.

The impact forced my arm backward.

Ais followed with a second attack.

I twisted.

A third.

I retreated.

A fourth.

I barely deflected it.

Well.

That was considerably more interesting.

Her speed had increased dramatically, and the wind surrounding her blade made every attack more dangerous.

The previous exchange had been a contest of technique.

Now, it was becoming a contest of physical capability.

I stepped backward.

Ais pursued.

Her sword swept horizontally.

I lowered my body.

The wind followed the blade, cutting through the air with enough force to disturb the ground behind me.

I counterattacked.

She parried.

Our weapons collided.

CLANG!

I shifted my wrist.

She adjusted.

I attempted to redirect her sword.

She forced it back.

Then she attacked again.

I retreated.

Not because I couldn't counter.

But because I needed to understand what was happening.

The ordinary Ariel enchantment enhanced her physical abilities and reinforced her weapon. The wind around her attacks made her movements increasingly difficult to read and defend against.

Her strength had increased.

Her speed had increased.

And the pressure surrounding her attacks was becoming increasingly difficult to manage.

I stepped away from another attack.

"Ais!"

No response.

Her sword descended.

I deflected it.

The impact sent a shock through my arm.

Then the wind expanded.

I raised a barrier.

BOOOOOOM!

The barrier shattered.

I was thrown backward.

My feet left the ground for a brief moment before I landed and rolled across the dirt.

I stopped myself with one hand.

"...Okay."

I stood up.

"That was considerably stronger than I expected."

Ais approached.

Her breathing was heavier.

Her eyes were fixed on me.

And the wind around her body was beginning to fluctuate.

I recognized the pattern.

She was pushing Ariel harder.

Possibly harder than necessary.

I stepped away.

"That's enough for now."

Ais shook her head.

"...No."

I raised an eyebrow.

"You're already using your enchantment."

"Again."

I looked at her.

She held Desperate firmly.

"Please."

That last word made me pause.

It wasn't a demand.

It wasn't an order.

It was a request.

I sighed.

"Fine."

I raised Crimson Order.

"But don't overdo it."

She nodded.

I wasn't entirely convinced she had heard me.

The next exchange began.

Ais attacked with greater speed.

I parried.

She redirected.

I retreated.

She pursued.

Our weapons collided repeatedly, each impact sending sparks into the air.

I attempted to use the same movements I had demonstrated earlier.

Ais adapted.

She began recognizing the transitions between my attacks.

When I shifted my shoulders, she adjusted her guard.

When I moved my hips, she prepared for the thrust.

When I attempted to control her sword, she changed the angle before I could establish leverage.

Excellent.

She was learning.

More importantly, she was thinking.

I smiled.

"That's it!"

She attacked again.

This time, I barely avoided the strike.

Desperate passed close enough to cut a strand of my hair.

I glanced at it.

"...That was close."

Ais didn't stop.

Her sword came around.

I blocked.

The impact rang through the training ground.

I stepped backward.

She followed.

Then—

Her movements changed.

The wind around her body became turbulent.

Her eyes narrowed.

And I noticed something familiar.

Not ordinary aggression.

Not the focused determination of someone trying to win.

Something much deeper.

Something that had been buried beneath years of discipline and restraint.

I lowered my weapon slightly.

"Ais?"

She didn't respond.

The wind intensified.

And I realized that the sparring session had crossed into something else.

Something neither of us had intended.

***

### The Black Wind

The first sign was the color.

The wind surrounding Ais darkened.

At first, I thought it was an illusion caused by the lighting. The crystals overhead cast strange shadows, and the turbulent air distorted everything around her.

But no.

It was real.

The wind became darker, heavier, and considerably more violent.

My expression changed.

"...Oh."

I recognized that phenomenon.

Ariel combined with Avenger.

Black Wind.

I had read about the destructive potential of her abilities, but witnessing it at this distance was an entirely different experience.

The air around her seemed to lose its ordinary properties.

It no longer behaved like a simple enchantment.

It was almost as if the wind itself had become hostile.

I tightened my grip on Crimson Order.

Now, this was a problem.

A rather serious one.

I had intended to teach her something about swordsmanship and perhaps encourage her to express herself more freely.

I hadn't intended to activate one of her most dangerous abilities.

Ais raised Desperate.

Her eyes were no longer entirely focused on me.

There was something else behind them.

Something distant.

Something angry.

The black wind surged.

The ground beneath her feet cracked.

And then she moved.

I barely managed to react.

BOOOOM!

Her sword struck my guard.

The impact sent me sliding backward.

My boots tore through the dirt, leaving two deep grooves behind me.

I looked at my arm.

...That was unpleasant.

I raised my eyes.

Ais was already approaching.

Desperate swept toward my shoulder.

I twisted.

The blade passed by.

But the wind followed.

A violent current struck my side and sent me stumbling.

I recovered almost immediately.

Then she attacked again.

A horizontal strike.

A downward cut.

A thrust.

Each movement was accompanied by a violent burst of black wind.

I retreated.

Not because I couldn't counter.

But because I needed to understand what was happening.

The ordinary Ariel enchantment enhanced her physical abilities and reinforced her weapon. The addition of Avenger transformed that enhancement into something considerably more destructive.

Her strength had increased.

Her speed had increased.

And the pressure surrounding her attacks was becoming increasingly difficult to manage.

The danger wasn't limited to her opponent.

It was also affecting her.

I remembered the descriptions.

Avenger was not a harmless enhancement.

It consumed the user alongside the enemy.

The more she relied on it, the more dangerous the consequences became.

And right now, Ais was pushing herself.

Far too much.

I stepped away from another attack.

"Ais!"

No response.

Her sword descended.

I deflected it.

The impact sent a shock through my arm.

Then the black wind expanded.

I raised a barrier.

BOOOOOOM!

The barrier shattered.

I was thrown backward.

My feet left the ground for a brief moment before I landed and rolled across the dirt.

I stopped myself with one hand.

"...Okay."

I stood up.

"That was considerably stronger than I expected."

Ais approached.

The black wind continued swirling around her.

Her breathing was irregular.

There were tears in her eyes.

I stared at her.

Not because I was afraid.

But because I finally understood something.

She wasn't simply trying to defeat me.

She was fighting against something inside herself.

Perhaps the sight of the technique had awakened memories she wasn't prepared to confront. Perhaps the emotions associated with it had overwhelmed her ability to remain composed.

Whatever the reason, she was no longer approaching this as an ordinary sparring session.

And I wasn't going to let her hurt herself.

Still...

I couldn't simply force her to stop without giving her an opportunity to regain control.

I raised Crimson Order.

"All right."

My expression became serious.

"One more exchange."

Ais moved.

The black wind erupted.

I stepped forward.

Not backward.

Forward.

Her eyes widened slightly.

Desperate came toward me.

I tilted my body.

The sword passed my shoulder.

The wind tore through the fabric of my clothing.

I continued moving.

My rapier slid along the side of her blade.

A small adjustment.

A rotation of my wrist.

A change in the position of my feet.

Then I was inside her guard.

Crimson Order stopped beside her ribs.

I could have ended the exchange there.

But I didn't.

Instead, I stepped away.

Ais turned.

Her sword followed.

And I attacked again.

This time, I used the Waldstein technique.

Her own swordsmanship.

I shifted my lower body.

My shoulders rotated.

The rapier moved.

Ais reacted instinctively.

Desperate intercepted the thrust.

But that was precisely what I wanted.

I redirected my weapon.

The point passed her guard.

Then I withdrew before it could touch her.

Ais stared.

For a fraction of a second, the black wind seemed to hesitate.

I spoke.

"You're still thinking about winning."

She didn't answer.

"That's not what I want you to learn."

I adjusted my stance.

"Look at me, Ais."

Her eyes moved toward mine.

"You're allowed to be angry."

The black wind intensified.

"You're allowed to be sad."

I lowered Crimson Order slightly.

"And you're allowed to remember."

For a moment, her expression faltered.

The next attack came anyway.

I parried.

CLANG!

She attacked again.

I redirected.

CLANG!

A third strike.

I stepped aside.

The wind exploded against the ground.

I raised my voice.

"But you don't have to destroy yourself just to prove that you haven't forgotten!"

Her eyes widened.

And for the first time—

She hesitated.

I saw it.

A tiny opening.

A moment when her attention shifted away from the fight.

I could have taken advantage of it.

Instead, I waited.

Because that was the lesson.

Not every opening needed to be exploited.

Sometimes, the most important thing a swordsman could do was recognize when the fight had already become something else.

Ais's sword lowered slightly.

The black wind surged once more.

Then she moved.

Faster than before.

A direct attack.

A final attempt to overwhelm me.

I raised Crimson Order.

And chanted.

"Ariel!"

The wind answered.

But I didn't allow it to envelop my entire body.

Instead, I concentrated the enchantment along the rapier.

A narrow current gathered around the blade.

The air compressed.

The tip of Crimson Order trembled.

I had no intention of matching her destructive output with brute force.

That would have been foolish.

Ais was already pushing herself beyond reasonable limits.

I needed precision.

Not power.

I stepped forward.

And swung.

The resulting gust erupted from the rapier.

WHOOOOOOOOOSH!

A violent current tore across the training ground.

The surrounding dust disappeared in an instant.

The grass flattened.

Loose stones scattered.

And the trees at the edge of the clearing bent beneath the pressure.

For a brief moment, the entire area was swallowed by the wind.

Ais stood directly in its path.

Her black enchantment resisted.

The two currents collided.

The resulting pressure shook the surrounding ground.

I kept my arm extended.

My eyes remained fixed on her.

The wind continued pushing.

Then—

It broke through.

Ais's black wind scattered around her.

She was thrown backward.

But she didn't fall.

Her feet struck the ground.

One step.

Then another.

Desperate scraped against the earth.

She managed to remain standing.

A Level Five adventurer.

Even after receiving that attack, she was still capable of maintaining her footing.

Impressive.

Really impressive.

I lowered my arm.

"You're tougher than I thought."

Ais raised her sword.

Her breathing was ragged.

The black wind began gathering again.

I narrowed my eyes.

Oh, come on.

You're still going?

She moved her right foot.

Her sword shifted.

I recognized the preparation.

She was trying to regain her footing and launch another attack.

But the wind had already begun destabilizing.

Her body was reaching its limit.

I wasn't going to give her another opportunity to injure herself.

I raised Crimson Order.

The point of the rapier aligned with her weapon.

Then I moved.

One step.

A slight rotation.

A controlled thrust toward the space between her sword and her body.

The point touched Desperate.

A small adjustment.

And suddenly, the angle of her weapon changed.

Her guard opened.

I raised the rapier.

Its tip stopped directly in front of her.

There was no need to continue.

I had already won.

But Ais was still trying to move.

Her sword trembled.

The black wind swirled around her.

And then—

I spoke.

"Dispel."

The magic around her collapsed.

Not gradually.

Not with a slow dissipation.

It simply stopped.

The black wind vanished as though someone had removed the foundation holding it together.

The pressure disappeared.

The surrounding air became still.

Ais's expression changed.

Her eyes widened.

Her sword lowered.

For a brief instant, she looked genuinely confused.

Then gravity reclaimed its authority.

Her knees gave way.

She landed on her feet first, but her body couldn't maintain its balance.

Desperate slipped from her hand.

She fell backward onto the ground.

I immediately lowered Crimson Order.

"...Ais?"

No response.

She was staring at the ceiling.

Her chest rose and fell rapidly.

Her golden eyes remained open, but the expression behind them was difficult to read.

I approached.

Then crouched beside her.

"Hey."

She blinked.

I waved a hand in front of her face.

"Still with me?"

"...Yes."

"Good."

I exhaled.

"That was quite something."

She turned her head toward me.

"...I lost."

I smiled.

"Yes."

She looked away.

I waited.

For several seconds, neither of us spoke.

Then she whispered something.

"...Again."

I stared at her.

"...What?"

"Again."

I looked at the fallen sword.

Then at her.

Then at the damaged training ground.

I slowly covered my face.

"You're unbelievable."

Ais blinked.

I sighed.

"Not today."

Her eyes shifted toward me.

"Why?"

"Because you're exhausted, your magic just went out of control, and I would rather not spend the rest of the day explaining to everyone why the training ground looks like a natural disaster."

She looked around.

The surrounding area was...

Well.

Let's just say it needed some maintenance.

"...Sorry."

I laughed.

"Don't apologize. I was the one who suggested sparring."

I offered my hand.

Ais looked at it.

Then accepted.

I helped her stand.

She wavered slightly.

I immediately supported her by the shoulder.

"Careful."

"I'm fine."

"You're not."

"..."

"Don't argue. You're practically swaying."

She looked down.

Then, reluctantly, allowed me to support her.

I glanced at her forearm.

The earlier graze had stopped bleeding.

But there were several new marks on her hands and arms, probably from the strain of using her enchantment.

I frowned.

"We should get those checked."

Ais nodded.

Then she looked at me.

"...You knew."

I tilted my head.

"About what?"

"The sword technique."

I smiled.

"Yes."

"How?"

I considered the question.

Then gave her the same answer as before.

"Experience."

She narrowed her eyes.

"...You're hiding something."

I laughed.

"Probably."

She didn't seem particularly pleased.

But she didn't press the matter.

Instead, she looked toward the ground where Desperate had fallen.

Then she asked something I hadn't expected.

"Why did you stop?"

I looked at her.

"What do you mean?"

"You could have attacked."

I was silent for a moment.

Then I smiled.

"Because I wasn't fighting an enemy."

Ais stared.

I continued.

"There's a difference between defeating someone and helping them become stronger."

She lowered her eyes.

"Even if they lose?"

"Especially if they lose."

I looked toward the damaged training ground.

"Winning doesn't automatically mean you've learned something. And losing doesn't automatically mean you've failed."

I turned back toward her.

"Today, you learned that your swordsmanship has room to improve. You also learned that you can recognize and adapt to techniques you've never encountered before."

I paused.

"And I learned that you have a rather frightening tendency to ignore your own limits."

Ais blinked.

"...I do?"

I stared at her.

She stared back.

Then I sighed.

"Yes."

For a moment, she seemed to consider that.

Then she looked toward me again.

"...Thank you."

I smiled.

"You're welcome."

And just like that, the sparring session was over.

At least, the fighting part.

I had a feeling the conversation was only beginning.

***

### What It Means to Be Strong

We eventually found a relatively undamaged place to sit.

Ais was resting against a nearby rock while I examined her injuries. Nothing appeared immediately life-threatening, but the strain from her enchantment was concerning.

I wasn't particularly fond of seeing people injure themselves unnecessarily.

Especially when the entire purpose of the exercise had been to help them improve.

"Does it hurt?"

"A little."

"That's not reassuring."

She looked at her arm.

"...I'm fine."

I gave her a look.

She blinked.

Then, after a moment, corrected herself.

"It hurts."

"Better."

I retrieved a small medical kit from my spatial storage.

Ais watched me work.

For someone who could fight monsters with extraordinary ferocity, she was surprisingly quiet when receiving treatment.

I cleaned the scratches and applied a bandage.

"There."

She examined it.

"Thank you."

"Don't mention it."

I returned the medical supplies to storage.

For a while, we simply sat there.

The silence wasn't uncomfortable.

It was simply a moment to breathe after an unusually eventful morning.

Eventually, Ais spoke.

"Your sword."

I looked at Crimson Order.

"What about it?"

"It's unusual."

"That's one way of describing it."

She studied the blade.

"The material?"

"Mostly synthetic mithril, with some orichalcum components."

Her eyes moved toward the weapon's guard.

"You made it?"

I nodded.

"Yes."

She looked at me.

"Why?"

I rotated the rapier slightly.

There was no particular reason to hide the story.

"It was supposed to be a gift."

"For whom?"

"Astrea."

Ais blinked.

"...Astrea?"

I nodded.

"After what happened to her Familia, I thought she might appreciate something that could remind her to keep moving forward."

Ais lowered her eyes.

I continued.

"I wasn't trying to replace anything she had lost. That would be impossible. I just wanted to give her something that might help her remember that her story wasn't necessarily over."

I examined the blade.

"Unfortunately, she declined."

Ais remained silent.

"She had her reasons. I didn't force the matter."

I returned Crimson Order to its sheath.

"Still, it would have been a shame to leave it unused."

Ais looked at the weapon again.

Then she looked toward me.

"You made it for her."

"Yes."

"Because you wanted her to stand again."

I paused.

Then smiled.

"Something like that."

Ais looked down at her bandaged arm.

"...I understand."

I tilted my head.

"Do you?"

She nodded.

For a moment, her expression became distant.

Then she spoke again.

"Earlier, you said I didn't have to destroy myself."

I nodded.

"Yes."

"Why?"

I considered the question.

There were several possible answers.

I could have explained the dangers of Avenger. I could have discussed the physical consequences of excessive magical enhancement or the importance of preserving one's body.

But those weren't really what she was asking.

So I gave her a simpler answer.

"Because you're important too."

Ais looked at me.

I continued.

"You spend so much time thinking about becoming stronger that you sometimes forget the person who needs to live with that strength."

She remained silent.

"Your swordsmanship is beautiful. Your determination is admirable. And your desire to become stronger isn't something I intend to criticize."

I smiled.

"But you don't need to hurt yourself to justify any of those things."

Ais lowered her eyes.

I leaned back.

"Besides, becoming stronger should give you more opportunities. Not fewer."

She looked toward me again.

"Opportunities?"

"To protect someone. To explore somewhere new. To meet people. To experience things you haven't experienced before."

I paused.

"Or even to enjoy a peaceful morning without having to fight someone."

Ais blinked.

"...You don't seem to enjoy fighting."

I laughed.

"That's a complicated question."

"Why?"

"Because I enjoy learning how to fight. I enjoy testing techniques, understanding different abilities, and discovering ways to improve."

I looked at my hands.

"But I don't particularly enjoy the part where someone gets hurt."

Ais considered that.

Then she asked:

"Does that make you weak?"

I looked at her.

And smiled.

"No."

I leaned forward slightly.

"Knowing when to fight and knowing when to stop are both important. If you can only do the first, you're not necessarily strong. You're just dangerous."

She remained silent.

I continued.

"And if you can protect someone without destroying everything around you, that's even better."

Ais stared at me.

For several seconds, she didn't say anything.

Then—

"...I want to learn."

I blinked.

"Learn what?"

"That."

She looked at Crimson Order.

"How you fight."

I smiled.

"Well, that's going to take some time."

"That's fine."

"You're going to have to practice."

"I will."

"And you'll need to stop using your body as a substitute for proper judgment."

She looked away.

"...I'll try."

I laughed.

"Good enough."

Then I stood.

I offered her my hand again.

She accepted it.

This time, she managed to stand without losing her balance.

I looked toward the damaged training ground.

Then toward the surrounding trees.

Then at the remaining traces of black wind slowly disappearing from the air.

I sighed.

"...I should probably repair this place."

Ais looked around.

Then back at me.

"Sorry."

"Again, don't apologize."

I smiled.

"Besides, I was the one who wanted to see what you could do."

She tilted her head.

"Did you learn something?"

I considered the question.

Then nodded.

"Quite a lot, actually."

"What?"

I raised one finger.

"First, your swordsmanship is considerably better than I initially expected."

Ais blinked.

"Second, your ability to adapt is excellent."

I raised another finger.

"Third, you have a terrible habit of pushing yourself too far."

She looked away.

"..."

"And fourth."

I smiled.

"You're capable of expressing yourself more than you think."

Ais looked at me.

I continued.

"You were angry. You were frustrated. You were remembering something important to you. And instead of hiding those feelings, you allowed them to influence your movements."

I paused.

"That's not necessarily a bad thing."

Her eyes softened.

"But you need to learn how to control them. Not suppress them. There's a difference."

She nodded.

"...I understand."

I wasn't entirely sure she did.

But that was fine.

Understanding something intellectually and applying it to oneself were two different matters.

It would take time.

And she had plenty of it.

At least, I hoped she would.

***

### My Hero

We began walking back toward the campsite.

Ais was moving slowly, understandably so.

I adjusted my pace to match hers.

For a while, neither of us spoke.

Then she suddenly asked:

"Bell."

"Hm?"

"Do you think I'm strong?"

I glanced at her.

"Of course."

She looked down.

"Strong enough?"

I raised an eyebrow.

"Strong enough for what?"

She didn't answer immediately.

Her fingers moved slightly.

Then she spoke.

"To protect someone."

I stopped walking.

Ais took another step before realizing I was no longer beside her.

She turned.

I looked at her for a moment.

Then smiled.

"Yes."

Her eyes widened slightly.

I continued.

"But strength isn't something you measure only by how many people you can defeat."

She listened.

"Sometimes, protecting someone means standing in front of them. Sometimes, it means helping them stand on their own. And sometimes, it means accepting that you can't save everyone."

Ais lowered her eyes.

I understood that the last part was difficult for her.

Perhaps more difficult than anything else I had said that morning.

I continued carefully.

"You don't have to carry everything by yourself."

She looked at me.

"And you don't have to become someone else's idea of a hero."

I smiled.

"Just become someone you can be proud of."

For a moment, she simply stared.

Then her expression changed.

It was subtle.

Almost imperceptible.

But I saw it.

Her lips curved upward.

A small smile.

Not the brief, uncertain expression I had occasionally seen before.

Something warmer.

Something genuine.

"...Thank you, Bell."

I smiled back.

"You're welcome."

We continued walking.

After a few steps, she spoke again.

"You're strange."

I blinked.

"Excuse me?"

"You fight strangely."

"That's fair."

"You know techniques from a long time ago."

"Apparently."

"You make weapons."

"Occasionally."

"And you say strange things."

I stared at her.

"...I'm beginning to think you're insulting me."

She tilted her head.

"Am I?"

I sighed.

"Never mind."

For some reason, she smiled again.

And this time, I was certain I had seen it.

We reached the edge of the campsite.

The others were still occupied with their morning preparations. A few had noticed the condition of the training ground, but I decided not to draw attention to it.

There would be questions.

There would be explanations.

And there would almost certainly be complaints about the repair work.

I was already regretting my decision.

Ais stopped beside me.

"Bell."

I turned.

"Yes?"

She looked at me for a moment.

Then lowered her head slightly.

"...You're my hero."

I froze.

Oh.

That was unexpected.

I stared at her.

She seemed perfectly serious.

There was no teasing in her voice, no hidden meaning that I could immediately identify.

Just a simple statement.

I scratched my cheek.

"Well..."

I looked away.

"That's a little embarrassing."

Ais tilted her head.

"Why?"

"Because I don't think I've done anything particularly heroic."

She looked at me.

Then smiled.

"You did."

I didn't know what to say.

For once, my usual collection of responses failed me.

So I simply smiled.

"...Thank you."

Ais nodded.

Then she walked ahead.

I watched her for a moment.

And eventually followed.

### A Rather Unfortunate Realization

I had barely returned to the campsite when another thought occurred to me.

The rapier.

Crimson Order.

I looked at the weapon in my hand.

Then toward the damaged training ground.

Then back at the weapon.

"...I should probably clean this."

The blade had accumulated a considerable amount of dust.

I retrieved a cloth from storage and began wiping it.

It was a rather peaceful activity.

Unfortunately, my thoughts returned to the earlier conversation.

Ais had called me her hero.

That was...

Well.

I wasn't particularly comfortable with the title.

A hero was someone who could accomplish extraordinary things, someone who could inspire others, someone who could stand against overwhelming odds and still find a reason to move forward.

I wasn't certain I qualified.

I made mistakes.

I misunderstood people.

I occasionally made decisions that I regretted.

And, judging by the condition of the training ground, I wasn't particularly good at keeping things intact.

Still.

Perhaps that wasn't the point.

Maybe being someone's hero wasn't necessarily about being perfect.

Maybe it was simply about being there when they needed someone.

I looked toward the direction Ais had gone.

Then smiled faintly.

"Well..."

I returned Crimson Order to my spatial storage.

"That's something worth thinking about."

I stretched my arms.

The morning had certainly become more eventful than I anticipated.

And I still had breakfast to eat.

Hopefully, nobody would ask me to explain the condition of the training ground before then.

Because I really didn't want to.
