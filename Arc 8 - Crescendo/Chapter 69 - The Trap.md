# Chapter Sixty-Nine: The Trap
*Arc 8 – Crescendo*

*POV: Ein / Filvis Chalia / Bell Cranel*

---

### I. The Hunter's Perspective

The twenty-fifth floor was unusually quiet.

Not silent. The Dungeon was never truly silent. Somewhere beyond the winding corridors, monsters roared, stones crumbled, and the distant sounds of adventurers fighting echoed through the labyrinth. The Dungeon breathed, if one could call its ceaseless activity breathing.

But this particular section had become strangely peaceful.

Perhaps that was why she disliked it.

Ein stood behind the enormous trunk of a Jewel Tree, her pale fingers resting against its rough bark. Its branches extended high above her, adorned with crystals that reflected the faint blue illumination of the Dungeon. Even without sunlight, the tree glittered like something stolen from a fairy tale.

A beautiful place to die.

How appropriate.

She looked down at the object in her hand.

A magic-dampening device.

It was an expensive little thing, an artifact designed to interfere with the formation and operation of magic. Its construction was complicated, its effects troublesome, and its price alone would have been enough to discourage most ordinary adventurers.

Sixty-nine million valis.

She still couldn't quite believe Lord Enyo had authorized its purchase.

*This should do the trick.*

Her lips curled faintly.

*That troublesome mage. Bell Cranel.*

She had heard enough reports about him to understand why Enyo considered him dangerous. A young adventurer whose abilities were difficult to classify, whose magic was even more troublesome than his physical capabilities, and whose seemingly unremarkable appearance concealed an assortment of unusual abilities.

He was supposed to arrive on this floor.

And she was supposed to capture him.

Alive.

That last instruction had been repeated several times.

Lord Enyo wanted the boy intact. No unnecessary injuries, no killing, and absolutely no mistakes.

There was also the matter of Filvis.

*Lord Enyo said I would need to fuse with her to capture him.*

Ein's expression soured.

Was that really necessary?

She was Level Six. A formidable adventurer in her own right, even if she had little interest in comparing herself with the more celebrated members of the city's strongest Familia.

Surely she could handle one troublesome mage.

*Do we actually need to go that far?*

She glanced at the device again.

It would suppress his magic. That was the entire point. Without his spells, the boy would lose a considerable portion of his advantage.

And if he attempted to escape?

Well, she was hardly inexperienced in capturing prey.

There was only one problem.

She needed to make sure he walked into the trap.

Not suspiciously. Not under duress.

He had to enter the designated area willingly.

Fortunately, the Dungeon was generous with its distractions.

A Jewel Tree was a rare sight, particularly one growing in a location where the surrounding monsters had already been eliminated. Its branches were full of valuable materials, and the corpse of a Green Dragon nearby suggested that someone had already paid a considerable price to clear the area.

A tempting prize.

A believable reason for an adventurer to stop.

A perfect location for an ambush.

Ein leaned against the tree and waited.

Then she sensed him.

A presence approaching from the corridor.

She straightened.

*There you are.*

### II. The Prey That Refused to Be Caught

Bell Cranel walked into the chamber.

And then Ein frowned.

He was... casual.

That was the first thing she noticed.

The boy wasn't moving like someone who had entered a dangerous section of the Dungeon. He wasn't cautiously examining every corner, nor was he keeping his weapon drawn in anticipation of an ambush.

He was simply walking.

Every so often, he extended a hand and released a small magic projectile toward some distant monster.

A monster would fall.

He would continue walking.

Another monster appeared.

Another projectile.

Another corpse.

It was almost insulting.

*What is he doing?*

She watched him for several minutes.

The boy's movements were economical. There was no unnecessary flourish, no extravagant chanting, and no apparent hesitation between attacks.

He didn't even seem particularly interested in the monsters.

His attention wandered between the walls, the ceiling, and the scattered remains of previous battles.

Then he fired another projectile.

*Does he not need to replenish his Mind?*

Ein narrowed her eyes.

She had seen mages exhaust themselves after considerably fewer spells. Even talented adventurers had limits. Magic required Mind, and Mind was not something one could replenish simply by wishing for it.

Yet Bell continued.

One spell.

Another.

Another.

His expression remained unchanged.

*Almost as though he has an unlimited supply.*

She clicked her tongue.

No matter.

She was not here to understand his abilities.

She was here to capture him.

And she had no allies waiting nearby. This was a solo operation, and the success of the entire arrangement rested on her ability to execute it properly.

The boy finally approached the Jewel Tree.

His gaze shifted toward its glittering branches.

Ein smiled.

*That's right.*

*Come a little closer.*

Bell stopped.

For a moment, he simply stared at the tree.

Then he took another step.

*And another.*

Ein's fingers tightened around the device.

*You've walked right into my trap.*

The floor erupted.

A massive magic circle illuminated the chamber, its intricate symbols spreading across the stone like a web of glowing veins. Before Bell could take another step, magical restraints burst from the ground, forming a vast net that enveloped his body.

The trap had been activated.

Ein emerged from behind the tree.

"Good evening, Bell Cranel."

She stepped into the open, her pale face illuminated by the magical formation.

"Or perhaps I should say... unfortunate evening."

The boy looked down at the net.

Then at her.

And smiled.

Ein's smile disappeared.

"Good evening?" Bell repeated, looking at her with an almost apologetic expression. "That's a rather unusual greeting for someone who just tried to wrap me in a magical net."

Ein stared.

Then she glanced at the magic circle.

It was functioning perfectly.

The restraints were intact. The formation had activated without interference, and the device in her possession was operating exactly as intended.

So why wasn't he struggling?

"You're remarkably composed."

"Am I supposed to be panicking?"

"Most people would."

"Then I suppose I'm not most people."

Bell casually lifted one hand.

The magical restraints tightened.

Or, at least, they were supposed to.

Instead, the glowing strands began to tremble.

Ein's eyes widened.

"Wait—"

*Crack.*

A thin fracture appeared across one of the magical strands.

Then another.

And another.

The entire formation shattered in a burst of blue light.

Bell stepped out of the remains of the net, brushing a few magical particles from his clothes.

"That's a rather elaborate trap. You must have spent quite a bit preparing it."

Ein took a step backward.

Impossible.

The dampening device should have interfered with his magic. Even if he possessed an unusual reserve of Mind, the artifact was specifically designed to disrupt magical formations.

Unless—

"How?"

Bell tilted his head.

"How what?"

"Your magic! The device should have suppressed it!"

"Oh. That."

He looked at the object in her hand.

For a moment, he seemed genuinely interested.

"That's an expensive piece of equipment. Sixty-nine million valis, if I'm not mistaken?"

Ein's expression changed.

He knew its price?

"How did you—"

"Never mind. It's not important."

Bell raised his hand and produced a small sphere of mana.

It hovered above his palm, spinning slowly.

Ein recognized the phenomenon as the precursor to a spell.

Except there was no chant.

No visible expenditure of Mind.

No familiar signs of magical activation.

"What are you doing?"

"Showing you something."

The sphere expanded, then contracted, changing its shape several times before dissolving into the surrounding air.

Bell looked at her.

"Most conventional mages gather mana, process it through their Mind, and then convert it into a spell. That's the usual procedure, isn't it?"

Ein didn't answer.

"Well, I've found another method. I can manipulate the mana around me directly without relying on the usual process of consuming my Mind to produce the same result."

He shrugged.

"Of course, it's not as simple as it sounds. There are plenty of limitations. But your little device isn't particularly effective against something that doesn't depend on the mechanism it's designed to suppress."

Ein's fingers tightened around the artifact.

*That's why.*

It wasn't that the device had malfunctioned.

It was that the boy had developed an entirely different way to use magic.

*Lord Enyo was right.*

She should have listened.

She should have brought Filvis.

She should have—

A sudden movement interrupted her thoughts.

Bell was approaching.

Ein immediately raised her free hand.

The air distorted.

Her body moved before the thought had fully formed, her feet sliding across the stone as she accelerated toward him.

She wasn't going to let him dictate the pace of the encounter.

Her claws extended.

Bell turned slightly.

A short blade appeared in his hand.

*Clang!*

The impact echoed throughout the chamber.

Ein's attack was intercepted.

She twisted her body, attempting to strike from another angle, but Bell shifted his weapon and redirected the blow.

His movements were almost frustratingly simple.

No wasted energy.

No unnecessary motion.

He wasn't trying to overpower her.

He was reading her.

Ein retreated and immediately attacked again.

This time, she changed her trajectory halfway through the movement.

Bell avoided the first strike, blocked the second, and stepped outside the range of the third.

A small projectile appeared beside his shoulder.

Ein noticed it too late.

*Boom!*

She was thrown backward.

Her body struck the trunk of the Jewel Tree hard enough to scatter several crystalline leaves.

For a moment, she remained still.

Then she stood.

The damage was already disappearing.

Bell observed her with renewed interest.

"Regeneration."

Ein wiped a thin line of blood from her cheek.

"You really are an unpleasant opponent."

"I've heard that before."

"You're not frightened?"

"Should I be?"

She laughed.

It wasn't a pleasant sound.

"You have no idea what you're dealing with."

Bell lowered his weapon.

"That's true."

He looked directly at her.

"Which is why I'd prefer to talk before either of us does something we might regret."

Ein's smile vanished.

"Talk?"

"Yes."

"With you?"

"Well, I don't see anyone else here."

She stared at him.

Then she attacked.

The battle lasted longer than Ein expected.

Much longer.

She had fought adventurers before. She had killed adventurers before. Her body had been designed to survive encounters that would have reduced ordinary people to pieces.

Yet Bell Cranel was proving to be a particularly irritating exception.

His magic was strange.

His movements were precise.

His reactions were almost unnaturally quick.

And the most infuriating part was that he seemed to be holding back.

Every time she thought she had found an opening, he was already somewhere else.

Every time she committed to an attack, he had already anticipated her trajectory.

He wasn't simply stronger.

He was thinking.

Constantly.

*What is this boy?*

Ein swung her arm.

Bell ducked.

A blade passed over his head.

He countered with a kick to her abdomen, sending her skidding across the stone.

She landed on one knee.

Her breathing was uneven.

Not because she was tired.

Her body didn't tire in the same way anymore.

It was something else.

An uncomfortable sensation deep within her chest.

A familiar pressure.

*Why?*

She looked at Bell.

He wasn't smiling anymore.

His eyes had changed.

There was no longer any amusement in them.

Only observation.

And something else.

Concern.

That was what irritated her the most.

"Stop looking at me like that."

Bell lowered his weapon.

"Like what?"

"Like you pity me."

"I don't."

"Don't lie!"

Her voice echoed through the chamber.

For a moment, neither moved.

Then Ein noticed something.

Her hands were shaking.

She looked down.

The trembling was slight, almost imperceptible, but it was there.

Why?

She had experienced pain before.

She had experienced death.

She had experienced the agony of being torn apart and forced back together.

She had even grown accustomed to the sensation of her own body being something fundamentally different from what it once was.

So why was she shaking now?

Bell took one careful step forward.

"What's your name?"

Ein froze.

"...What?"

"Your name."

"Why?"

"Because I'd rather not keep calling you that."

She stared at him.

Then she laughed.

A short, hollow sound.

"You're either incredibly foolish or incredibly confident."

"Perhaps both."

She looked away.

"Ein."

Bell nodded.

"Ein."

The name sounded strange coming from someone else's mouth.

Almost unfamiliar.

Almost foreign.

She had heard it spoken countless times.

By Enyo.

By the others.

By herself.

Yet Bell said it as though it belonged to a person.

Not a designation.

Not a weapon.

Not a monster.

A person.

And she hated how much that bothered her.

### III. The Banshee's Lament

Minutes passed.

Neither of them attacked.

Bell had lowered his weapon completely.

Ein remained several paces away, her fingers curled tightly against her palms.

She could feel the Dungeon.

It was always there.

A constant presence beneath her feet, within the walls, and throughout the air.

She could sense the mana circulating through its corridors.

She could feel the strange pull that connected her body to something far deeper than the floor they stood upon.

It was an unpleasant sensation.

Like being submerged in water that never allowed her to surface.

Bell noticed her looking down.

"Does it hurt?"

Ein's expression hardened.

"What?"

"Whatever you're feeling."

She didn't answer.

He continued.

"Your body. The magic stone. The mana flowing through you. Something about it seems uncomfortable."

Ein stared at him.

"You're observant."

"I try to be."

"Then observe this."

She opened her hand.

A dark pulse spread across her palm.

The surrounding mana responded immediately.

The Jewel Tree trembled.

The magical energy within the chamber began moving toward her, drawn into her body through invisible channels.

Bell watched closely.

He could see it now.

The mana wasn't merely being absorbed.

It was being continuously supplied.

Her body was connected to the Dungeon itself, as though it had become an extension of its internal structure.

There was something deeply unnatural about the arrangement.

"You're not replenishing your own energy," he murmured.

Ein smiled bitterly.

"Congratulations. You've figured it out."

"You're drawing mana from the Dungeon."

"Is that a problem?"

"Not necessarily."

"Then why are you looking at me like that?"

Bell hesitated.

"Because I don't think you're doing it willingly."

The smile disappeared.

For several seconds, Ein said nothing.

Then her shoulders began to tremble.

"Do you know what it feels like?"

Bell remained silent.

"Do you know what it's like to wake up and realize that your own body no longer belongs to you?"

Her voice was quiet.

Far quieter than before.

"To look at your hands and recognize them, but not understand why they feel so foreign? To breathe and realize that even something as simple as breathing has become an imitation of what you used to do?"

She looked at her palms.

"I used to be an elf."

The words came out slowly.

"I used to have a name. A Familia. Companions. I had a home, and people who trusted me. I had a goddess—or rather, a god. I had a purpose."

Her fingers tightened.

"And then I died."

Bell didn't interrupt.

"I remember the floor. The blood. The screaming. My companions were dying around me, and I couldn't save them. I fought until there was nothing left to give."

She swallowed.

"And when I finally died, I thought it was over."

Her expression twisted.

"But it wasn't."

The Dungeon's distant rumbling seemed to grow louder.

"I woke up again."

She pressed a hand against her chest.

"Something had been placed inside me. A magic stone. Something that should never have been there. My body was no longer mine, and the thing that brought me back didn't care whether I wanted to live."

Her voice began to crack.

"It kept me alive."

She laughed weakly.

"Isn't that wonderful? I could die, and it would bring me back. I could tear myself apart, and it would put me together again. I could beg for death, and it would simply refuse."

Her eyes became wet.

"Do you know what that means?"

Bell shook his head.

"It means I can't even have the dignity of dying."

A tear ran down her cheek.

Then another.

"I tried."

The words were almost inaudible.

"I tried everything. I thought perhaps if I destroyed this body, if I removed the thing inside my chest, if I simply stopped existing, then maybe..."

She stopped.

Her lips trembled.

"But I couldn't."

Bell's expression changed.

He had encountered many people who wanted to become stronger.

People who wanted power.

People who wanted to overcome their limitations.

But this was different.

Ein wasn't asking for strength.

She wasn't asking to be saved from an enemy.

She was asking for an end to something she could no longer endure.

And perhaps the most disturbing part was that she had been forced to endure it for so long that she had begun to regard suffering as her natural condition.

"I don't know what to call you," Bell said quietly.

Ein looked up.

"You're not a monster in the way you think you are."

Her eyes widened.

"Don't."

"I mean it."

"Don't say that."

"You were an adventurer. You were an elf. You were Filvis Chalia."

Her breathing became ragged.

"And now you're something else," Bell continued. "Something that was made from what remained of you. But that doesn't mean the person you were has disappeared."

Ein covered her face.

"Stop..."

Bell fell silent.

For the first time since the encounter began, he seemed unsure of what to say.

He could fight monsters.

He could dismantle traps.

He could analyze magical formations and devise ways to circumvent them.

But there was no obvious solution to someone who had spent years being convinced that their own existence was a punishment.

Then he asked a question.

"Are you doing all of this for your deity?"

Ein's hands stopped moving.

"Or is it for the Dungeon?"

Her lips parted.

"Why...?"

"Because I don't think you want to be here."

The words struck harder than any of his attacks.

Ein's face crumpled.

"YOU DON'T KNOW ANYTHING!"

The cry burst from her throat.

"YOU DON'T KNOW WHAT IT'S LIKE!"

She struck the ground.

The surrounding mana surged violently.

"Every day! Every single day, I have to feel it! I can hear it! I can feel it crawling through my body! It tells me where to go, what to do, what to protect! And when I try to resist, it reminds me that I don't even have the right to exist without it!"

Her voice broke.

"I was supposed to be dead!"

The Dungeon rumbled.

The Jewel Tree's branches trembled, scattering crystals across the ground.

"I was supposed to die with them!"

She fell to her knees.

"Why couldn't it just leave me there?"

Bell approached.

Slowly.

Carefully.

He stopped in front of her.

He crouched until their eyes were level.

"Ein."

She looked up.

"Do you want me to make it easier?"

Her lips quivered.

"What?"

"I think I can remove those constraints."

He looked at her chest.

"Whatever is binding your body to the Dungeon. Whatever is forcing you to keep existing in this condition. I believe I can separate it from you."

Ein stared at him.

For a moment, she couldn't understand the words.

Then she understood.

And that frightened her more than anything else.

"Can you...?"

"I can try."

"Don't make promises you can't keep."

"I won't."

He paused.

"But I won't lie to you either. I can see the mana connecting you to the Dungeon. I can see the foreign structure within your body. I don't know everything about how it works, but I believe I can sever the connection without destroying you."

Her eyes searched his face.

For deception.

For pity.

For some hidden intention.

She found none that she could recognize.

Only a strange, quiet determination.

"Why?"

Bell blinked.

"Why what?"

"Why would you help me?"

He considered the question.

Then answered honestly.

"Because you asked me to understand."

Ein lowered her head.

Her tears fell onto the stone floor.

She had spent so long being treated as something other than a person that she had almost forgotten what it felt like to be addressed as one.

And now a boy she had been ordered to capture was offering her something she had stopped believing existed.

A choice.

She closed her eyes.

"...Make it quick."

Bell nodded.

"All right."

Then she whispered something else.

"Please."

### IV. The Rite of Three Severances

Bell extended his right hand.

Ein watched it approach.

There was something peculiar about the boy's mana. She had noticed it during their battle, but now that he was standing so close, the difference was impossible to ignore.

It didn't behave like ordinary magic.

It wasn't simply an enormous quantity of energy waiting to be released. It was something more fundamental, something that could be shaped and directed without following the usual rules of spellcasting.

The mana around him responded to his presence.

It gathered.

It flowed.

And then, at his command, it became something else.

"Before I begin," Bell said, "I need you to understand something."

Ein looked at him.

"I can remove the foreign influence. I can sever the connection that's forcing your body to draw power from the Dungeon. I can attempt to remove the corruption that's keeping you in this condition."

He paused.

"But I cannot promise that everything will return to how it was before."

Ein lowered her gaze.

"I know."

"You might still have scars. Your body might not become what it once was. And even if I succeed, the memories won't disappear."

She gave a faint, almost humorless smile.

"Those are the things I'm least concerned about."

Bell nodded.

"Then let's begin."

His hand moved toward her chest.

Ein instinctively flinched.

It was an involuntary reaction, born from years of pain and violation. Her body remembered things her mind would rather forget.

Bell stopped.

"Sorry."

He waited.

It took several seconds before she managed to steady herself.

"...Continue."

His palm finally rested against her chest.

Beneath the strange, unhealthy surface of her body, something pulsed.

A magic stone.

It wasn't entirely foreign to her anymore. It had become a part of her existence, fused with her body and intertwined with whatever remained of her original life.

But it was not supposed to be there.

Bell closed his eyes.

The surrounding mana began to move.

Not toward him.

Not away from him.

It gathered around his hand, forming an intricate pattern that extended across Ein's body.

For a brief moment, the chamber became completely still.

Then Bell spoke.

"Depart, powers of darkness, sickness, and ill fortune."

His voice was quiet.

Yet the words seemed to carry through the very foundations of the Dungeon.

"With the purest light, set free this place and this soul."

A pale radiance emerged beneath his palm.

It wasn't the violent brilliance of an offensive spell.

It was gentle.

Almost warm.

"I break the bonds, I call back health and peace."

The light expanded.

Three distinct patterns appeared around Ein.

The first was a dark, twisted formation that extended from the magic stone in her chest, spreading throughout her body like the roots of a diseased tree.

The second was a dense network of foreign magical structures, intertwined with her flesh and the core that sustained her unnatural regeneration.

The third was something different.

A thread.

Thin, almost invisible.

It extended from her body into the Dungeon itself.

Bell opened his eyes.

*There you are.*

He understood the problem now.

Her condition wasn't the result of one curse.

It was the convergence of three separate afflictions.

The first was the corruption that had transformed her.

The second was the parasitic structure that had made her body dependent on the Dungeon's mana.

And the third was the binding that allowed the Dungeon's influence to reach her consciousness.

It was the reason she couldn't escape.

The reason she couldn't die.

The reason she couldn't even distinguish between her own desires and the impulses imposed upon her.

Bell tightened his fingers.

"First severance."

The dark formation trembled.

"Darkness."

The mana surrounding his hand changed.

A wave of light passed through Ein's body, illuminating every hidden connection within it.

The corrupted structures resisted.

They twisted.

They attempted to reinforce themselves.

For an instant, Bell felt something push back against his magic.

A distant consciousness.

Something enormous.

Something that had existed long before either of them.

The Dungeon.

Its response was immediate.

The surrounding walls groaned.

The ground beneath them shook.

The Jewel Tree's crystalline branches began to fracture.

But Bell didn't withdraw his hand.

He simply adjusted the flow of mana.

He wasn't attempting to overpower the Dungeon.

He was removing its influence from a single individual.

There was a difference.

And that difference mattered.

*You don't own her.*

The dark formation began to unravel.

One strand.

Then another.

The corruption that had spread throughout Ein's body slowly detached from the structure sustaining her existence.

She gasped.

A sharp sensation passed through her chest.

For the first time in years, she could feel something other than the constant pressure of the Dungeon.

It was painful.

But it was different.

It was her pain.

Not something imposed upon her.

"Second severance."

"Sickness."

The light deepened.

Bell focused on the magic stone.

He could feel its unnatural composition, the foreign energy embedded within it, and the countless connections that had formed between the stone and Ein's body.

Removing it entirely would be dangerous.

It was no longer merely an intruder.

It had become part of the structure keeping her alive.

Destroying it might destroy her.

So he did something else.

He separated the foreign influence from the core itself.

The parasitic structure began to peel away, dissolving into particles of pale light.

Ein's body convulsed.

Her fingers dug into the ground.

She wanted to scream.

But no sound came out.

Then, suddenly—

The pressure disappeared.

The constant hunger for mana.

The unnatural demand.

The endless cycle of regeneration.

All of it began to subside.

The magic stone remained.

But something fundamental had changed.

It no longer dictated the condition of her existence.

Bell exhaled.

One more.

He could feel the final connection.

The thread that stretched beyond her body, disappearing into the depths of the Dungeon.

It was thinner than the others.

But it was also the most stubborn.

"Third severance."

"Ill fortune."

Bell raised his left hand.

The mana in the chamber converged around Ein.

For an instant, the entire formation resembled a vast, luminous flower.

Then the thread snapped.

A sound like breaking glass reverberated through the chamber.

The Jewel Tree stopped trembling.

The mana flowing through the floor settled.

And the Dungeon—

For a brief, extraordinary moment—

Was silent.

Ein's body collapsed forward.

Bell caught her before she struck the ground.

Her breathing was shallow.

Her eyes were closed.

The magic stone in her chest gave one final pulse.

Then settled into a steady rhythm.

Bell remained still.

He examined the mana surrounding her.

No foreign influence.

No active parasitic network.

No connection forcing her body to draw power from the Dungeon.

The ritual had succeeded.

He lowered his gaze.

"How are you feeling?"

No response.

"Ein?"

Her eyelids fluttered.

Then she opened her eyes.

They were different.

The unhealthy redness had faded, leaving behind a deep, dark blue.

Her long black hair spilled across the floor.

The unnatural tension in her face had disappeared, though the exhaustion remained.

She stared at Bell.

Then at her hands.

Slowly, she raised one.

She opened and closed her fingers.

Once.

Twice.

She could feel them.

Not as extensions of something else.

Not as instruments.

As her own.

Her lips parted.

"...It's quiet."

Bell smiled faintly.

"Good."

She looked at him again.

"Where is it?"

"Where's what?"

"The voice."

Bell glanced toward the ceiling.

"The Dungeon?"

She nodded.

He shook his head.

"I can't hear anything from it through you anymore."

Ein stared at her chest.

Her fingers moved toward the place where the magic stone rested.

She could still feel it.

But it wasn't screaming.

It wasn't demanding.

It wasn't forcing her to move.

For the first time since her resurrection, she could exist without feeling something else breathing through her.

And then she began to cry.

Not the desperate, broken sobbing from before.

These were different.

Quiet tears.

The kind that came when someone finally realized that the nightmare had ended.

Bell remained beside her.

He didn't tell her to stop.

He didn't tell her everything would be fine.

He simply allowed her to cry.

After a while, Ein looked up.

"...Did you do it?"

Bell nodded.

"I think so."

She stared at him.

Then, very slowly, she placed her hand over his.

"Thank you."

The words were barely audible.

But they were genuine.

Bell returned her smile.

"You're welcome, Filvis."

She froze.

Her eyes widened.

"How..."

He tilted his head.

"How did I know?"

She nodded.

Bell glanced at her.

"Well, you told me your name was Ein. But you also told me you used to be an elf. You mentioned your Familia, your companions, and the people you lost."

He paused.

"I figured Ein wasn't the only name you had."

Her lips trembled.

"Filvis..."

She whispered it.

A name she hadn't allowed herself to use in so long.

"Filvis Chalia."

Bell nodded.

"It's a beautiful name."

And for the first time in a very long time, she didn't disagree.

### V. The Name Beneath the Monster

The chamber gradually returned to its former state.

The magical disturbances faded.

The Jewel Tree remained standing, although several of its crystalline branches had fallen during the ritual.

Bell sat against its trunk, examining his hand.

The Rite of Three Severances had consumed a considerable amount of mana.

Not Mind, exactly.

The distinction was important.

His method of manipulating external mana allowed him to avoid the usual limitations of conventional spellcasting, but it wasn't without cost. The precision required to separate three interconnected structures had demanded an extraordinary degree of concentration.

He could still feel the strain.

*That was closer than I expected.*

He glanced toward the elf sitting a short distance away.

Filvis was examining herself.

Her appearance hadn't returned completely to normal.

The unhealthy pallor remained, and the magic stone was still embedded in her chest. Her body still carried the unmistakable traces of what had happened to her.

But the unnatural pressure was gone.

She looked more like an injured adventurer than an imitation of one.

"Bell."

He looked up.

"Yes?"

"What happens now?"

It was a simple question.

Yet he understood the weight behind it.

For years, her existence had been defined by other people's expectations.

Her Familia.

Her deity.

The Dungeon.

The creature that had brought her back.

She had been given instructions, purposes, and obligations.

Even her suffering had become something she was expected to endure.

Now she had none of those things.

At least, not in the same way.

Bell considered his answer.

"That's up to you."

Filvis looked at him.

"What?"

"You can decide what happens next."

She lowered her eyes.

"I don't know how."

"Then start with something simple."

"Such as?"

"Whether you want to stay here or leave."

She looked around the chamber.

The Jewel Tree.

The remains of the trap.

The distant corridors.

The place where she had intended to capture him.

"...I want to leave."

"Then we'll leave."

She hesitated.

"Just like that?"

"Just like that."

"You're not going to ask me about Enyo?"

Bell blinked.

"Should I?"

Her expression changed.

"You're aware of him?"

"Not personally. But I know you weren't acting alone."

She looked away.

"Then you should understand that helping me could make you an enemy of someone dangerous."

Bell shrugged.

"I've had a few of those."

"You're taking this too lightly."

"Maybe."

He stood and offered his hand.

"But I don't think leaving someone in pain just because helping them might be inconvenient is particularly sensible."

Filvis stared at his hand.

Then she accepted it.

He pulled her upright.

She wavered slightly.

Bell immediately steadied her.

"Careful."

"I'm fine."

"You're clearly not."

She frowned.

"You sound like an old man."

Bell laughed.

"That's a first."

For a moment, something almost resembling a smile appeared on her face.

Then it faded.

"Bell."

"Hm?"

"What should I call myself?"

He looked at her.

She glanced down at her hands.

"Ein was the name I used after... everything. Filvis was the person I used to be. I don't know which one I am anymore."

Bell considered that.

Then he answered.

"Why not both?"

She looked at him.

"You don't have to pretend that nothing happened. And you don't have to throw away everything that came before it, either."

He smiled.

"You're still Filvis Chalia. You're also someone who survived something terrible. Those things can exist together."

Her expression softened.

"Both..."

"Besides, I don't think a name should be something other people decide for you."

Filvis remained silent.

Then she nodded.

"Filvis Chalia."

She repeated the name.

This time, it sounded more natural.

"That's what I'll use."

Bell smiled.

"Then it's settled."

He turned toward the corridor.

"Come on. Let's get you somewhere safe."

Filvis followed.

After several steps, she stopped.

Bell noticed.

"What's wrong?"

She looked back at the Jewel Tree.

At the place where the trap had been prepared.

At the spot where she had intended to capture him.

Then she looked at the boy who had dismantled her trap, defeated her in battle, listened to her lament, and finally done something no one had ever managed to do.

He had given her back the right to choose.

"...Bell."

"Yes?"

"Why did you really help me?"

He thought about it.

Then he gave her a rather simple answer.

"Because I could."

Filvis stared at him.

He continued walking.

For a moment, she remained where she was.

Then she followed.

And somewhere deep within the twenty-fifth floor, a small change had occurred.

A creature that had once belonged to the Dungeon was no longer connected to it.

A soul that had been trapped between life and death had finally been allowed to exist without chains.

And a name that had been buried beneath the identity of a monster had been spoken once more.

**Filvis Chalia.**

Not Ein.

Not the Banshee.

Not the creature.

Just Filvis.

Alive.
