# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

## Arc 8 — Crescendo

# Chapter Sixty-Five — The Under Resort

### I. A Necessary Trip

Bell Cranel had come to a realization.

He needed to visit the Dungeon.

Personally.

It was not that he lacked people capable of gathering information or handling ordinary errands. Quite the opposite, in fact. There were plenty of people who could take care of things in his stead.

However, there were certain matters that could only be addressed through firsthand experience.

Besides, he had been spending far too much time occupied with other affairs.

A little exploration might do him some good.

With that decision made, Bell began preparing for his departure.

His first stop was the equipment room.

The manor was enormous.

That was probably the first thing a visitor would notice upon entering the property. Its spacious corridors, numerous rooms, storage facilities, and various accommodations made it feel less like a residence and more like a small estate.

Sometimes, Bell wondered whether the building was unnecessarily large.

Then again, considering the number of things that happened under its roof, perhaps it wasn't.

He walked through the corridor, checking the equipment he intended to bring.

A few minutes later, the smell of freshly prepared food caught his attention.

Bell stopped.

He turned toward the kitchen.

Haruhime was standing beside the stove, carefully preparing breakfast. Her long golden hair was neatly arranged, and her fox ears occasionally twitched as she concentrated on her cooking.

She noticed him almost immediately.

"Good morning, Bell-sama."

"Good morning, Haruhime."

"Are you heading somewhere?"

"Yes. I'm planning to visit the Dungeon."

Her hands stopped moving momentarily.

"The Dungeon?"

"That's right. I intend to stay for a few days, perhaps a week, depending on how things go."

Haruhime looked at him with mild concern.

"Will you be going alone?"

"Most likely."

"Then perhaps I should prepare additional provisions for you."

Bell smiled.

"That would be appreciated, but don't trouble yourself too much. I've already prepared most of what I need."

"Nevertheless, I would feel better if I prepared something myself."

There was a certain determination in her voice.

Bell decided not to argue.

"All right. Thank you."

Haruhime smiled before returning to her cooking.

Bell continued toward the equipment room.

He had no particular schedule to follow. There were several things he wanted to investigate, some observations he wished to verify, and a few places he hadn't visited in quite some time.

Nothing particularly urgent.

For once, he intended to enjoy a relatively uncomplicated expedition.

Or at least, that was the plan.

***

His preparations took longer than expected.

Bell laid his equipment across a table and inspected everything individually.

"Rations, check."

He examined the food containers.

"Water, check."

Next came his tools, spare equipment, and other necessities.

"Medicine, check. Repair materials, check."

He paused.

There was something satisfying about preparing for an extended journey.

Unlike his usual activities, which frequently involved unexpected interruptions, political complications, and situations that somehow escalated beyond reasonable expectations, this was relatively straightforward.

He was going into the Dungeon.

He would explore.

He would hunt monsters.

He would return.

Simple.

At least, it sounded simple.

Bell gathered the supplies and placed them into his spatial storage.

The equipment disappeared one after another, leaving the table practically empty.

He checked his weapons one final time before securing them.

Everything was ready.

When he returned to the kitchen, Haruhime had already finished preparing a small package.

"For your journey, Bell-sama."

Bell accepted it.

"Thank you. I'll make sure to eat it."

"Please take care of yourself."

"I will."

He gave her a reassuring smile before finally leaving the manor.

The morning air was pleasant.

The city was already bustling with activity, adventurers moving through the streets while merchants opened their shops and carriages passed along the roads.

Bell walked toward the Dungeon entrance.

He had no intention of rushing.

After all, he had a week.

There was no reason to hurry.

***

### II. An Unwelcome Welcome

The Dungeon greeted him with familiar hostility.

Bell stepped onto the fifteenth floor.

He hadn't even been there for particularly long when something caught his attention.

Several large figures emerged from the surrounding passages.

Minotaurs.

Bell stopped walking.

The monsters stopped as well.

For a brief moment, they simply stared at one another.

Then the Minotaurs roared.

"...Seriously?"

They charged.

Bell sighed.

He had expected monsters, naturally.

He simply hadn't expected such an enthusiastic welcome.

He moved aside as the first Minotaur swung its enormous weapon. The impact struck the ground, scattering fragments of stone across the passage.

Another approached from the opposite direction.

Bell raised his hand.

An explosion erupted.

The monster was thrown backward, crashing into the others.

He moved through the resulting confusion, avoiding their attacks while returning fire whenever an opening presented itself.

It was not particularly difficult.

Still, the number of monsters was somewhat irritating.

After dealing with the immediate threat, Bell continued deeper.

Another group appeared.

Then another.

By the time he reached the seventeenth floor, he had encountered more Minotaurs than he cared to count.

He eventually found a relatively quiet place to rest.

Bell sat down and leaned against a rocky formation.

"...What was that?"

He looked toward the passage he had come from.

The monsters had been unusually aggressive.

Not that Minotaurs were particularly friendly creatures to begin with, but there was something almost personal about the way they had pursued him.

He considered the possibilities.

Perhaps they were responding to his presence.

Perhaps there was something unusual about the Dungeon's current monster population.

Or perhaps he was simply unlucky.

Bell dismissed the thought.

"Likely hatred."

He took out some food from his storage and began eating.

The seventeenth floor was not exactly an ideal resting location, but it would suffice.

He finished his meal and drank some water.

For several minutes, everything remained quiet.

Then—

*Rumble.*

Bell stopped moving.

The sound came from somewhere nearby.

The ground trembled.

Dust fell from the ceiling, and several loose stones rolled across the floor.

Bell slowly raised his head.

"...Oh?"

The wall ahead of him began to distort.

A massive shape pushed through the Dungeon's surface, tearing apart the surrounding rock as it emerged.

Its enormous body was covered in thick, pale flesh. Its grotesque head and immense frame made its identity unmistakable.

The Monster Rex.

Goliath.

Bell stared at it.

He had heard of the creature before.

A monster of considerable significance, one whose appearance was hardly an ordinary occurrence.

More importantly, something like this could be worth quite a lot.

Bell's eyes briefly examined its enormous body.

"Hm. Interesting."

The Goliath roared.

The sound reverberated throughout the floor.

Bell tilted his head.

"...I expected something more impressive."

That was probably an unfair assessment.

Nevertheless, he couldn't help feeling somewhat underwhelmed.

The creature advanced.

Bell raised his hand.

A magic circle formed.

Then another.

And another.

The Goliath barely had time to react.

**BOOOOOOM!**

An explosion swallowed its enormous frame.

The surrounding air shook violently.

The impact scattered debris across the floor, and the resulting smoke obscured everything ahead.

Bell remained where he was.

He waited.

A few seconds passed.

The smoke gradually cleared.

The Goliath was gone.

Not merely defeated.

There was practically nothing left to indicate that the Monster Rex had ever existed.

Bell looked at the empty space.

"...Well."

He lowered his hand.

That had been rather anticlimactic.

He briefly considered whether there might have been something worth recovering, but the result of his attack made that prospect rather unlikely.

A small inconvenience.

Nothing more.

Bell dusted off his clothes and continued toward the lower floors.

He had already spent enough time on the seventeenth floor.

Besides, there was somewhere he wanted to visit.

The eighteenth floor.

The Under Resort.

***

### III. The Under Resort

The transition was almost immediate.

One moment, Bell was walking through the familiar stone corridors of the Dungeon.

The next, he found himself surrounded by an entirely different environment.

The eighteenth floor.

A vast underground paradise.

Unlike the hostile passages above, this floor possessed an almost tranquil atmosphere. Its enormous cavern stretched across the horizon, illuminated by the artificial sky and the enormous crystal formations scattered throughout the area.

Trees, grass, and flowing water created an environment that seemed almost impossible to exist within the Dungeon.

It was beautiful.

Bell stopped for a moment.

He looked around.

"...It's been a while."

The eighteenth floor had always been an interesting place.

A safe zone.

A resting point for adventurers who had survived the dangers of the upper floors.

There was something almost amusing about finding such a peaceful environment deep beneath the surface.

Bell began walking.

He hadn't gone very far when something caught his attention.

A familiar presence.

He stopped.

His expression changed slightly.

It was difficult to describe.

A feeling of recognition, almost like encountering something from a distant memory.

He had felt something similar before.

Someone he had met.

Someone he had fought.

Bell tilted his head.

"...Who?"

He concentrated.

The presence was unmistakable, although he couldn't immediately identify its owner.

He began walking in that direction.

As he approached, the familiar aura became clearer.

His thoughts gradually settled on a particular individual.

A certain prum.

Bell's expression became slightly more complicated.

"...No way."

He continued forward.

And eventually, he found him.

***

### IV. The Unexpected Explosion

**Finn's POV**

*BOOOOOOM!*

Finn's head turned toward the source of the explosion.

The sound echoed throughout the seventeenth floor, powerful enough to be heard even from a considerable distance.

He immediately stopped.

"...What was that?"

His companions also turned toward the disturbance.

The expedition had finally reached its conclusion.

Nearly two months.

That was how long they had spent away from Orario.

Two months of navigating the Dungeon's deeper floors, fighting powerful monsters, managing supplies, and ensuring that everyone returned alive.

It had been a demanding expedition.

Naturally, there were still matters to attend to.

However, the most immediate concern was the enormous monster that had appeared on the seventeenth floor.

Goliath.

Finn narrowed his eyes.

"Was that the Monster Rex?"

One of the adventurers beside him nodded.

"It sounded like an explosion."

Finn looked toward the source.

They had originally intended to deal with Goliath themselves.

That was part of the plan for their return journey.

After all, encountering a Monster Rex was an opportunity that shouldn't be casually wasted.

Its materials and magic stone were valuable, and defeating it would be a suitable conclusion to their expedition.

But now...

Finn considered the possibility that another group had already engaged it.

Or perhaps the monster had appeared unexpectedly and attacked someone.

Either way, he wanted to investigate.

"Let's move."

His companions followed.

Finn increased his pace, moving through the passageway toward the source of the disturbance.

The closer he approached, the more curious he became.

That explosion...

There was something familiar about it.

He couldn't immediately identify what.

Perhaps it was simply the nature of the magic.

Perhaps he was overthinking.

Then the smoke began to clear.

Finn slowed down.

A figure stood in the middle of the passage.

White hair.

A familiar silhouette.

A young adventurer whose appearance had remained surprisingly memorable despite the passage of time.

Finn stopped.

"..."

For several seconds, he simply stared.

The figure turned around.

Their eyes met.

And then—

"Oh."

The young man raised his hand in greeting.

"Hello."

Finn blinked.

Once.

Twice.

"...Bell?"

The boy smiled.

"Long time no see, Finn."

For a moment, Finn didn't know what to say.

Then he laughed.

A genuine, somewhat surprised laugh.

"Well, I'll be damned."

He walked closer.

"What are you doing here?"

Bell shrugged.

"Exploring."

"Exploring?"

"Yes."

Finn glanced around.

Then he looked at the enormous space where Goliath had presumably been.

"...Did you happen to encounter something?"

Bell followed his gaze.

"Oh, that?"

He pointed casually toward the empty space.

"Yes. There was a monster."

Finn stared at him.

"A monster?"

"A rather large one."

"...Goliath?"

"That's what I assumed."

Finn looked at the empty space again.

There was no corpse.

No visible remains.

Not even the usual evidence of a prolonged battle.

He slowly turned back toward Bell.

"Where is it?"

Bell considered the question.

"...Gone?"

Finn closed his eyes briefly.

He had spent nearly two months exploring the Dungeon, fighting dangerous monsters, and carefully managing his resources.

And now he was standing in front of someone who had apparently eliminated a Monster Rex before he could even reach the scene.

He sighed.

"I see."

Bell tilted his head.

"Was there something wrong?"

"No. Nothing particularly wrong."

Finn smiled.

"Although I must admit, I wasn't expecting to return from an expedition and immediately find you destroying the scenery."

Bell chuckled.

"Sorry about that."

"Don't apologize. I'm simply curious."

Finn examined him.

The last time they had properly met, Bell had been considerably younger.

Not that much time had necessarily passed for the boy himself.

But the circumstances surrounding their previous encounter had been rather memorable.

Finn remembered their duel.

The unusual tactics.

The magic.

The explosions.

And, perhaps most importantly, the conversation that followed.

Bell had refused to join the Loki Familia.

Finn hadn't forgotten that.

He also hadn't forgotten the reason.

The boy wanted to pursue his own path.

And apparently, he was still doing exactly that.

"How have you been?" Finn asked.

"Busy."

"Busy?"

"Very."

Bell's answer was immediate.

Finn laughed.

"That sounds rather vague."

"It is."

"Anything interesting?"

Bell thought about it.

There were quite a few things he could mention.

Unfortunately, explaining even half of them would probably take several hours.

"...A lot has happened."

Finn raised an eyebrow.

"That sounds suspicious."

"Does it?"

"Quite."

Bell smiled.

Finn shook his head.

"Well, I suppose I'll hear about it eventually."

He looked toward the lower floors before returning his attention to Bell.

"How long have you been here?"

"I arrived today."

"Today?"

"Yes. I was planning to stay for a few days."

Finn nodded.

"Then you picked an interesting day to visit."

"Because of Goliath?"

"Among other things."

Bell looked at him.

"How about you?"

"Returning from an expedition. Nearly two months away from the city."

"Two months?"

"That's right."

Bell nodded thoughtfully.

"That explains it."

"Explains what?"

"Why I haven't seen you."

Finn laughed again.

"Indeed. I suppose disappearing into the Dungeon for two months will do that."

He glanced at Bell.

"Although I imagine you've been rather occupied yourself."

Bell gave him an innocent smile.

"Perhaps."

Finn recognized that expression.

It was the sort of expression that suggested the boy had several stories he wasn't particularly interested in explaining.

He decided not to press.

For now.

***

### V. A Familiar Face

The two stood facing one another.

There was something strangely comfortable about the encounter.

Their previous meeting had been anything but ordinary.

Finn had challenged Bell, tested his abilities, and eventually found himself reconsidering several assumptions about the boy.

Yet here they were.

Meeting again in the middle of the Dungeon.

Without a duel.

Without negotiations.

Without any particular reason to be hostile toward one another.

Just two adventurers who happened to cross paths.

Finn looked at Bell again.

"You've changed."

Bell blinked.

"Have I?"

"Certainly."

Finn smiled.

"Although I can't quite put my finger on it."

"Maybe you've simply forgotten what I looked like."

"Unlikely. You made quite an impression."

Bell chuckled.

"I'll take that as a compliment."

"You should."

Finn crossed his arms.

"Still, I didn't expect to encounter you here."

"Neither did I."

"Do you often visit the eighteenth floor?"

"Not particularly."

"Then perhaps we're both fortunate today."

Bell smiled.

For a moment, neither spoke.

The distant sounds of adventurers moving through the safe zone filled the silence.

Finn eventually glanced toward his companions, who had been watching the exchange with varying degrees of curiosity.

"Ah. I should introduce you properly."

He turned slightly.

"This is Bell Cranel. We've met before."

Bell offered a polite greeting.

"Nice to meet you."

Finn continued.

"He is... an interesting adventurer."

Bell looked at him.

"That's an unusual introduction."

"Would you prefer something else?"

"Maybe something less suspicious."

Finn laughed.

"I'll consider it."

He turned back toward Bell.

"Anyway, what are your plans now?"

"I was going to find somewhere to rest."

"Alone?"

"Most likely."

Finn considered that.

Then he smiled.

"Well, since we're both here, why don't you join us for a while?"

Bell blinked.

"Join you?"

"We've just returned from an expedition. I'm sure everyone could use a little time to relax."

He paused.

"And I wouldn't mind catching up with you."

Bell looked at the group behind Finn.

Then he looked back at the captain.

For once, he didn't have any particularly urgent business.

His supplies were prepared.

His schedule was flexible.

And the opportunity to speak with someone he hadn't seen in a long time was certainly welcome.

"...Sure."

Finn smiled.

"Excellent."

Bell returned the smile.

"Besides, I have quite a few things to tell you."

Finn raised an eyebrow.

"Do you?"

"Yes."

"Should I be worried?"

"Probably not."

"That's not reassuring."

Bell laughed.

And Finn found himself doing the same.

Two months away from Orario.

An expedition that had finally reached its conclusion.

And an unexpected encounter with someone he hadn't seen in quite some time.

Finn had expected a relatively ordinary return to the city.

Apparently, that expectation had been misplaced.

But perhaps that wasn't such a bad thing.

He looked at the white-haired boy standing before him.

"Well then, Bell."

"Yes?"

"Let's catch up."

Bell smiled.

"Gladly."

For the first time in nearly two months, Finn found himself looking forward to something that had absolutely nothing to do with the Dungeon.

And for Bell, who had spent what felt like an entire lifetime dealing with extraordinary circumstances, it was rather refreshing to simply meet an old acquaintance.

No grand objective.

No complicated arrangements.

No immediate danger.

Just a familiar face.

A long-awaited reunion.

And perhaps, if they were fortunate, a peaceful afternoon beneath the artificial sky of the Under Resort.

**End.**
