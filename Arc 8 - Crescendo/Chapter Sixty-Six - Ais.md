# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

## Chapter Sixty-Six — The Sword Princess and the Airhead

The camp was almost ready.

The expedition had taken more out of them than Ais had expected. Even after the immediate danger had passed, the lingering unease remained, like the unpleasant sensation of walking through a corridor while knowing something was watching from the darkness.

The Dungeon had been unusually troublesome.

The violas, the corrupted spirit, and Revis.

Three different problems, each dangerous enough to demand their attention. Yet Ais couldn't shake the feeling that they were connected somehow. Perhaps not directly, but there was something wrong with the pattern of events. The Dungeon had always been dangerous, but lately, its dangers seemed to be taking on a different character.

More deliberate.

More malicious.

She sat quietly near the edge of the temporary camp, her sword resting beside her. Her golden eyes followed the adventurers moving around the clearing. Some were arranging supplies, others were checking their equipment, and a few were already settling down after the exhausting expedition.

The familiar sounds of camp preparation gradually filled the area.

Ais lowered her gaze to her hands.

*I wasn't strong enough.*

The thought came without warning, though it had been following her for some time. She remembered the battles, the moments when her blade had failed to reach its target, and the occasions when someone else had needed to intervene. She remembered the feeling of standing before an enemy whose strength exceeded her own, knowing that every second spent hesitating could cost someone their life.

She had become stronger.

She knew that.

Her abilities had improved, her swordsmanship had advanced, and she had overcome enemies that once would have been impossible for her to defeat. Yet the distance between herself and the things she wanted to destroy remained frustratingly large. No matter how much she trained, the world continued producing monsters and enemies that demanded more from her.

*If I become stronger, I can protect more people.*

Her fingers curled slightly.

*If my sword is strong enough, I won't have to watch someone else fight in my place. I won't have to wait for someone to save me.*

Ais looked toward the darkened forest beyond the camp.

There were things she wanted to destroy.

Things she wanted to cut down with her own hands.

And for that, she needed strength.

"Ais?"

She turned.

Lefiya stood nearby, holding a small bundle of firewood. Her expression was polite, although a little tired.

"May I light the campfire?"

Ais nodded.

"Yes."

"Thank you."

Lefiya placed the firewood in the designated area and began preparing the kindling. She carefully arranged the smaller branches before reaching for her magic equipment.

Ais watched her for a moment.

Lefiya was a capable mage. Her magic was powerful, and her control had improved considerably through their expeditions. Although she could sometimes become overly emotional, especially when something unexpected happened, Ais had never doubted her ability.

The elf concentrated.

A small flame appeared.

Then—

BOOOOOOM!

The explosion shook the entire clearing.

Ais immediately rose to her feet.

Her hand reached for her sword before the sound had even finished echoing through the surrounding trees. Dust and smoke burst outward, scattering loose branches and sending several adventurers stumbling backward.

"What happened?!"

"Enemy attack!"

"Everyone, weapons ready!"

The camp erupted into movement.

Ais stepped forward, placing herself between the disturbance and the nearest group of adventurers. Her eyes narrowed as she examined the smoke.

That wasn't an ordinary explosion.

The force had been concentrated, and its output was far beyond what a simple campfire accident could produce.

*High-class magic?*

She assessed the lingering energy in the air.

The explosion's output was considerable. Based on what she could sense, the magic might have belonged to an adventurer around Level Five or even Level Six.

But that was only an estimate.

The Dungeon was full of unexpected possibilities, and she had learned not to underestimate an unknown enemy.

"Everyone, form a perimeter!"

Finn's voice cut through the commotion.

The small captain had already moved toward the front. His expression was calm, but his eyes were alert.

"Don't approach carelessly. Check the surroundings first. We don't know whether that was an attack or a diversion."

Several adventurers immediately followed his orders.

Ais moved toward the edge of the clearing.

She could hear the sounds of weapons being drawn, boots moving through the dirt, and people calling out to one another. The smoke was slowly clearing, revealing the area where the explosion had occurred.

There was no immediate sign of a monster.

No group of adventurers.

No second attack.

Only one person stood in the middle of the disturbed ground.

A young man.

White hair.

Red eyes.

A familiar face.

Ais stopped.

"...Bell?"

The young man looked toward the gathered adventurers.

He seemed almost embarrassed.

"Ah. Sorry about that."

Ais stared.

He had arrived with an explosion.

And now he was apologizing as though he had accidentally stepped on someone's foot.

*Bell Cranel.*

The name immediately brought back several memories.

The duel with Finn.

The confrontation with Bete.

The strange circumstances surrounding his recruitment attempt.

And the sheer difference between his appearance and the strength he had demonstrated.

Ais had seen many adventurers in her life. Some were strong, some were talented, and some possessed unusual abilities that made them difficult to evaluate.

Bell was different.

There was something about him that refused to fit neatly into her understanding of strength.

He looked young.

He acted casually.

And yet, he had defeated Finn in a duel.

He had also toyed with Bete, despite the werewolf's considerable combat experience.

Ais had not forgotten those events.

"Bell Cranel."

Finn approached him, his expression gradually relaxing.

"That was quite an entrance."

Bell rubbed the back of his head.

"I didn't mean to cause trouble."

"Then I assume you have a reasonable explanation?"

"I was trying to get here quickly."

Finn looked at the disturbed ground.

"By exploding your way into camp?"

Bell glanced behind himself.

"...It seemed faster."

For a moment, Finn said nothing.

Then he smiled.

"I see."

Ais tilted her head slightly.

*That isn't a reasonable explanation.*

Bell looked around.

"Is everyone all right?"

"Aside from a few startled adventurers and the unfortunate condition of our camp entrance, everyone appears unharmed," Finn answered.

"That's good."

Ais continued observing him.

He didn't appear injured. His breathing was steady, and there was no visible sign that the explosion had placed any significant strain on him.

But that didn't mean anything.

She remembered his previous fights.

He had a habit of making dangerous things look ordinary.

"You're alone?" Finn asked.

Bell nodded.

"Yes."

"And what brings you here?"

"I wanted to meet you."

Finn raised an eyebrow.

"Me?"

"Well, you and the others."

Bell looked around the clearing.

"I heard you were conducting an expedition, so I thought I might find you here."

Finn studied him for a moment before glancing toward the surrounding adventurers.

"Well, you've certainly found us."

Bell smiled awkwardly.

"I suppose I have."

Ais remained silent.

She was still trying to understand something.

*How did he become this strong?*

It had only been a few months since their previous encounters.

A few months.

That wasn't enough time for an ordinary adventurer to develop such an extraordinary difference in strength. Even with exceptional talent, the growth he had demonstrated was difficult to explain.

Was he Level One?

Level Two?

Or was his power unrelated to his Falna?

She remembered the way he had fought Finn. His movements, his timing, and the way he had handled the exchange were not something she would expect from an inexperienced adventurer.

There was more to him than his apparent level.

Perhaps much more.

"You're staring."

Ais blinked.

Bell was looking at her.

She tilted her head.

"Am I?"

"A little."

"Sorry."

Bell smiled.

"It's fine."

Ais looked at him for another moment.

*He's still the same.*

At least, his expression was.

His strength had changed, but the way he spoke and behaved seemed almost identical to what she remembered.

It was strange.

She had met many powerful adventurers who carried themselves differently. Some were arrogant, others were intimidating, and a few seemed to enjoy making certain that everyone knew how strong they were.

Bell didn't seem interested in any of that.

He simply stood there, smiling as though arriving through an explosion was perfectly normal.

"Bell."

Finn's voice drew his attention.

"Since you're here, would you like to stay with us for a while?"

Bell glanced toward the camp.

"Would that be all right?"

"Of course. We've already suffered the damage from your arrival. It would be rather wasteful to send you away now."

Bell laughed quietly.

"That's fair."

Finn gestured toward the camp.

"Come along. We can talk properly after everyone has settled down."

"Thank you."

Bell followed him.

Ais watched as the two walked past.

She remembered something.

He had rejected Loki Familia's recruitment attempt.

At the time, she hadn't understood why someone with such unusual abilities would refuse an invitation from one of Orario's strongest Familias. The opportunity to join them would have provided him with resources, support, and access to experienced adventurers.

But he had declined.

He wanted to make his own path.

Ais didn't know whether that was admirable or foolish.

Perhaps both.

She returned her attention to the camp.

And then she noticed Lefiya.

The elf had been standing near the supplies, apparently attempting to organize several pieces of equipment.

She was also staring at Bell.

Not casually.

Not briefly.

She was staring at him with such intensity that Ais wondered whether something was wrong.

"Lefiya?"

The elf immediately flinched.

"Y-YES?!"

Ais blinked.

"Are you all right?"

"Y-Yes! Of course! I'm perfectly fine!"

Lefiya straightened her back.

Too quickly.

The bundle of supplies in her hands slipped.

A small container rolled across the ground.

Lefiya looked down.

Then at Ais.

Then toward Bell.

Then back at the container.

"...Ah."

She hurriedly bent down to retrieve it.

Unfortunately, she moved too quickly and nearly knocked her forehead against the wooden crate beside her.

"Ow!"

Ais stared.

Lefiya stood upright again, rubbing her forehead.

"...I'm fine."

Ais tilted her head.

"Are you sure?"

"Absolutely!"

"Your forehead is red."

"It's nothing!"

Lefiya looked away.

Ais followed her gaze.

Bell was speaking with Finn.

The elf immediately looked in another direction.

Ais considered this.

*Is she sick?*

Perhaps the explosion had startled her more than the others.

Or perhaps she had simply become exhausted during the expedition.

Elves were generally composed, although Lefiya could be unusually expressive when something upset her.

Ais stepped closer.

"You should rest."

Lefiya froze.

"R-Rest?"

"Yes."

"No! I don't need to rest!"

"You look tired."

"I-I am not tired!"

Ais stared at her.

Lefiya stared back.

For a few seconds, neither moved.

Then Lefiya suddenly turned away.

"I have something to do!"

She walked toward the supplies.

Ais watched her go.

*She's definitely acting strangely.*

***

The campfire was eventually lit.

Properly, this time.

The adventurers gradually returned to their usual activities, and the disturbance caused by Bell's arrival became another amusing topic of conversation.

Some were still complaining about the damaged ground.

Others were curious about the young adventurer who had arrived with such an excessive display of magic.

Bell, for his part, seemed entirely comfortable.

He sat near Finn and answered several questions about his recent activities. His responses were polite, although Ais noticed that he occasionally avoided giving particularly detailed answers.

He was careful.

Not necessarily secretive, but careful.

Ais found herself listening.

"Have you been traveling alone?" Finn asked.

"Mostly."

"That sounds troublesome."

"It can be."

"And dangerous."

Bell smiled.

"Sometimes."

Finn rested his chin against one hand.

"You make it sound rather ordinary."

"I suppose I've gotten used to it."

Ais looked toward him.

*Used to it?*

That was an interesting answer.

Most adventurers didn't become accustomed to danger. They learned to manage it, prepare for it, and survive it.

But becoming used to it was something different.

It suggested experience.

Perhaps more experience than his appearance implied.

"Your arrival certainly made an impression," Finn continued.

Bell looked toward the damaged ground.

"I'll try to be more careful next time."

"Please do. We would appreciate having a camp left when we wake up tomorrow."

Bell laughed.

"I'll keep that in mind."

Ais sat a short distance away.

She listened to the conversation while occasionally glancing toward the fire.

The flames moved gently, casting warm light over the faces of the adventurers. The earlier tension had mostly disappeared, replaced by the familiar atmosphere of a temporary camp.

Yet her attention kept returning to Bell.

She wanted to know how strong he had become.

More importantly, she wanted to understand how he fought.

His battle with Finn had been unusual. His movements had not followed the patterns she expected, and his approach to combat seemed to rely on more than physical ability.

Then there was the explosion.

Ais remembered its output.

If he could produce magic of that magnitude, then fighting him would be an entirely different experience from fighting a swordsman.

She looked down at her sword.

*I want to fight him.*

The thought came naturally.

She wanted to see his movements.

His reactions.

The way he responded when an opponent pressured him.

She wanted to know what he would do when faced with someone who could attack him from close range.

And perhaps, more importantly, she wanted to know whether she could keep up.

*If I fight someone stronger, I can learn.*

Her gaze returned to Bell.

*If I understand how he fights, I might become stronger.*

It was a simple thought.

Ais had never been particularly good at explaining why she wanted to fight someone. She simply knew that there were things she could only understand through battle.

Words could describe strength.

Training could improve technique.

But there was something different about standing in front of an opponent and testing herself against them.

That was where she learned the most.

"Bell!"

Lefiya's voice suddenly rang out.

Ais looked up.

The elf was standing near the young adventurer, holding a cup.

"Would you—"

She stopped.

Bell turned toward her.

"Yes?"

Lefiya's face became noticeably red.

"I-I mean... would you like something to drink?"

Bell looked at the cup.

"Oh. Thank you."

He accepted it.

Lefiya nodded rapidly.

"Y-You're welcome!"

She turned around.

Then walked directly into a wooden post.

*THUNK.*

Ais blinked.

The cup in Bell's hand remained perfectly steady.

Lefiya stood motionless.

Slowly, she raised one hand to her forehead.

"...Ow."

Bell stared.

"Are you all right?"

"Y-YES!"

Ais tilted her head.

"Again?"

Lefiya turned toward her.

"A-Ais!"

"What?"

"N-Nothing!"

The elf hurried away.

Bell watched her retreating figure.

"...Is she feeling unwell?"

Ais considered the question.

"I think so."

"Should we do something?"

Ais looked toward Lefiya.

The elf had stopped near the supplies and was now pretending to inspect a bundle of rope.

"Maybe she needs to rest."

Bell nodded.

"That's probably a good idea."

Ais looked at him.

He seemed genuinely concerned.

*He's kind.*

That was another thing she had noticed about him.

He didn't seem particularly interested in drawing attention to himself, but he was quick to notice when someone might be hurt.

Even after arriving through an explosion, he had immediately asked whether everyone was all right.

Ais found that interesting.

She looked toward the fire again.

The flames flickered.

For a while, the camp settled into a comfortable silence.

***

Later, when the conversation had become less crowded, Ais approached Bell.

He was standing a little away from the others, looking toward the surrounding forest.

The light from the campfire illuminated one side of his face.

"Bell."

He turned.

"Ais?"

She stopped in front of him.

For a moment, she simply looked at him.

She had already decided what she wanted to ask.

But she wasn't entirely certain how to begin.

"You're stronger."

Bell blinked.

"Am I?"

Ais nodded.

"Yes."

He smiled faintly.

"That's good, I suppose."

Ais tilted her head.

"You don't seem surprised."

"Should I be?"

She considered that.

"No."

Bell laughed softly.

Ais looked toward his hands.

Then at the sword he carried.

She remembered the duel with Finn.

The way he had moved.

The way he had controlled the fight.

And the way he had used his abilities without revealing everything he could do.

There were still many things she didn't understand.

"Will you spar with me?"

Bell's expression changed slightly.

"...A spar?"

Ais nodded.

"Yes."

He looked at her for a moment.

"Why?"

The question made her pause.

She had expected him to ask when.

Not why.

Ais considered her answer.

"I want to know how strong you are."

Bell remained silent.

"And I want to see how you fight."

"Is that all?"

Ais tilted her head.

"I also want to become stronger."

Bell's expression softened.

"Ah."

Ais looked directly at him.

"Will you?"

Bell glanced toward the campfire.

Several adventurers were still talking nearby. Finn appeared to be discussing something with Riveria, while Lefiya was pretending not to watch them from the other side of the clearing.

Bell looked back at Ais.

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

"You know I don't always fight with a sword, right?"

Ais nodded.

"I know."

"I use magic, too."

"I know."

"And I might use other things."

Ais considered that.

"That's fine."

Bell raised an eyebrow.

"You want a full spar?"

"Yes."

Ais answered without hesitation.

She wanted to understand his actual fighting style.

A sword-only match would tell her something, but not enough. If Bell relied on magic, then she needed to experience that magic. If he possessed unusual techniques, she wanted to see them.

She didn't want an incomplete picture.

Bell studied her expression.

Then he smiled.

"You're serious."

Ais nodded.

"Yes."

He looked away briefly.

"Well..."

Ais waited.

Bell scratched his cheek.

"Sure."

Her eyes widened slightly.

"Really?"

"Really."

Ais felt something inside her settle.

He had agreed.

But Bell raised one finger.

"Perhaps not tonight."

Ais blinked.

"Why?"

He gestured toward the camp.

"Because I just arrived, and I think everyone would prefer not to have another explosion tonight."

Ais looked toward the damaged ground.

That was reasonable.

"Tomorrow?"

Bell looked at her.

"Tomorrow?"

Ais nodded.

"Yes."

He thought for a moment.

Then smiled.

"All right. Tomorrow."

Ais nodded once.

"Good."

"Although..."

She tilted her head.

"What?"

Bell looked toward the camp.

"Perhaps we should find somewhere away from everyone."

Ais nodded again.

"Yes."

"I'd rather not destroy the camp."

"That's reasonable."

Bell smiled.

"And we should probably agree on a few conditions."

Ais listened.

"No lethal attacks. We stop if either of us decides to stop. And we'll choose a place where nobody else gets caught in the middle."

Ais considered his words.

"All right."

"Also, I'd prefer to know what kind of spar you're expecting."

Ais looked at him.

"Everything."

Bell blinked.

"Everything?"

"Your sword. Your magic. Your other abilities."

She paused.

"I want to fight the real you."

For a moment, Bell didn't answer.

His expression became thoughtful.

Then he gave a small smile.

"That's a rather demanding request."

Ais tilted her head.

"Is it?"

"A little."

"Sorry."

Bell laughed.

"No, it's fine. I agreed, didn't I?"

Ais nodded.

"Yes."

"Then I suppose I'll have to prepare."

She looked at him.

"Will you hold back?"

Bell's smile became slightly awkward.

"That depends."

"On what?"

"On how much I need to."

Ais stared at him.

She wasn't satisfied with that answer.

But she also understood that he was trying to be careful.

She had no intention of asking him to injure her.

She simply wanted to fight.

"Don't underestimate me," she said.

Bell's expression changed.

Only slightly.

But she noticed.

He looked at her more carefully.

Then he nodded.

"I won't."

Ais felt pleased.

That was what she wanted to hear.

Not an empty promise.

Not a polite dismissal.

He had acknowledged her request.

And he had agreed to take her seriously.

"Tomorrow, then," Bell said.

Ais nodded.

"Tomorrow."

***

Unfortunately, someone had overheard them.

"A-A SPAR?!"

Ais turned.

Lefiya stood several steps away.

Her eyes were wide.

Her face had lost its usual composure.

Ais blinked.

"Yes."

"Y-You mean... tomorrow?!"

Ais nodded.

Lefiya looked between them.

Then at Bell.

Then at Ais.

Then back at Bell.

"Y-You can't just—"

She stopped.

Bell tilted his head.

"Can't just what?"

Lefiya's mouth opened.

Closed.

Opened again.

"Y-You can't just agree to something like that so casually!"

Bell looked confused.

"Why not?"

"Because—!"

Lefiya stopped herself.

Her eyes darted toward Ais.

Then back to Bell.

"Because... because it's dangerous!"

Ais tilted her head.

"It's only a spar."

"That's not what I mean!"

Bell looked at Ais.

Ais looked at Bell.

Neither seemed to understand.

Lefiya pressed both hands against her temples.

"You're both acting as though this is perfectly normal!"

Ais considered that.

"It is."

"IT IS NOT!"

Bell coughed lightly.

"Well, we'll be careful."

Lefiya stared at him.

Then at Ais.

"Careful? You two are talking about fighting with magic and everything else! What if something goes wrong? What if one of you gets injured? What if—"

She stopped.

Her gaze lingered on Bell.

Then she quickly looked away.

"...What if the entire training ground gets destroyed?"

Bell smiled awkwardly.

"I'll try not to do that."

"That's not reassuring!"

Ais looked toward Lefiya.

"Do you want to watch?"

Lefiya froze.

"...What?"

"The spar."

Lefiya's expression became complicated.

She looked as though she had been offered something wonderful and terrifying at the same time.

"I..."

Her gaze shifted toward Bell.

He was still smiling.

Then she looked at Ais.

"...I suppose I should."

Ais nodded.

"All right."

Lefiya seemed to regret her answer almost immediately.

She turned around.

"I-I need to prepare!"

She walked away.

Two steps.

Three.

Then—

*THUNK.*

Ais watched her collide with the same wooden post.

Again.

Lefiya stood completely still.

Slowly, she raised one hand to her forehead.

"...Ow."

Bell slowly lowered his cup.

"...Is she really all right?"

Ais considered the question.

Then looked at the post.

Then at Lefiya.

"I don't know."

Lefiya turned around, her face burning.

"I'M FINE!"

Ais nodded.

"All right."

The elf hurried away before anyone could say anything else.

Bell watched her disappear behind the supplies.

"...Should we tell her to be more careful?"

Ais looked at the wooden post.

"Maybe."

Bell nodded.

Then glanced toward Ais.

"She seems rather worried about tomorrow."

Ais tilted her head.

"Why?"

Bell paused.

"...I'm not sure."

Ais considered that.

Perhaps Lefiya really was exhausted.

She had been working hard throughout the expedition, and the unexpected arrival had probably startled her.

Still, she hoped the elf would feel better by morning.

After all, she wanted her to watch.

***

The night gradually grew quieter.

The campfire burned steadily, and the adventurers began preparing for rest. Some had already retired to their tents, while others remained awake, talking in low voices.

Ais returned to her place near the fire.

Her sword rested beside her.

She looked at the flames.

Tomorrow.

She would fight Bell Cranel.

The thought brought a strange feeling of anticipation.

She had fought many opponents before. Some were stronger than her, others weaker, and a few had forced her to reconsider everything she understood about combat.

But Bell was different.

He had defeated Finn.

He had overwhelmed Bete.

He possessed magic powerful enough to create an explosion that had startled an entire camp.

And yet, she still didn't know what his limits were.

That uncertainty bothered her.

But it also made her curious.

*I want to see how he fights.*

She rested her hands on her knees.

*I want to know what he can do. How he moves, how he thinks, and how he reacts when someone attacks him.*

Her eyes followed the flames.

*If I can understand him, perhaps I can learn something.*

Ais knew that simply watching another adventurer wouldn't necessarily make her stronger. She had spent years observing others, studying their techniques, and learning from their mistakes.

But there was a difference between observing a fight and participating in one.

When she fought, she could feel the pressure of an opponent's movements. She could recognize the smallest changes in their stance, the rhythm of their breathing, and the moments when they committed to an attack.

She could learn things that no explanation could properly convey.

And Bell had many things she wanted to learn.

*I wonder what he'll use.*

Her gaze drifted toward the place where he had been sitting earlier.

She remembered his answer.

He wouldn't necessarily hold back.

That was good.

She didn't want him to treat her like someone who needed to be protected.

She wanted him to fight seriously.

Not recklessly.

Not carelessly.

But seriously.

Ais slowly looked down at her sword.

*Tomorrow, I'll become stronger.*

It was a simple thought.

One she had repeated countless times.

Yet tonight, it felt different.

Perhaps because she had finally found someone whose strength she couldn't properly measure.

Perhaps because she wanted to understand the strange adventurer who had rejected Loki Familia and continued walking his own path.

Or perhaps because, for the first time in a while, she felt genuinely excited about a fight that hadn't even begun.

Across the camp, Bell was speaking quietly with Finn.

Ais watched him for a moment.

He laughed at something the captain said.

Then he glanced toward the fire.

Their eyes met.

Bell smiled and raised one hand in a small greeting.

Ais returned the gesture with a slight nod.

Then she looked away.

Tomorrow.

She would find out how strong he really was.

And somewhere behind her, Lefiya was probably still worrying about the consequences of allowing two unusually dangerous adventurers to spar.

Ais didn't understand why.

It was only a spar.

How bad could it be?

**End of Chapter Sixty-Six.**
