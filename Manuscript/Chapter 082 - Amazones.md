# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

## Chapter Eighty-Two — Amazones

The sea was beginning to change.

Not because of the weather.

Not because of the currents.

But because something was approaching.

A fleet of ships moved steadily across the water, their sails stretched against the wind. Their hulls cut through the waves with practiced precision, carrying an army that had little interest in peaceful diplomacy.

The destination was Melen.

The port city was already visible in the distance.

On the largest vessel, a group of women occupied the deck.

Amazones.

Nearly two hundred warriors, each possessing a temperament shaped by a culture that valued strength, pride, and victory.

They were not ordinary travelers.

Their weapons were everywhere.

Swords.

Spears.

Axes.

Daggers.

Some sharpened their blades while others inspected their equipment. A few preferred not to rely on weapons at all.

Their fists and feet were more than sufficient.

Two women were currently exchanging blows near the center of the deck.

Their movements were fast and aggressive.

A punch narrowly missed its target.

A knee followed.

The opponent twisted aside, caught the attacking leg, and threw her across the deck.

The woman landed heavily.

Then laughed.

"Again!"

Her opponent grinned.

"Gladly!"

Neither appeared particularly concerned about the possibility of injury.

This was normal.

Combat was a familiar part of their lives.

And their Goddess had taught them that strength was something worth pursuing.

Kali.

She stood near the forward section of the ship, observing the approaching coastline.

Her expression was calm.

There was something almost childlike about her appearance, but the atmosphere surrounding her was anything but innocent.

She was their Goddess.

Their leader.

And the person whose expectations shaped the direction of their expedition.

"How long?"

A woman approached her.

"At the current speed, we'll reach the port shortly."

Kali nodded.

"Good."

Her gaze remained fixed on the distant city.

Melen.

A beautiful destination.

A place with beaches, merchants, and people who probably had very little understanding of what was approaching.

Behind the leading vessel sailed another ship.

It was equally large.

However, there was one significant difference.

Its passengers were not warriors.

They were men.

Several male slaves were chained together below deck, guarded by Amazones who treated their presence as an ordinary part of the expedition.

Some sat silently.

Others looked toward the approaching coastline with expressions of apprehension.

Their chains rattled whenever the ship shifted.

One of the guards kicked a container aside.

"Move."

A man stumbled forward.

He barely managed to regain his balance before another chain pulled him backward.

The Amazones laughed.

There was no particular concern for their condition.

To them, these men were possessions.

Nothing more.

Their treatment reflected the uglier side of their culture, where strength could become an excuse for cruelty and pride could become an excuse for domination.

On the upper deck, Kali remained uninterested in the details.

She looked toward the horizon.

"Prepare yourselves."

Her voice wasn't particularly loud.

Yet the surrounding warriors immediately responded.

Weapons were collected.

Equipment was secured.

The training stopped.

One by one, the Amazones gathered.

Some smiled.

Others cracked their knuckles.

A few looked almost impatient.

They wanted action.

They wanted an opportunity to demonstrate their strength.

And they wanted to enjoy themselves.

"Are we going to have a fight?"

One of them asked.

Kali smiled.

"Perhaps."

That single word was enough.

The atmosphere changed.

The warriors began exchanging excited looks.

Melen was approaching.

And whatever awaited them there, they would face it in their own way.

The fleet continued forward.

Its sails moved against the wind.

The waves parted beneath its hull.

And the approaching port city remained unaware of the nature of its visitors.

For now.

**End of Chapter Eighty-Two.**
