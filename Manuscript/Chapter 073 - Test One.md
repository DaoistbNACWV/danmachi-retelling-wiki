# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

## Chapter Seventy-Three — Test One

The first thing Bell did was ask permission.

Not because he lacked the means to proceed without it. Not because he was uncertain whether the experiment could work.

He asked because the person lying on the examination table was not a specimen.

She was Ein.

And whatever he intended to do, whatever discoveries awaited him beneath the layers of corruption that had invaded her body, he refused to make the decision on her behalf without first explaining what he could.

The laboratory was unusually quiet.

Several layers of protective formations surrounded the examination chamber. Magic circles had been drawn across the floor, walls, and ceiling, some overlapping one another in patterns that would have appeared nonsensical to anyone unfamiliar with their construction.

There were no ordinary medical instruments on the central table.

Instead, Bell had arranged a collection of unfamiliar implements: thin metallic rods engraved with ancient characters, several crystalline containers, carefully prepared parchment, and a handful of objects whose purposes were difficult to discern.

Some belonged to modern magical research.

Others were considerably older.

Bell stood beside the table, examining the last of his preparations.

Ein watched him.

She had been quiet for some time.

"Bell."

"Hm?"

"Are you going to explain what you're planning to do?"

He stopped writing.

"Right. I suppose I should."

He placed the writing implement down and turned toward her.

"First, I want to make something clear. I haven't completely understood the nature of the corruption inside your body. I have several theories, but none of them are sufficiently verified."

"Then you don't know if you can remove it?"

"I know I can remove some of it."

"Some?"

Bell nodded.

"The corruption isn't simply attached to your body. It's integrated into it. There are structures running through your magical pathways, and some of them appear to have become deeply embedded in your internal organs."

He paused.

"Calling them parasites wouldn't be entirely accurate. They behave more like an artificial network that has established itself inside a living organism."

Ein lowered her gaze.

"Can you remove it?"

"That's what we're going to find out."

She looked at him again.

Bell approached the examination table and pulled a nearby chair closer.

"I need to explain the risks before we begin."

His voice became more serious.

"Removing the corruption may cause considerable damage to your body. Some of the structures are intertwined with your own biological functions. If I remove them carelessly, I could damage the surrounding tissue, interrupt the flow of mana, or cause complications that I might not be able to reverse."

"Could I die?"

"Yes."

He didn't hesitate.

Ein's expression remained relatively unchanged, but her fingers tightened slightly against the fabric beneath them.

Bell noticed.

"I won't lie to you about that. There are things I can repair, and there are things I can't. I don't intend to discover the difference by gambling with your life."

"Then why proceed?"

"Because leaving it alone isn't exactly a safe alternative."

Bell glanced at the readings from the magical instruments.

"The corruption is consuming mana continuously. Not occasionally. Not whenever you use magic. It's happening even while you're resting."

He pointed toward one of the diagrams.

"Your body is constantly supplying something that shouldn't be there. The network takes what it needs, and your body compensates by producing and circulating more mana."

"Like an endless drain?"

"Exactly."

He folded his arms.

"And if my observations are correct, the problem isn't going to resolve itself."

Ein remained silent.

Bell softened his expression.

"However, I can stop if you want me to. We don't have to do this today."

For a moment, she simply looked at him.

Then she asked something he hadn't expected.

"Would you be able to save me?"

Bell didn't immediately answer.

He considered the question.

Not the comforting answer.

Not the answer she might have wanted.

The honest one.

"I don't know."

Ein looked down.

"But I want to try," he continued. "And I believe I have a reasonable chance of making progress."

"Then..."

She looked back at him.

"Please do it."

Bell watched her for several seconds before nodding.

"All right."

He stood.

"Then let's begin with Test One."

***

The first stage involved establishing a stable baseline.

Bell had already prepared the necessary formations, but he wanted to observe Ein's condition one final time before introducing any external magical interference.

He placed several engraved instruments around the examination table.

A faint glow emerged from their surfaces.

The characters were unfamiliar to the modern magical systems commonly employed in Orario. Their structures were older, their arrangements less dependent on conventional spell construction.

Runes.

Ancient runic inscriptions.

Bell had spent considerable time studying different approaches to magical manipulation, and the ancient systems offered something particularly useful for the current situation.

Modern magic often relied on established structures, formulas, and predetermined effects.

Ancient runic magic, at least in the traditions Bell was employing, allowed him to establish relationships between individual concepts.

A command.

A boundary.

A restriction.

A connection.

A severance.

Rather than simply producing an effect, the runes could define the conditions under which an effect was permitted to occur.

Sorcery offered another layer.

Where the runes established the structure, sorcery allowed Bell to manipulate the underlying magical relationships.

He wasn't simply attempting to overpower the corruption.

He intended to separate it from the living system it had invaded.

A considerably more delicate undertaking.

"Can you feel anything?" he asked.

Ein shook her head.

"Not yet."

"Good. Tell me immediately if anything changes."

He activated the first formation.

A thin circle of light appeared beneath the examination table.

Then another.

And another.

The inscriptions began to illuminate in sequence, forming a complicated arrangement around Ein's body.

Bell watched the readings.

There.

The first reaction.

A small fluctuation in the mana circulating through her body.

Then another.

And another.

His eyes narrowed.

"Interesting."

"What is it?"

"The network is responding."

He leaned closer to the instruments.

It wasn't a particularly aggressive reaction. Rather, the corruption appeared to recognize the presence of an external magical structure.

Its response was immediate.

A portion of the mana circulating through Ein's body changed direction.

Bell watched the movement.

The network was diverting resources toward the affected region.

"You're anticipating interference," he muttered.

He adjusted the formation.

The runes shifted.

One of the metallic rods began vibrating.

"Let's see how you handle this."

A thin strand of light emerged from the formation and extended toward the corruption.

The instant it made contact, the readings changed dramatically.

Bell's expression hardened.

Mana consumption increased.

Not slightly.

Not by a negligible amount.

The corruption began drawing mana from Ein's body at an alarming rate.

The instruments flashed.

"That's considerably worse than I expected."

He immediately reinforced the surrounding formations.

The mana flow stabilized, but the readings continued to climb.

The corruption was responding to the attempt to isolate it by increasing its consumption.

It was almost as if the network had recognized the threat and decided to compensate through sheer resource expenditure.

Bell stared at the readings.

"You're not defending yourself."

His eyes narrowed.

"You're feeding yourself."

He terminated the initial connection.

The light disappeared.

The instruments gradually returned to their previous state.

Ein looked toward him.

"Did something go wrong?"

"Not entirely."

Bell removed one of the metallic rods and examined its surface.

"The corruption is considerably more responsive than I anticipated. It can redirect its mana consumption toward areas experiencing interference."

"Is that bad?"

"It's inconvenient."

He glanced at her.

"Very inconvenient."

He returned to the examination table.

"However, I learned something."

He pointed toward the diagram.

"The network isn't uniformly distributed. It has several major concentrations, and the connections between them are responsible for maintaining its structure."

"Can you remove those?"

"Probably."

He paused.

"That's what I intend to test next."

***

The second attempt was more aggressive.

Bell altered the runic formations, changing their arrangement from observation to controlled separation.

He placed additional inscriptions around the affected regions.

The ancient characters illuminated one after another.

This time, he didn't attempt to establish a connection with the entire network.

He targeted a single section.

A small portion of the corruption extending through one of Ein's limbs.

The formation activated.

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then the corruption became visible.

Blackened magical threads appeared beneath the surface of her skin, their unnatural patterns extending along the surrounding pathways.

They weren't ordinary veins.

They weren't muscles.

They were something else entirely.

Bell watched them carefully.

"Found you."

He raised his hand.

The runes surrounding the affected area changed.

One inscription established a boundary.

Another restricted the flow of mana.

A third began separating the foreign structure from the surrounding tissue.

The process was slow.

Painfully slow.

Bell could feel the resistance through the magical feedback.

The corruption was attempting to maintain its connection.

He adjusted the formation.

"Come on..."

A strand separated.

Then another.

The network resisted, drawing mana from elsewhere in Ein's body.

The instruments immediately registered the increase.

Bell frowned.

"Of course."

He redirected the incoming flow.

A new formation appeared beneath the examination table, catching the excess mana before it could circulate into the affected region.

The process continued.

And finally—

The first section came free.

It didn't disappear.

Instead, the corrupted material gathered into a small, dark mass suspended above the formation.

Bell immediately sealed it inside a prepared crystalline container.

The container's surface darkened.

He watched the readings.

Then he looked toward Ein.

"How are you feeling?"

"Strange."

"How strange?"

"I can feel something moving, but it doesn't hurt."

Bell nodded.

"That's good. Don't move yet."

He examined the extracted material.

The corruption continued to pulse faintly inside the container.

Even separated from Ein, it retained some degree of magical activity.

Bell's expression became thoughtful.

"That's interesting."

"Is it still alive?"

"Not necessarily. But it hasn't become inert."

He turned the container slightly.

The dark material responded to the surrounding mana.

"Whatever this is, it retains some of its original properties even after separation."

He placed the container aside.

One successful extraction.

One confirmation.

The method worked.

At least on the outer sections.

But the real problem remained.

Bell returned to the readings.

The deeper concentrations were considerably more complicated.

Some were connected to structures that appeared essential to Ein's survival.

Removing them would require considerably more than separating foreign magical material.

He needed to preserve the surrounding tissue.

And that brought him to another problem.

A rather important one.

***

"Let's stop here."

Ein looked toward him.

"Already?"

"For now."

Bell began removing the active formations.

"We've confirmed that the runic separation method works. There's no reason to continue increasing the risk when we haven't established a reliable way to protect the surrounding tissue."

He glanced at the instruments.

"The corruption has already begun compensating for the missing section. Continuing without changing our approach would be inefficient."

"Does that mean you can't remove the rest?"

"No."

Bell leaned against the nearby table.

"It means I need a better method."

He examined the extracted material again.

The foreign structure was remarkably persistent.

Even after separation, it retained its ability to consume mana.

That meant simply destroying it wouldn't necessarily be sufficient.

He needed to remove it completely.

And more importantly, he needed to prevent the remaining corruption from reconstructing the portions he had already removed.

Bell stared at the diagrams.

Then his eyes moved toward a different set of notes.

Healing magic.

He had been considering the possibility before beginning the experiment, but hadn't been entirely certain whether it would be applicable.

The problem wasn't simply that Ein's body had been invaded.

The corruption had become intertwined with her own biological structures.

Removing it would inevitably damage something.

Unless...

Bell straightened.

"Wait."

Ein tilted her head.

"What is it?"

"I might have overlooked something."

He hurried toward the nearby shelves and retrieved several documents.

He began comparing different magical principles, moving between his notes and the diagrams he'd prepared.

The first was the runic separation process.

The second concerned the reconstruction of damaged biological structures.

The third involved high-tier healing magic.

Bell stopped.

There.

He read the relevant section again.

Then again.

His expression gradually changed.

"That's it."

"Bell?"

He looked toward her.

"Ein, I think I've found a way to deal with the damage."

***

The discovery wasn't particularly complicated in principle.

Its application, however, was another matter.

Healing magic wasn't limited to closing wounds or restoring damaged tissue.

At sufficiently high levels, certain forms of healing could accomplish considerably more.

They could regenerate structures that the body had lost.

Even something as complicated as an entire limb.

Bell had witnessed the principle behind such magic before.

If a spell could reconstruct an entire missing limb, then theoretically, it could also reconstruct damaged tissue after the removal of foreign material.

He stared at the diagrams.

The implications were significant.

The process didn't need to preserve every individual cell during extraction.

It needed to preserve the overall integrity of the organism.

If he could isolate the corruption, remove it, and immediately restore the affected tissue through sufficiently powerful healing magic, then the damage might become manageable.

He began writing.

"Separate. Remove. Reconstruct."

He repeated the sequence under his breath.

Then he stopped.

There was another problem.

The corruption wasn't merely present in the outer portions of Ein's body.

It extended into her internal organs.

And some of its deepest concentrations were located in particularly sensitive areas.

The heart.

The brain.

Bell's expression gradually darkened.

He examined the diagrams again.

The network extended into both.

Not superficially.

Deeply.

His hands stopped.

The corruption had intertwined with structures essential to maintaining life.

If he attempted to remove those sections using the same method, the consequences could be fatal.

Healing magic might repair the damage afterward.

But only if the body remained capable of sustaining itself long enough for the healing to take effect.

The heart couldn't simply be removed and reconstructed while the rest of the body waited.

Neither could the brain.

At least, not with anything Bell could confidently perform.

He exhaled.

"So that's where the problem is."

Ein watched him.

"Can you heal the damage?"

"Most of it, yes."

"Then..."

"Not everything."

He turned toward her.

"The corruption in your heart and brain is different. Removing it would mean damaging structures that are essential to keeping you alive."

"Would the healing magic not repair them?"

"That's the problem."

He tapped the relevant portions of the diagram.

"I can reconstruct damaged tissue, but I can't guarantee that your body will remain alive during the process. The heart and brain aren't interchangeable components."

Ein remained silent.

Bell continued.

"If I remove the corruption from your arm, I can repair the surrounding tissue afterward. If I remove it from an organ that can survive temporary disruption, I may be able to do the same."

He pointed toward the two deepest concentrations.

"But these are different."

He didn't need to explain further.

Ein understood.

"So you can remove everything except those parts."

"Potentially."

"And they could grow back?"

Bell nodded.

"That's what I'm worried about."

The remaining corruption could serve as a source from which the rest of the network might regenerate.

Even if he successfully removed every other concentration, the portions embedded in the heart and brain could eventually spread again.

He would have accomplished nothing permanent.

Worse, the remaining corruption might adapt to the procedure.

Bell looked at the diagrams.

He had found a way to remove the corruption.

He had also found the limitation that prevented him from removing all of it.

And the latter was considerably more troublesome.

***

There was one more precaution he needed to establish before attempting the full procedure.

Suspended animation.

Bell had considered the possibility earlier, but the discovery of the healing method made it considerably more important.

If he was going to remove large sections of the corruption, he couldn't allow Ein's body to respond normally to the process.

Her nervous system would react to the damage.

Her body would attempt to compensate for the sudden changes.

Her mana circulation would fluctuate.

And the corruption would continue drawing from those fluctuations.

He needed to reduce all of those variables.

More importantly, he needed to ensure that Ein wouldn't experience the procedure.

Bell returned to the central formation.

"There's something else I'd like to try."

Ein looked toward him.

"What?"

"A suspended animation state."

"Like sleeping?"

"Not quite."

He considered his explanation.

"Your body's functions would be suppressed to an extremely low level. Your perception would be suspended, and you wouldn't experience the passage of time."

"Would I feel anything?"

"No."

"Not even pain?"

"That is the intention."

He adjusted one of the formations.

"Your body would remain alive, but its normal activity would be reduced considerably. I'd maintain the necessary functions through the surrounding magical formations."

Ein looked at the inscriptions.

"And while I'm like that?"

"I'll work."

He smiled faintly.

"Rather than having you endure the procedure, I'll put you somewhere you can't feel it."

She studied him for a moment.

"Will I wake up?"

Bell's expression softened.

"That's the plan."

"Then I trust you."

He didn't immediately respond.

There was something about those words that made him feel considerably more responsible for what he was about to do.

Trust wasn't something he could measure with an instrument.

It couldn't be represented through a diagram or calculated through a formula.

But it could be broken.

And Bell had no intention of doing that.

"All right."

He returned to the formation.

"Let's get you comfortable first."

***

The suspended animation formation required a different arrangement of runes.

Bell established several layers.

The outermost formation would maintain the boundaries of the process.

The second would regulate mana circulation.

The third would suppress unnecessary bodily activity.

And the innermost structure would maintain the connection between Ein's body and the magical support sustaining her.

He examined each inscription before activating the next.

There was no room for carelessness.

"Ready?"

Ein nodded.

Bell raised his hand.

The first formation illuminated.

A faint light spread beneath the examination table.

Then the second.

The surrounding air grew noticeably heavier as the magical structures began interacting with one another.

Ein's breathing gradually slowed.

Bell watched the instruments.

Her pulse remained stable.

The mana circulating through her body began decreasing.

Not disappearing.

Simply slowing.

The corruption reacted almost immediately.

The dark structures beneath her skin began shifting, attempting to draw more mana from the surrounding pathways.

Bell's eyes narrowed.

"Not this time."

He activated the restriction formation.

The runes illuminated.

The mana flow stabilized.

The corruption continued attempting to siphon energy, but the formation prevented it from drawing beyond the established limits.

Bell carefully adjusted the parameters.

A few seconds passed.

Then another.

Ein's breathing became almost imperceptible.

Her muscles relaxed.

The tension gradually disappeared from her face.

And finally, her eyes closed.

Bell watched the readings.

"Suspended animation established."

He checked the instruments again.

Her bodily functions remained stable.

The surrounding magical formations continued supplying the necessary support.

He approached the table.

"Ein?"

No response.

Of course.

That was the point.

He examined the readings one final time.

No significant fluctuations.

No unexpected reaction.

No signs of distress.

She couldn't feel anything.

Bell released a breath he hadn't realized he'd been holding.

"Good."

He turned toward the laboratory.

The real work was about to begin.

***

The procedure was considerably more complicated once he began working on the larger concentrations.

With Ein suspended, Bell could concentrate entirely on the magical structures without worrying about her experiencing the process.

He activated the runic formations.

The ancient characters illuminated across the chamber.

One by one, the corrupted structures became visible.

They appeared like an unnatural network woven through her body.

Some sections were thin.

Others were considerably thicker, extending into deeper regions.

Bell began with the outermost concentrations.

The first separation was successful.

The second followed.

Then the third.

Each extracted section was immediately contained within a prepared vessel.

The containers gradually accumulated dark material.

Bell watched the readings.

Mana consumption remained high.

Even with the suspended animation formation restricting the network, the corruption continued attempting to draw energy.

The surrounding magical structures compensated for the difference.

Bell adjusted the formations as needed.

He wasn't going to allow the process to destabilize.

Hours seemed to pass.

The work was meticulous.

He separated each section, removed the foreign structure, and immediately applied high-tier healing magic to restore the damaged tissue.

The results were encouraging.

The affected areas began reconstructing themselves.

Damaged structures regenerated.

The surrounding tissue recovered.

And the corruption failed to establish itself again in those particular regions.

Bell examined the results.

Then he moved to the next section.

Again.

And again.

The process continued.

The corruption was removable.

That much was no longer in question.

The healing magic was also functioning as intended.

Wherever he successfully extracted the foreign structures, he could restore the damage left behind.

For the first time since beginning the experiment, Bell felt that he was making meaningful progress.

But then he reached the deeper concentrations.

His hands stopped.

The readings were different.

He carefully examined the diagrams.

The corruption extended into the heart.

And the brain.

Bell leaned closer.

The surrounding magical structures were considerably more complicated.

The foreign material had intertwined with essential biological systems.

He attempted to establish a boundary around one of the affected sections.

The formation activated.

Immediately, the readings changed.

The heart's magical circulation fluctuated.

Bell terminated the attempt.

He tried a different angle.

The same result.

The corruption wasn't simply surrounding the organ.

It had become integrated with the structures supporting its function.

He turned toward the brain.

The situation was even more delicate.

The network extended through multiple regions, and separating it would require interfering with structures that Bell couldn't safely disrupt.

He slowly lowered his hand.

"That's enough."

The formations dimmed.

He stepped away from the examination table.

For several moments, he simply stood there.

The procedure had succeeded.

He had removed a substantial portion of the corruption.

He had demonstrated that high-tier healing magic could repair the damage left behind.

And he had discovered that the suspended animation formation could prevent Ein from experiencing the procedure.

But the most important problem remained.

The heart.

The brain.

He couldn't remove the corruption from either without risking her life.

And even if he successfully eliminated every other section, the remaining corruption could eventually spread again.

Bell looked at the containers.

The extracted material continued moving faintly inside them.

He picked up one of the vessels and examined its contents.

It was an unpleasant reminder.

He could remove the corruption.

He could isolate it.

He could repair the damage it caused.

But he couldn't yet eradicate it completely.

Not without finding a way to deal with the two remaining concentrations.

He placed the container down.

"Well..."

His eyes returned to the examination table.

"That's a problem."

***

Bell spent several more minutes confirming Ein's condition.

The suspended animation formation remained stable.

Her bodily functions were being maintained, and the healing process had restored the sections he had treated.

He examined the readings one final time.

No unexpected deterioration.

No significant abnormalities beyond the remaining corruption.

He adjusted the magical support formations and ensured that the mana supply would remain consistent.

Then he looked at Ein.

She appeared peaceful.

There was no visible sign of the procedure she had just undergone.

No pain.

No discomfort.

Nothing.

Bell pulled a chair beside the examination table.

He sat down.

For a while, he didn't do anything.

He simply watched the readings.

He had entered the laboratory expecting to learn whether the corruption could be removed.

Now he had his answer.

Yes.

It could.

But the answer had come with another question.

How could he remove something that had become part of the very structures keeping its host alive?

Destroying it wasn't enough.

Removing it wasn't enough.

Even if he managed to eliminate every accessible section, the remaining corruption could grow back.

He needed something different.

A method that could distinguish between the corruption and the living structures surrounding it without disrupting either.

Or perhaps a way to neutralize the corruption without physically removing it.

He didn't know yet.

And he wasn't going to pretend otherwise.

Bell leaned back in his chair.

"Test One."

He glanced at the containers.

"Successful, I suppose."

Then he looked toward Ein again.

"With a rather inconvenient result."

He allowed himself a small, tired smile.

"Don't worry. I'll figure something out."

The laboratory remained quiet.

The ancient runes continued glowing faintly across the chamber, maintaining the suspended animation.

Ein couldn't hear him.

She couldn't feel anything.

And for the moment, that was precisely how Bell wanted it.

He had bought himself time.

Now he needed to make that time count.

Because the next experiment would have to solve a problem that brute force, careful extraction, and even the highest healing magic he could employ had failed to overcome.

He needed to find a way to eradicate the corruption without killing the person it had infested.

And until he did, the experiment remained unfinished.

*End of Chapter Seventy-Three.*
