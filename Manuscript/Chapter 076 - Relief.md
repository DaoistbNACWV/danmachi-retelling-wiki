# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

# Chapter Seventy-Six — Relief

> **Status: DRAFT — saved at the author's request; not yet marked finalized.**

### I. A Quiet Night

The laboratory had finally become quiet.

Not the artificial silence that had once surrounded the examination chamber, nor the uncomfortable stillness that accompanied the long hours Bell had spent studying Ein's condition.

This was different.

The instruments had been switched off. The inscriptions that had covered the floor were no longer illuminated, and the crystalline containers had been carefully secured. There were still documents scattered across the workbench, but the most important procedures had already been completed.

There was no longer any reason to keep the laboratory operational throughout the night.

And, more importantly, there was someone sleeping beside him.

Ein had fallen asleep while holding Bell's hand.

It hadn't been intentional.

They had been talking for some time after the purification. Their conversation had wandered from one subject to another, occasionally interrupted by laughter or the comfortable silences that neither seemed particularly interested in breaking.

Eventually, Ein's responses had become shorter.

Her eyelids had grown heavy.

And before Bell realized it, she had fallen asleep.

Her fingers remained loosely wrapped around his.

Bell looked down at her.

"...You really fell asleep."

There was no response.

He smiled.

The expression was different from the one he wore when examining an interesting experiment or discovering a promising solution to a difficult problem.

It was softer.

He carefully shifted his hand, making certain not to disturb her.

Ein stirred slightly.

Her fingers tightened around his.

Bell stopped.

"...All right."

He waited.

A few seconds passed before her breathing returned to its previous rhythm.

Only then did he move.

He slipped one arm beneath her knees and the other behind her back, lifting her carefully from the chair.

She was lighter than he expected.

Or perhaps he had simply become accustomed to carrying considerably heavier equipment.

Either way, he adjusted his hold.

Ein's head settled against his chest.

Her hair brushed against his shoulder.

Bell glanced at her sleeping face.

There was something strangely satisfying about seeing her like this.

Not because she was unconscious.

Quite the opposite.

She was finally sleeping without the corruption interfering with her body. There was no formation maintaining her condition, no artificial restraint preventing her from moving, and no uncertain process threatening to undo everything he had accomplished.

She was simply resting.

A normal person enjoying a peaceful night's sleep.

That was something worth appreciating.

Bell carried her toward the bed he had prepared earlier.

He lowered her gently onto the mattress, adjusted the pillow beneath her head, and pulled the blanket over her.

Ein shifted.

Her hand reached toward the space where his had been.

Bell caught it before she could wake.

"...I'm here."

Her expression relaxed.

He remained beside her for a moment, holding her hand until she settled again.

Then he leaned forward and gently kissed her forehead.

"Good night, Ein."

He waited a little longer.

Eventually, he released her hand.

There was still work to do.

The laboratory wasn't going to clean itself.

Bell returned to the examination area and began dismantling the remaining apparatus.

He approached the task methodically.

The instruments used during the purification were separated according to their functions. Those that required cleaning were placed on the designated workbench, while the equipment that could be stored immediately was returned to its containers.

He carefully inspected the crystalline vessels.

The corrupted material had already lost its structure, but he wasn't about to become careless simply because the procedure had succeeded.

Some of the remaining equipment contained valuable measurements.

Those records needed to be preserved.

He organized the documents, labeled the samples, and secured the research notes concerning the Dungeon's corruption.

The examination chamber received particular attention.

He checked its interior, confirmed that no residual magical formations remained active, and finally deactivated the surrounding mechanisms.

It was a long process.

But Bell didn't mind.

Every instrument he put away represented another step toward returning the laboratory to normal.

And when he finally finished, he stood in the middle of the room and examined his work.

Everything was accounted for.

Everything was secure.

He glanced toward the bedroom.

A faint smile appeared.

For once, he didn't feel particularly inclined to return to his research.

There was someone waiting for him.

Well, technically, she was asleep.

Still.

That was good enough.

Bell extinguished the remaining lights and left the laboratory.

The night passed peacefully.

### II. Breakfast for Two

Morning arrived quietly.

Sunlight entered through the windows, spreading across the room and gradually illuminating the furniture.

The first person awake was Bell.

He had already washed, changed his clothes, and begun preparing breakfast.

The kitchen was considerably more active than it usually was.

A pot rested over the stove.

Several ingredients had been arranged across the counter, and a wooden cutting board sat beside a collection of utensils.

Bell moved between them with practiced familiarity.

Cooking was one of those skills he had never considered particularly difficult to develop.

It required observation, patience, experimentation, and an understanding of how different ingredients behaved.

In that regard, it wasn't especially different from his other interests.

Of course, cooking had one considerable advantage.

The results could be enjoyed immediately.

Bell cracked several eggs into a bowl.

He added a little seasoning, whisked the mixture until its consistency became uniform, and prepared the pan.

The butter melted.

The familiar aroma gradually filled the kitchen.

He poured the eggs into the pan and adjusted the heat.

A few careful movements later, the omelette began taking shape.

Meanwhile, another pot contained freshly prepared rice.

The grains had been cultivated by Bell himself.

It wasn't something he had acquired from a nearby merchant.

He had taken an interest in cultivating rice some time ago, experimenting with suitable growing conditions and improving the process until he could produce a consistent harvest.

It had required considerably more work than simply purchasing it.

But the result was worth it.

There was something satisfying about eating something one had grown personally.

Bell checked the rice.

Perfect.

He transferred a portion onto two plates.

The omelettes followed.

He prepared the accompanying dishes, arranged everything neatly, and inspected the final presentation.

A hearty breakfast.

Nothing extravagant.

Just a warm, satisfying meal prepared with care.

Bell stepped back.

"...Not bad."

He glanced at the clock.

Ein would probably be awake soon.

He covered the plates to preserve their warmth and made his way toward the bedroom.

The room was still quiet.

Ein remained beneath the blanket, her hair slightly disheveled.

She looked considerably more peaceful than she had during the previous days.

Bell approached the bed.

"Ein."

No response.

He smiled.

"Good morning."

Her eyelids moved slightly.

He sat beside her and brushed a few strands of hair away from her face.

Then he leaned forward and kissed her forehead.

"Wake up, sleepyhead."

Ein slowly opened her eyes.

For several seconds, she simply looked at him.

"...Bell?"

"Morning."

She blinked.

Then smiled.

"Good morning."

"Did you sleep well?"

She nodded.

"Very well."

"That's good."

He gently brushed his thumb against her cheek.

"How are you feeling?"

Ein considered the question.

Then she stretched her arms slightly.

"Rather good, actually."

"Any dizziness?"

"No."

"Discomfort?"

"None."

"Then I suppose I can stop worrying for approximately five minutes."

Ein laughed.

"Only five?"

"I'm being generous."

She reached for his hand.

Bell accepted it naturally.

For a moment, they simply looked at each other.

Then Ein shifted closer.

"You're already awake."

"I've been awake for a while."

"And you didn't wake me?"

"You looked comfortable."

She lowered her eyes.

"...Thank you."

Bell smiled.

"Come on. I've prepared breakfast."

Ein looked up.

"Breakfast?"

"Yes."

"For us?"

"Unless you were expecting someone else."

She giggled.

"No."

Bell stood and offered his hand.

Ein accepted it.

He helped her rise from the bed, although she was perfectly capable of standing on her own.

They left the bedroom together.

Bell guided her toward the kitchen, keeping his hand loosely around hers.

The moment they entered, Ein noticed the table.

Two plates.

Two cups.

Freshly prepared food.

And a rather pleasant aroma.

Her expression brightened.

"...You made all of this?"

"Of course."

She approached the table.

There was rice, a beautifully prepared omelette, and several accompanying dishes.

Everything was arranged neatly.

Bell pulled out a chair for her.

"Please."

Ein looked at him.

Then at the chair.

"...You're treating me rather well."

"I should hope so."

She smiled and sat down.

Bell took the opposite seat.

"Well, let's eat before everything gets cold."

### III. Something Warm

Ein picked up her utensils.

She looked at the food for a moment before taking her first bite.

The omelette was soft.

The seasoning was balanced, and the texture was almost impossibly delicate.

The rice was warm and fragrant.

She took another bite.

Then another.

Bell watched her with a faint smile.

"How is it?"

Ein paused.

"...Delicious."

"Good."

"Really delicious."

"That's reassuring."

She looked at him.

"Did you make this yourself?"

"Yes."

"All of it?"

"Everything."

Ein glanced at the rice.

"Even this?"

Bell followed her gaze.

"Ah. The rice?"

She nodded.

"I cultivated it."

Her eyes widened slightly.

"You grew it?"

"More or less. It took some time to get the conditions right, but the results have been satisfactory."

Ein looked down at her plate.

Then back at him.

"...You really do everything, don't you?"

"Not everything."

He considered it.

"Although I suppose I could learn."

Ein laughed.

Bell began eating his own breakfast.

He wasn't particularly interested in making a spectacle of his abilities.

Cooking was simply something he enjoyed.

He had experimented with different recipes, ingredients, and preparation methods over the years. Given sufficient ingredients and a little time, he was confident he could prepare almost anything.

But he wasn't about to start listing his culinary accomplishments during breakfast.

There were more important things to appreciate.

For instance, the person sitting across from him.

Ein continued eating.

Slowly at first.

Then with increasing enthusiasm.

She had eaten before.

Of course she had.

But there had always been something missing.

Food had once been a necessity, something that filled an empty stomach without necessarily providing satisfaction.

There had been little pleasure in it.

Sometimes, she had barely noticed what she was eating.

Now, everything seemed different.

The warmth of the meal.

The taste of the ingredients.

The simple sensation of being able to sit comfortably and enjoy something without worrying about what might happen afterward.

It was almost overwhelming.

She took another bite.

Then another.

And before long, she realized she had eaten considerably more than she usually would.

Bell noticed.

"Still hungry?"

Ein looked at her plate.

"...A little."

"Then eat."

He pushed the remaining portion of rice slightly closer to her.

"There's plenty."

She looked at him.

"You're not going to complain?"

"About what?"

"That I'm eating too much."

Bell raised an eyebrow.

"Why would I?"

Ein hesitated.

Then smiled.

"...I don't know."

"Well, I certainly won't."

He reached for the serving dish.

"Besides, I prepared enough for two people."

She watched him place another portion onto her plate.

"...Thank you."

"You're welcome."

They continued eating.

The conversation wandered between ordinary subjects.

Bell asked whether she preferred stronger seasoning.

Ein admitted that she hadn't given it much thought before.

They discussed the rice, the ingredients, and the possibility of preparing something different for their next meal.

At one point, Ein asked whether Bell had always been this good at cooking.

He answered with a modest shrug.

"I've had plenty of practice."

"How much practice?"

"Enough."

"That's not an answer."

"I learned by making mistakes."

Ein smiled.

"Did you make many?"

"Enough to develop an appreciation for not wasting ingredients."

She laughed.

It was an ordinary conversation.

And that was precisely what made it so valuable.

There were no difficult questions.

No medical examinations.

No experiments.

No desperate attempts to keep someone alive.

Just breakfast.

When they finally finished, Ein leaned back slightly.

She placed a hand against her stomach.

"...I'm full."

Bell looked at her.

"Completely?"

She nodded.

"Very."

"Good."

He began collecting the empty plates.

But before he could stand, Ein reached toward him.

"Bell?"

"Hm?"

She moved closer and rested her head against his shoulder.

Then she spoke quietly.

"Thank you."

Bell looked down at her.

"For breakfast?"

"For everything."

Her fingers lightly held the fabric of his sleeve.

"For bringing me back. For staying with me. For making me feel like I can finally live normally."

Bell remained silent for a moment.

Then he placed a hand over hers.

"You don't have to thank me for wanting you here."

Ein lifted her head slightly.

He smiled.

"I love you, Ein."

Her expression softened.

"And I want you to enjoy the things you can finally do. Eat until you're satisfied. Sleep when you're tired. Complain when something bothers you."

He gently brushed her hair aside.

"And if you want another breakfast tomorrow, I'll make one."

Ein's eyes curved into a smile.

"...You're spoiling me."

"Is that a problem?"

"No."

She leaned against him again.

"Not at all."

Bell chuckled.

He kissed the top of her head.

For a while, neither spoke.

Eventually, Ein straightened.

"We should probably get ready."

Bell nodded.

"You're right."

There was something they needed to take care of.

Something that would determine where they stood in the eyes of the city.

And, more importantly, where Ein would belong.

The registration.

### IV. A Name in the Guild's Records

The streets of Orario were already busy when Bell, Ein, and Hestia arrived at the Guild.

Merchants were opening their shops.

Adventurers moved through the streets in groups, discussing their next expeditions, while the occasional carriage passed through the wider roads.

Ein remained close to Bell.

She wore appropriate clothing for going outside, and her appearance was carefully maintained.

There was one particular distinction they had agreed upon.

In private, she was Ein.

But in public, she would be known as Filvis.

It was a name that already existed in the city's records.

And until they decided otherwise, maintaining that identity would make their circumstances considerably easier to explain.

Bell glanced at her.

"Everything all right?"

Ein nodded.

"Yes."

"You're quiet."

"I'm thinking."

"About the registration?"

She looked at him.

"About what happens afterward."

Bell smiled.

"Well, afterward, we'll probably have to return home."

She laughed.

"I meant something else."

"I know."

He gently took her hand.

"Whatever happens, we'll deal with it together."

Ein looked at their joined hands.

Then nodded.

They entered the Guild.

The building was already occupied by numerous adventurers and employees.

The familiar atmosphere of the establishment surrounded them.

Bell approached the reception area, where a red-haired werewolf was attending to her duties.

She looked up, and her expression changed slightly.

"...Bell Cranel?"

"Good morning."

The woman glanced toward the person standing beside him.

For a moment, her eyes remained fixed on the girl's face.

Then she recognized her.

"Filvis?"

Ein returned her gaze.

"Good morning, Miss Fannett."

Rose Fannett stared at her.

There was something almost disbelieving in her expression.

"...You're alive."

Ein gave a small smile.

"Yes."

Rose leaned forward slightly.

"I was under the impression that you were no longer active."

"That is a rather complicated matter."

"I can imagine."

Rose glanced between the two.

Then her eyes settled on Bell.

"May I ask what brings you here?"

Bell answered.

"We're here to discuss her registration into Hestia Familia."

Rose blinked.

Then looked at Ein again.

"...Hestia Familia?"

"Yes."

The response was immediate.

Rose leaned back slightly.

She was familiar with Filvis's previous circumstances.

She had served as her Guild advisor, after all.

Although the details surrounding her condition had always been unusual, her previous affiliation was a matter of record.

And that created a problem.

"Before we proceed, I'll need to examine the existing registration."

Bell nodded.

"Of course."

Rose retrieved several documents and began reviewing the relevant records.

Her expression gradually became more serious.

"According to the Guild's records, Filvis Challia was registered under Dionysus Familia."

Ein remained quiet.

Rose continued.

"Ordinarily, changing Familia requires the consent of both the previous and receiving deities."

Bell nodded.

"That's what we expected."

Rose looked toward him.

"Then you understand the difficulty."

"Yes."

He glanced at Ein.

"But we'd still like to proceed."

Rose studied them for a moment.

Then she looked down at the documents.

"...I'll have to request Dionysus's attendance."

Ein's fingers tightened slightly.

Bell noticed.

He didn't say anything immediately.

Instead, he gently squeezed her hand.

"All right."

Rose looked at the two.

"I'll arrange it."

She stood.

"Please wait in one of the private consultation rooms."

Bell nodded.

"Thank you."

Rose gave Ein one final glance before leading them toward the consultation area.

The room was relatively small.

There was a table, several chairs, and enough privacy to discuss administrative matters without attracting unnecessary attention.

Hestia had joined them before the appointment, and she now sat beside Bell.

Ein occupied the seat next to him.

For a while, nobody spoke.

Eventually, Hestia broke the silence.

"So..."

She looked toward Ein.

"How are you feeling?"

Ein smiled.

"I'm fine, Lady Hestia."

"That's good."

Hestia rested her hands on the table.

"Well, I suppose we should get this settled."

Bell nodded.

"Agreed."

He glanced toward the door.

The next part would be considerably more complicated.

### V. An Unwelcome Guest

Nearly an hour passed before Dionysus arrived.

He entered the consultation room looking precisely as one would expect a respectable god to look.

His clothes were immaculate.

His expression was pleasant.

And his demeanor carried the carefully cultivated composure of someone accustomed to presenting himself as a benevolent deity.

He offered a courteous smile.

"My apologies for the wait."

Hestia's expression remained unchanged.

Bell merely acknowledged him with a slight nod.

Dionysus took the available seat.

His eyes moved toward Ein.

"...Filvis."

Ein returned his gaze.

"Dionysus."

There was no affection in the exchange.

Nor was there any particular hostility.

They simply acknowledged each other.

Dionysus folded his hands.

"I understand that there is a matter concerning your registration."

"Yes," Bell answered.

"We would like to register her under Hestia Familia."

Dionysus looked toward Hestia.

"Is that so?"

Hestia crossed her arms.

"That's right."

He smiled faintly.

"Well, I suppose I should hear the circumstances."

Bell didn't appear particularly interested in discussing the past.

Neither did Ein.

Their objective was simple.

They wanted to complete the registration.

Nothing more.

Dionysus seemed to understand that.

He had already anticipated this possibility.

Perhaps not the exact circumstances, but certainly the possibility that Ein would eventually seek a different affiliation.

He glanced toward Rose.

"I presume the Guild has already explained the necessary procedure?"

Rose nodded.

"Yes. According to the existing records, Filvis Challia was previously registered under your Familia. Therefore, we requested your attendance to confirm the transfer."

"Naturally."

Dionysus turned toward Ein.

"However, I would like to understand your intentions."

Ein met his eyes.

"My intentions are straightforward."

She placed her hands on the table.

"I wish to join Hestia Familia."

Dionysus remained silent.

"Is that your final decision?"

"Yes."

There was no hesitation.

Bell looked at her.

He didn't interrupt.

This was something Ein needed to say herself.

Dionysus smiled.

"I see."

He leaned back.

"Then perhaps we should discuss—"

A knock interrupted him.

Before anyone could respond, the door opened.

A Guild employee appeared at the entrance.

"Excuse me. Guild Head Royman Mardeel has arrived."

Rose immediately straightened.

"...Understood."

The employee stepped aside.

A moment later, Royman entered.

His expression was considerably less pleasant than the one Dionysus had worn.

He carried a document folder beneath one arm.

His eyes swept across the room.

"Good."

He approached the table.

"Everyone is here."

Dionysus looked toward him.

"Royman. I wasn't aware you intended to participate."

Royman placed the folder on the table.

"I was informed that an administrative matter had developed into an unnecessary dispute."

His gaze moved toward Ein.

Then toward Rose.

"Miss Fannett."

Rose stood.

"Guild Head."

"Please provide the relevant documents."

She immediately handed him the records.

Royman examined them.

Several seconds passed.

Then he turned toward Dionysus.

"I believe there has been a misunderstanding."

Dionysus raised an eyebrow.

"Has there?"

"Indeed."

Royman opened the folder.

"Before discussing the approval of a Conversion, we should establish whether a Conversion is even applicable."

The room became quiet.

Bell's expression changed slightly.

Dionysus looked at him.

Royman continued.

"Miss Challia, according to the Guild's records, you were previously registered under Dionysus Familia."

He paused.

"However, the current examination results indicate that you possess no Falna."

Dionysus's smile weakened.

Royman placed a separate document on the table.

"An official examination was conducted before this meeting. Your back was inspected, and no Falna engraving was found."

Ein remained silent.

Royman continued.

"That means there is no active divine blessing to transfer."

Dionysus leaned forward.

"That is an interesting interpretation."

Royman adjusted his position.

"It isn't an interpretation. It is the result of the examination."

He looked directly at the god.

"Conversion concerns the transfer of an existing Falna from one deity to another. If no Falna is present, there is nothing to convert."

Dionysus's expression remained composed.

"Nevertheless, her previous affiliation—"

"Her previous affiliation is recorded."

Royman interrupted him.

"But an old record cannot substitute for a blessing that is no longer present."

A brief silence followed.

Bell glanced toward Ein.

She was listening carefully.

Royman turned toward Hestia.

"Lady Hestia, do you still wish to accept her into your Familia?"

Hestia didn't hesitate.

"Of course!"

She leaned forward.

"I wouldn't have come all this way if I didn't."

Royman nodded.

"Then the matter is straightforward."

Dionysus's expression became somewhat colder.

"You're proceeding without my approval?"

Royman looked at him.

"Why would your approval be required?"

The question was delivered without any particular emotion.

"She is not currently your dependent. She has no Falna engraved upon her back, and she is not undergoing a Conversion."

He closed the folder.

"Unless you intend to argue that the Guild should force an unaffiliated individual to remain under the authority of a deity simply because of an old registration, I see no grounds for delaying the process."

Dionysus said nothing.

Royman continued.

"Besides, the Guild has no intention of dictating the personal relationships of adventurers."

His gaze briefly shifted toward Bell and Ein.

"The union of two people who have chosen to stand beside one another is not something a former patron deity can prohibit merely because it is inconvenient."

Hestia smiled faintly.

Bell remained composed.

Ein, however, looked slightly surprised.

Royman returned his attention to the documents.

"The relevant distinction has been established. This will be recorded as an initial Familia registration, not a Conversion."

He looked toward Rose.

"Please prepare the necessary paperwork."

Rose nodded.

"Understood."

Royman then addressed Hestia.

"Lady Hestia, if you are prepared to accept her, the Guild will recognize the registration."

Hestia's expression brightened.

"Absolutely!"

Dionysus remained seated.

For a moment, he seemed to consider whether there was anything further worth saying.

Then he slowly stood.

"...I see."

His eyes moved toward Ein.

She returned his gaze without looking away.

"Very well."

There was no farewell.

No apology.

No attempt to reclaim something that was no longer his.

Dionysus simply turned and left the room.

The door closed behind him.

And the consultation room became quiet.

### VI. A Place to Belong

Rose began preparing the paperwork.

The process was considerably simpler now that the distinction had been established.

There was no existing Falna to convert.

No divine authorization was required from a former patron.

And Hestia had already expressed her willingness to accept Ein.

Several documents needed to be completed, including the confirmation of her identity and the new Familia registration.

Bell remained beside her throughout the process.

Hestia occasionally leaned forward to examine the paperwork, while Royman reviewed the final details.

Eventually, Rose placed the completed documents on the table.

"That should be everything."

She looked toward Ein.

"Congratulations."

Ein blinked.

"...Thank you."

Rose smiled faintly.

"It's been quite some time since I last handled your affairs."

Ein lowered her eyes.

"Yes."

"I'm glad things have worked out."

There was genuine warmth in her voice.

Then Rose glanced toward Bell.

"Please take good care of her."

Bell smiled.

"I intend to."

Ein looked at him.

Her cheeks acquired a faint color.

Hestia noticed.

And, naturally, she couldn't resist.

"Well, well. Someone's getting embarrassed."

"Lady Hestia..."

The goddess laughed.

Bell merely smiled.

Royman closed the folder.

"Then the matter is settled."

He stood.

"Filvis Challia will be recognized as a member of Hestia Familia following the completion of the registration."

He looked toward Hestia.

"Please ensure that the Guild receives any necessary updates regarding her status."

"Of course."

Royman nodded.

Then he turned toward the door.

"I have other matters to attend to."

And with that, the Guild Head departed.

The remaining three stayed in the room for a little longer.

Ein looked down at the document in front of her.

Her name.

Her new affiliation.

A place where she could finally belong without having to answer to someone who no longer had any authority over her.

She slowly touched the paper.

"...It's finished."

Bell looked at her.

"Yes."

Hestia smiled.

"Welcome to the Familia."

Ein raised her eyes.

For a moment, she seemed unable to find the appropriate words.

Then she smiled.

"Thank you, Lady Hestia."

Hestia stood and approached her.

"There's no need to be so formal."

She gently placed a hand on Ein's shoulder.

"You're family now."

Ein's expression softened.

Family.

The word felt different when someone said it without demanding anything in return.

She looked toward Bell.

He was already smiling.

And somehow, that made everything feel more real.

Ein reached for his hand.

Bell accepted it.

Their fingers intertwined.

Hestia watched them for a moment.

Then she sighed dramatically.

"Honestly. You two are already acting like an old married couple."

Bell chuckled.

"Is that a problem?"

"Not particularly."

Hestia crossed her arms.

"But don't forget that I'm your goddess."

"We wouldn't dare."

"Good!"

Ein laughed.

The three of them eventually left the consultation room.

The Guild continued operating around them.

Adventurers came and went.

Employees handled their responsibilities.

And the city remained as busy as ever.

Nothing about the outside world had changed.

Yet, for Ein, something fundamental had.

She was no longer waiting for someone to decide what would happen to her.

She had made her own decision.

And it had been accepted.

Bell glanced toward her.

"Shall we go home?"

Ein looked at him.

Then at Hestia.

She smiled.

"Yes."

Hestia raised an eyebrow.

"Home?"

Bell nodded.

"Home."

Ein gently squeezed his hand.

And together, the three of them walked out of the Guild.

For the first time in a long while, there was nothing waiting for Ein at the end of the road that she needed to fear.

There was simply a place to return to.

A meal to share.

Someone to hold her hand.

And a life that was finally hers to live.

Perhaps that was what relief truly meant.

Not the absence of suffering.

Not the certainty that nothing unpleasant would ever happen again.

But the knowledge that, whatever came next, she would no longer have to face it alone.

And that was enough.
