# Chapter Eighty-Nine: Gojo Toki

There were very few things in the world that could surprise a person who had lived more than one lifetime.

Death, for instance, was no longer particularly frightening.

Neither was being born again.

And yet, there was something profoundly strange about discovering that one's second life had begun in a world where monsters were born from humanity's negative emotions.

Gojo Toki was four years old.

He was also Bell Cranel.

At least, that was the name by which he had once been known.

His memories were not entirely complete. Some were fragmented, others distant, and a few seemed to belong to a different person altogether. Nevertheless, he remembered enough. He remembered his previous life, the people he had met, the places he had visited, and the things he had accomplished.

He remembered being Bell Cranel.

More importantly, he remembered being Chronos.

The distinction mattered.

His present circumstances, however, were considerably less divine.

He was now the son of Gojo Gin and Gojo Haku, a member of the prestigious Gojo clan. More specifically, he had initially been born into one of its branch families.

His name was Gojo Toki.

A rather peculiar name, considering the circumstances surrounding its creation.

His father, Gin, had apparently spent nearly an entire month deciding what to name his newborn son.

An entire month.

One might have expected the head of a family, or at least a responsible father, to have prepared a name beforehand. Unfortunately, Gin seemed to have approached the matter with the same seriousness one would give to choosing what to eat for breakfast.

Eventually, after exhausting what must have been an impressive collection of possibilities, he settled on Toki.

Time.

Quite literally.

He had all the time in the world to name his son, and so he named him after time itself.

Toki found the reasoning amusing.

He would have preferred something more meaningful, perhaps, but considering that his father had apparently spent weeks accomplishing absolutely nothing, he supposed the result was acceptable.

Besides, there were more important matters to concern himself with.

***

The Gojo clan possessed an extensive collection of books.

Its library was one of the more important repositories of knowledge within the household, containing records of cursed techniques, historical accounts, family genealogies, and various documents concerning the supernatural world.

For an ordinary four-year-old, the library would have been an overwhelming place.

For Toki, it was practically an invitation.

He sat on a cushioned chair, his small feet barely reaching the edge of the seat. An open book rested on his lap, its pages filled with unfamiliar terminology that he had gradually begun to understand.

*Cursed Energy.*

That was the name given to the energy that existed within human beings.

Unlike the magical energy he had once known, cursed energy originated from negative emotions. Fear, hatred, grief, resentment, and countless other unpleasant feelings produced it.

Humanity, in its remarkable ability to suffer, generated an enormous quantity of it.

Most ordinary people released cursed energy unconsciously. When accumulated in sufficient quantities, that energy could give rise to cursed spirits.

These beings were supernatural entities born from humanity's collective fears and negative emotions. Their existence was one of the principal reasons jujutsu sorcerers were needed.

Their occupation was straightforward, if hardly pleasant.

They exorcised cursed spirits.

Toki turned another page.

The information was surprisingly comprehensive. There were classifications for cursed spirits, explanations concerning cursed energy manipulation, and even accounts of historical sorcerers who had distinguished themselves through extraordinary abilities.

It was an entirely different supernatural ecosystem.

And yet, the fundamental concept was not entirely unfamiliar.

The world he had left behind had its own collection of supernatural phenomena, divine blessings, monsters, and mysterious energies. This world simply operated according to different rules.

He continued reading.

Among the most interesting subjects was the concept of an *Innate Cursed Technique*.

Unlike cursed energy, which could theoretically be learned and manipulated through training, an innate technique was an ability engraved into an individual's very being.

It was an inherent supernatural characteristic.

Most sorcerers possessed one, although the nature and strength of their techniques varied considerably.

According to the records, an individual's innate technique generally manifested around the age of five.

Toki glanced at his small hands.

Five years old.

He was four.

There was, therefore, approximately another year before the clan could formally determine what kind of technique he possessed.

Not that he particularly needed to wait.

He already understood that something within him was different.

***

There was another subject that deserved considerably more attention.

The Gojo clan.

Its history was extensive, its influence considerable, and its hereditary techniques among the most prestigious in jujutsu society.

Two characteristics were particularly important.

The first was the Limitless, an inherited cursed technique that allowed its user to manipulate space through an extraordinarily sophisticated application of cursed energy.

The second was the Six Eyes.

An exceptionally rare ocular ability that granted its possessor an extraordinary perception of cursed energy and its manipulation.

Together, the two abilities produced a combination that had made the Gojo clan particularly formidable throughout history.

However, the most interesting information was not contained in the historical records.

It was found in the section concerning the clan's current generation.

**Gojo Satoru.**

Sixteen years old.

The current bearer of both the Limitless and the Six Eyes.

He was, by all accounts, the strongest individual within the Gojo clan.

Toki stared at the name for a moment.

His uncle.

That was another peculiar aspect of his present life.

Apparently, everyone in the Gojo clan was related to everyone else in one way or another. The family possessed an intricate web of relationships, with branches connecting to other branches and members sharing varying degrees of familial ties.

It was almost comical.

He had apparently been reincarnated into a family where even introducing himself to another relative might require consulting a genealogy chart.

Still, the existence of Gojo Satoru was interesting.

A sixteen-year-old who possessed both the Limitless and the Six Eyes was undoubtedly an extraordinary individual.

Toki wondered what kind of person he was.

He had yet to meet his uncle properly, although he had heard enough about him from the adults to understand that the young man was anything but ordinary.

Perhaps they would get along.

Perhaps not.

He turned another page.

***

There was, however, one particular reason why Toki had become something of a sensation within the Gojo household.

His cursed energy manipulation.

More specifically, his ability to use Reverse Cursed Technique.

It was an advanced application of cursed energy that reversed its normally negative properties by multiplying negative energy against itself, producing positive energy.

The resulting energy could be used to restore damaged tissue, heal wounds, and repair injuries.

It was an exceptionally difficult technique.

Even among experienced jujutsu sorcerers, the ability to perform Reverse Cursed Technique was relatively uncommon.

And Toki could already use it.

At four years old.

His initial demonstrations had caused considerable commotion.

The adults had first assumed that he was merely displaying an unusually refined understanding of cursed energy. However, when they discovered that he could produce positive energy and use it to heal injuries, their assessment changed dramatically.

A child who could manipulate cursed energy at such a young age was already exceptional.

A child who could perform Reverse Cursed Technique was something else entirely.

Naturally, they called him a genius.

Toki did not particularly disagree.

He simply refrained from explaining that his understanding of supernatural energy had not begun with this world.

He had spent another lifetime learning to manipulate mana, developing spells, refining techniques, and understanding the intricacies of supernatural power.

Even if cursed energy followed different principles, the general concept of controlling an unfamiliar energy was hardly foreign to him.

There was also the matter of his previous existence.

He was not an ordinary reincarnated child.

He had been Chronos.

And although the circumstances surrounding his rebirth had changed his present existence, the knowledge and experience accumulated throughout his former life had not simply disappeared.

He possessed an understanding that no four-year-old should reasonably have.

The Gojo clan, of course, knew nothing about that.

They saw a prodigy.

A remarkably gifted child who might eventually become one of their greatest assets.

***

The consequences of his demonstrations were not limited to admiration.

His family's standing within the clan was also reconsidered.

Originally, Gojo Gin, Gojo Haku, and their son belonged to a branch family. Their position was respectable enough, but they did not enjoy the same privileges or influence as the main family.

That changed following Toki's demonstrations.

The clan elders recognized the significance of his abilities and approved the elevation of his family's standing.

They were no longer considered merely a branch household.

They were welcomed into the main clan.

It was a considerable improvement in their circumstances, one that brought with it greater privileges, access to resources, and a closer position to the center of the family's affairs.

His parents were naturally pleased.

Gin, in particular, seemed rather proud of his son.

Toki, meanwhile, found the entire development somewhat amusing.

He had spent his previous life navigating the complicated relationships between gods, adventurers, and various factions. Now, he was once again dealing with the politics of an influential family.

Some things apparently never changed.

He closed the book and carefully returned it to the table.

There was still much to learn.

He knew about cursed energy, cursed spirits, innate techniques, and the general structure of the jujutsu world. However, reading about these things was not the same as experiencing them firsthand.

There were also several questions he had yet to answer.

What would his innate technique be?

Would it be related to his previous existence?

And, more importantly, how much of what he once possessed would remain accessible in this new world?

He glanced towards the library window.

The afternoon sunlight illuminated the garden outside.

For the time being, there was no reason to rush.

He was four years old.

He had a family, a new name, and an entirely unfamiliar world waiting to be explored.

His fifth birthday was approaching.

And with it, the age at which his innate cursed technique would ordinarily manifest.

Toki smiled faintly.

He had already lived through one extraordinary life.

Perhaps this one would be interesting, too.

After all, he had been given a second beginning.

**This time, he was Gojo Toki.**
