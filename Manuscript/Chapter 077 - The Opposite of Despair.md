# Chapter Seventy-Seven — The Opposite of Despair

*Some people learned to endure suffering. Others learned to inflict it. And there were those who, having experienced both, eventually discovered that they could choose something different.*

---

## I. A New Beginning
*Bell's POV*

The walk back to the Hearth Manor was considerably quieter than the journey to the Guild.

Not because anyone was particularly unhappy.

Quite the opposite.

Hestia had spent the first several minutes enthusiastically discussing the paperwork, occasionally reminding Ein that she was now officially part of their Familia. Ein, meanwhile, seemed to be enjoying the unfamiliar experience of having someone welcome her so openly.

Bell found the entire situation rather pleasant.

Although, judging by the way Hestia kept glancing at their joined hands, he suspected that the goddess was already preparing several remarks.

"So!"

Hestia suddenly stopped.

Bell looked at her.

"Something wrong?"

"Nothing!"

She turned toward Ein.

"Actually, there's something I wanted to discuss."

Ein tilted her head.

"What is it, Lady Hestia?"

Hestia placed her hands against her hips.

"Well, now that you've officially joined my Familia, there's something rather important we need to take care of."

Ein blinked.

"...My Falna?"

"Exactly!"

Hestia smiled.

"You're technically a member now, but you haven't received my blessing yet. We should take care of that as soon as possible."

Bell nodded.

"That's probably for the best."

He glanced toward Ein.

"Unless you'd prefer to wait?"

Ein shook her head.

"No. I would like to receive it."

She paused.

Then looked at Hestia.

"If that's all right with you."

The goddess blinked.

Then her expression softened.

"Of course it is! You're my child now, aren't you?"

Ein smiled.

Bell couldn't help noticing how naturally she responded to that statement.

There had been a time when the idea of belonging to someone else's Familia might have carried an entirely different meaning for her.

Now, however, she seemed genuinely pleased.

Perhaps the distinction wasn't the Familia itself.

It was the freedom to choose.

"Then let's go home," Hestia declared. "We'll take care of it there."

Bell chuckled.

"You're rather enthusiastic."

"Of course I am! I'm a goddess! Giving Falna to my children is one of my responsibilities."

She puffed out her chest.

"And besides, I want to see what kind of status our newest member has!"

Bell raised an eyebrow.

"You're curious?"

"Naturally!"

Hestia looked toward Ein.

"Although, considering everything you've experienced, I wouldn't be surprised if your status turns out to be rather unusual."

Ein lowered her eyes.

"...I wonder."

Bell considered the matter.

There was something worth examining.

Ein had undergone a rather unusual sequence of transformations. The failed fusion, the separation of identities, the subsequent purification, and the restoration of her original biological condition.

He had already established that the corruption was gone.

But that didn't necessarily mean every consequence of the experience had disappeared.

More importantly, the Falna wasn't simply a record of physical capabilities.

It reflected something deeper.

An individual's development.

Their experiences.

Their potential.

And perhaps, to some extent, the nature of their soul.

Bell glanced toward Ein.

*I wonder what we'll find.*

He didn't particularly want to speculate.

Not before they had actual information.

For now, he was simply glad that she was walking beside them.

Alive.

Healthy.

And apparently looking forward to breakfast.

That was enough.

The Hearth Manor welcomed them with its familiar atmosphere.

Bell opened the door.

"Welcome home."

Hestia immediately raised both arms.

"WE'RE HOME!"

Her voice echoed throughout the building.

Bell looked at her.

"...You know, I don't think the house needed that announcement."

"Of course it did!"

"Why?"

"Because I said so!"

Ein giggled.

Bell shook his head.

Some things were apparently beyond rational explanation.

They entered the living area, and Hestia immediately made her way toward the sofa.

"All right! Let's get started."

Bell looked toward Ein.

"Would you like something to drink first?"

Ein considered it.

"Perhaps some water."

"Coming right up."

He headed toward the kitchen.

Behind him, Hestia began explaining the procedure.

There wasn't anything particularly complicated about receiving Falna. The deity would apply their ichor to the individual's back, allowing the divine blessing to establish its connection with the recipient.

The resulting status would then be recorded through the deity's authority.

However, the exact information revealed could vary.

Some individuals possessed extraordinary physical potential.

Others had unusual magical aptitude.

And occasionally, a person's experiences and circumstances resulted in skills that were considerably more specialized.

Bell returned with two glasses.

He placed one before Ein.

Then sat beside her.

"Ready?"

She nodded.

"Yes."

Hestia stood.

"Then let's move somewhere comfortable."

She smiled.

"And don't worry. I'll take care of everything."

Ein returned her smile.

"I trust you."

Hestia's expression brightened.

For a brief moment, the goddess looked as though she might cry.

Bell quietly looked away.

*Well, that's adorable.*

He decided not to say it aloud.

---

## II. The Blessing
*Ein's POV*

The room was quiet.

Ein sat with her back exposed, her hands resting loosely upon her knees.

Hestia stood behind her.

Bell remained nearby.

There was something strange about the situation.

Not unpleasant.

Simply unfamiliar.

For a long time, Ein's existence had been defined by circumstances beyond her control.

Her body had changed.

Her soul had been affected.

Her memories and experiences had become entangled with something she hadn't understood.

Even after the separation, she had spent considerable time wondering whether the person she remembered being still existed.

And now—

She was about to receive a blessing.

A new beginning.

One that she had chosen herself.

"All right, dear."

Hestia's voice interrupted her thoughts.

"Are you comfortable?"

"Yes."

"Good."

The goddess retrieved a small needle.

Bell watched quietly.

Hestia pricked her finger and allowed a single drop of ichor to fall upon Ein's back.

The divine substance touched her skin.

And the Falna began to take shape.

Ein closed her eyes.

For a moment, she felt nothing unusual.

Then—

Something stirred.

It wasn't pain.

Nor was it the familiar sensation of corruption attempting to spread through her body.

It was something considerably gentler.

A warmth.

A faint sensation that seemed to originate somewhere deep within her chest.

She inhaled.

The warmth spread.

Not violently.

Not forcefully.

It simply moved through her, following pathways she couldn't properly identify.

Her shoulders relaxed.

Her breathing became steadier.

And somewhere beneath the surface of her consciousness, something seemed to awaken.

Hestia's expression changed.

"...Oh?"

Bell immediately looked toward her.

"What is it?"

Hestia didn't answer immediately.

Her eyes remained fixed on Ein's back.

The divine markings were appearing normally.

At least, initially.

Then several inscriptions began developing alongside the ordinary structure of the Falna.

Hestia leaned closer.

"...That's unusual."

Ein opened her eyes.

"Did something happen?"

"Just a moment."

Hestia examined the markings more carefully.

Her expression gradually became thoughtful.

Bell approached.

"Can you read it?"

"Not yet. I'm still examining everything."

He nodded.

That was reasonable.

A status wasn't something he could interpret merely by looking at the surface.

The deity who had bestowed the Falna possessed the necessary authority to read it.

Bell decided not to interfere.

Instead, he observed Ein.

Her expression seemed peaceful.

There was no indication of pain or distress.

But something had changed.

He could feel a faint magical fluctuation around her.

Not particularly powerful.

More like a subtle resonance.

*Interesting.*

He glanced toward Hestia.

The goddess was still examining the inscription.

Then her eyebrows rose.

"...What?"

Bell looked at her.

"Something wrong?"

Hestia slowly straightened.

"No."

She looked toward Ein.

"Actually, I think we have something rather interesting here."

Ein tilted her head.

"Interesting?"

Hestia smiled.

"Very."

She glanced at Bell.

"Give me a moment. I want to read everything properly."

Bell nodded.

"Take your time."

Hestia placed her fingers against the inscription.

Her divine authority moved through the Falna.

And the information gradually became clear.

For several seconds, she remained silent.

Then she spoke.

"Well..."

Her expression became complicated.

"Ein, I think your soul has something to say."

## III. What the Soul Remembers
*Bell's POV*

Hestia looked at the inscription again.

Then she began reading aloud.

"All right. Let's see..."

Her eyes moved across the first section.

"Level One. Her basic abilities are still at their initial values, as expected."

Bell nodded.

Nothing unexpected there.

Ein had only just received her blessing. Whatever physical capabilities she possessed before receiving Falna, those weren't equivalent to recorded Falna development.

Hestia continued.

"Now, let's see what else..."

She stopped.

Her expression became increasingly unusual.

"Felicitas."

Bell tilted his head.

"Felicitas?"

"Yes. That's the first skill."

Hestia examined the inscription.

"Felicitas — Blessed Joy."

She read the description.

Then blinked.

"...Oh?"

Bell approached.

"What does it do?"

Hestia looked at him.

"It appears to convert positive emotions into magical and physical reinforcement."

Bell's expression became thoughtful.

"Positive emotions?"

"Yes. Happiness, hope, emotional fulfillment. Things like that."

She continued reading.

"There's something else. It becomes stronger when protecting someone precious, and it can apparently extend some of its effects to people with whom the user shares an emotional connection."

Bell remained silent.

He considered the implications.

An ability that converted emotional states into reinforcement wasn't entirely unprecedented as a concept, although the precise mechanism was unusual.

More importantly, the skill didn't appear to depend exclusively on the user's individual emotions.

It could draw upon something shared.

*An emotional feedback mechanism?*

He immediately reconsidered.

That was an interpretation, not an established explanation.

He needed observable evidence before assigning it a mechanism.

Hestia moved to the next inscription.

"Sancta Natura."

Her voice became slightly more serious.

"That means Sacred Nature."

She examined the description.

"...This one's considerably more interesting."

Bell raised an eyebrow.

"How so?"

"It seems to harmonize the user's body and soul with their natural existence."

Hestia continued reading.

"It suppresses abnormal transformations, resists interference with the soul, and improves natural regeneration. There's also resistance to curses, poison, and other abnormal conditions."

Bell's eyes narrowed slightly.

"That's quite a collection of properties."

"It is."

Hestia looked at the inscription again.

"There's a special property called Sanctuary."

"Sanctuary?"

"Apparently, it extends a weaker protective effect to nearby allies."

Bell considered the name.

A protective domain.

Or perhaps a localized extension of the user's natural state.

He didn't want to jump to conclusions.

Still, there was an interesting connection between the first two skills.

One reinforced life through emotional fulfillment.

The other preserved the integrity of the body and soul.

Neither appeared designed primarily for destruction.

"What's the third?"

Hestia looked down.

"Lux Aeterna."

She paused.

"Eternal Radiance."

Bell leaned slightly closer.

Hestia began reading.

"It changes the properties of the user's magic, allowing magical energy to interact positively with life and magical structures."

Her expression gradually brightened.

"Restorative light, improved healing, purification of hostile magical interference, and the ability to expose concealed magical structures."

Bell's eyes widened slightly.

"Wait."

He looked toward Ein.

"That sounds remarkably specialized."

Hestia nodded.

"Apparently, it is."

She examined the remaining inscription.

"There's a limitation, though. The purification doesn't automatically destroy enemies, and its effectiveness against ordinary physical threats is limited."

Bell nodded slowly.

That was important.

A power that specialized in purification and restoration shouldn't automatically become a universal defensive solution.

Otherwise, its scope would be far too broad.

"What's the first magic?"

Hestia looked down.

"Lux Thyrsos."

Then the second.

"Lux Grail."

And finally—

"Eunoia."

Bell went quiet.

Three spells.

The same number as the opposing skill structure.

He looked at Hestia.

"Can you read their descriptions?"

She nodded.

"Give me a moment."

Her eyes moved across the inscription.

"Lux Thyrsos appears to be an offensive lightning spell. It concentrates magical energy into golden-white lightning, with a particular ability to penetrate magical defenses through concentrated impact."

Bell considered that.

"Does it have any secondary properties?"

"There's something about residual energy accelerating the recovery of nearby allies."

He raised an eyebrow.

"An offensive spell with restorative side effects?"

"That's what it says."

Bell smiled faintly.

"That's certainly unusual."

Hestia continued.

"Lux Grail is defensive magic. It creates a circular barrier capable of absorbing and dispersing magical attacks. The barrier can gradually repair itself."

Bell nodded.

That was considerably more straightforward.

"Anything else?"

"There's an advanced property called Aegis of Heaven."

Hestia looked at him.

"It distributes incoming damage throughout the protective domain instead of allowing one section to collapse immediately."

Bell's expression became thoughtful.

"Interesting. But I assume it still has a limit."

"Yes. Sufficiently powerful attacks can break it."

"Good."

Hestia blinked.

"Good?"

"Not good for us, obviously. Good for the consistency of the ability."

He smiled.

"An absolute barrier would be a rather troublesome thing to work with."

Hestia gave him an exasperated look.

"You're really enjoying this, aren't you?"

"Not particularly."

He looked toward Ein.

"I'm more interested in what these abilities say about their owner."

Ein lowered her eyes.

For some reason, that statement made her nervous.

"Bell..."

He smiled.

"Don't worry. We don't need to understand everything today."

He turned toward Hestia.

"Could you record the complete status?"

"Of course."

Hestia retrieved a piece of paper.

She began copying the information.

There were no Development Abilities recorded.

No Mage.

No Healing.

No Abnormal Resistance.

No Spirit Healing.

No Protection.

Those remained possibilities for future development, not abilities she possessed simply because her skills resembled them.

Bell specifically noted that distinction.

The new Falna represented a beginning.

Not the completion of her development.

When Hestia finally finished, she placed the document on the table.

Ein looked at it.

For several seconds, she didn't speak.

Then she quietly asked:

"...Why?"

Bell turned toward her.

"Why what?"

She touched the paper.

"Why would I have these abilities?"

There was something uncertain in her voice.

"I don't understand. After everything that happened, I thought I would simply return to normal."

She looked down.

"But these abilities..."

Her fingers tightened around the document.

"They're not normal, are they?"

Bell considered his answer.

Then he sat beside her.

"Perhaps not."

He paused.

"But I don't think that's necessarily a bad thing."

Ein looked at him.

Bell continued.

"Remember what we discussed during the examination?"

She nodded.

"Being different doesn't automatically make someone a monster."

"That's right."

He smiled.

"Then I don't see why you should consider these abilities something to be ashamed of."

Ein remained silent.

Hestia approached them.

"Besides, they're your abilities, aren't they?"

She placed a hand against Ein's shoulder.

"You don't have to understand everything immediately. You can learn what they do, decide how you want to use them, and develop them at your own pace."

Ein looked toward the goddess.

Then slowly smiled.

"...Thank you."

Hestia returned the smile.

"You're welcome."

Bell glanced at the document again.

There was one detail he wanted to investigate.

Not the spells.

Not the individual skill descriptions.

Something much more fundamental.

The three skills seemed to form a coherent structure.

Joy.

Preservation.

Purification.

And the spells followed a similar direction.

Offense.

Defense.

Cooperation.

He leaned back.

*What exactly happened to her soul during the purification?*

The question remained unanswered.

And he wasn't going to pretend otherwise.

## IV. The First Experiment
*Bell's POV*

The laboratory was considerably more organized than it had been during the purification procedure.

Bell had deliberately removed most of the equipment that was no longer necessary.

There was no reason to preserve the appearance of an emergency examination chamber.

Instead, he had prepared a smaller testing area.

Several instruments occupied one side of the room. A collection of crystals, measuring devices, and magical containers had been arranged on a separate table.

Ein stood nearby.

Hestia sat comfortably in a chair.

She had insisted on accompanying them.

Apparently, she wanted to see her newest child's abilities firsthand.

Bell didn't particularly object.

Although he had established several precautions.

"Before we begin, I want to make something clear."

Ein looked toward him.

"What is it?"

"We're not going to test everything at once."

He pointed toward the instruments.

"First, we'll establish a baseline. Then we'll examine each ability individually, preferably through controlled applications."

Ein nodded.

"All right."

"Second, if anything feels uncomfortable, we stop."

"Understood."

"And finally..."

Bell looked at Hestia.

"Please don't encourage her to do anything reckless."

Hestia blinked.

"Why are you looking at me?"

"Because I know you."

"That's rude!"

Bell smiled.

Ein giggled.

The atmosphere became lighter.

And Bell found himself appreciating that.

Experiments didn't necessarily need to be conducted under unpleasant circumstances.

Especially not when the person being examined had already experienced enough of them.

"All right."

He retrieved a small notebook.

"Let's begin with the simplest question."

He looked toward Ein.

"What do you feel?"

Ein considered the question.

She closed her eyes.

At first, nothing happened.

Then she slowly raised her hand.

"...Warmth."

Bell wrote something down.

"Where?"

"Here."

She placed her hand against her chest.

"Not exactly on the surface. It's more like..."

She hesitated.

"...Something inside me is responding."

Bell nodded.

"Does it hurt?"

"No."

"Does it feel uncomfortable?"

"No."

"Then let's try something."

He approached her.

"Think about something that makes you happy."

Ein opened her eyes.

"...Something that makes me happy?"

"Yes. Don't force anything. Just think about it."

She looked down.

For a moment, she seemed uncertain.

Then her gaze moved toward Bell.

He blinked.

"...What?"

Ein shook her head.

"Nothing."

But a small smile had appeared on her face.

She closed her eyes again.

The warmth returned.

This time, the instruments reacted.

Bell immediately looked at the readings.

A slight fluctuation.

Then another.

The magical energy surrounding Ein became marginally denser.

Not enough to produce an obvious physical effect.

But enough to register.

Bell leaned closer.

"Again."

Ein opened her eyes.

"Again?"

"Yes. But this time, try to maintain the same emotional state."

She nodded.

The warmth returned.

The readings fluctuated.

Bell compared the measurements.

Then he frowned.

"Interesting."

Hestia leaned forward.

"What is it?"

"The magical output appears to increase alongside the emotional response."

He examined the measurements again.

"Not dramatically. But the correlation is consistent."

He looked toward Ein.

"Can you feel anything?"

She nodded.

"My body feels lighter."

"Any discomfort?"

"No."

Bell recorded the observation.

Then he considered another possibility.

"All right. Let's try something different."

He moved a short distance away.

"Think about the same thing again."

Ein did.

The fluctuation appeared.

But this time, Bell noticed something unusual.

The effect wasn't entirely concentrated around her.

A faint resonance extended toward him.

He glanced at the instruments.

Then at his own hand.

There was a subtle change.

Not enough to qualify as a meaningful increase in physical capability.

But something was happening.

"Wait."

He stepped closer.

The resonance strengthened slightly.

Hestia noticed his expression.

"What?"

Bell examined the readings.

"...I think it's extending beyond her."

Ein opened her eyes.

"To you?"

"Possibly."

He looked at the distance between them.

"The effect appears stronger when we're close."

Hestia smiled.

"Well, you two are practically inseparable."

Bell scratched his cheek.

"That's not exactly a scientific explanation."

Ein giggled.

But then Bell noticed something.

The readings changed again.

Not because Ein had become happier.

Rather, because she had started laughing.

And the magical resonance became noticeably more stable.

Bell slowly lowered his notebook.

*Shared happiness.*

The phrase appeared in his mind.

He looked at Ein.

Then at the instruments.

And finally, he smiled.

"All right."

He wrote another observation.

"Let's call that the first confirmed manifestation."

## V. The Nature of Preservation
*Bell's POV*

Bell changed the arrangement of the testing area.

The next experiment concerned Sancta Natura.

Unlike Felicitas, which appeared to respond to emotional conditions, the second skill seemed to operate through the relationship between the user's body and soul.

That distinction was important.

He placed a small crystal on the table.

"All right. For this experiment, we'll examine how your body responds to abnormal magical interference."

Ein nodded.

"Will it be dangerous?"

"Not with the arrangement I've prepared."

Bell pointed toward the crystal.

"I'm going to introduce a controlled disturbance into its magical structure. It won't affect your body directly. I simply want to observe how your skill responds when you interact with it."

Ein examined the crystal.

"And if it doesn't respond?"

"Then we'll have learned something."

Bell smiled.

"An unsuccessful experiment isn't necessarily useless."

Hestia nodded approvingly.

"That's right!"

Bell activated the formation.

A faint distortion appeared around the crystal.

The magical energy circulating inside it became irregular, producing several fluctuations that differed from its original pattern.

Ein watched.

At first, nothing happened.

Then she slowly extended her hand.

The moment her fingers approached the crystal, the readings changed.

The irregular fluctuations weakened.

Not immediately.

Gradually.

The abnormal pattern seemed to lose its coherence as the magical energy surrounding Ein became more stable.

Bell leaned closer.

"...That's interesting."

He adjusted the instrument.

The disturbance continued decreasing.

Then it stopped.

Ein withdrew her hand.

The crystal returned to its original condition.

Bell examined the measurements.

"Well, that's promising."

Hestia looked toward him.

"What happened?"

"Her skill appears to have suppressed the abnormal magical interference."

He checked the readings again.

"But I don't want to call this purification just yet. We haven't established whether it can affect actual curses, foreign magical structures, or other forms of abnormal energy."

Ein looked at her hand.

"Did I do something?"

"Yes."

Bell smiled.

"Your body seems to recognize the disturbance as something that doesn't belong."

He paused.

"Or at least, that's my current interpretation."

Ein considered his words.

"Something that doesn't belong..."

She looked at the crystal again.

Then lowered her hand.

"I think I understand."

Bell raised an eyebrow.

"Do you?"

She nodded.

"When I was corrupted, there was always something inside me that felt wrong. Even when I wasn't consciously thinking about it, I could feel that something was interfering with my existence."

Her fingers slowly curled.

"It was as though I had to constantly make room for something that shouldn't have been there."

She looked toward him.

"But now..."

She placed her hand against her chest.

"I don't feel that anymore."

Bell listened.

Ein continued.

"Maybe that's what this skill is doing. It recognizes something that doesn't belong and tries to preserve what was already there."

Bell became quiet.

That was an interesting way of describing it.

More importantly, it was an interpretation coming from the person experiencing the ability.

He wrote it down.

**Observation: Sancta Natura appears to favor the preservation of the user's original biological and spiritual integrity.**

He underlined the word *appears*.

Then added another note.

**Further testing required. The current experiment does not establish resistance to every curse, transformation, or form of soul interference.**

"All right."

Bell closed his notebook.

"Let's try something else."

Ein looked at him.

"Something else?"

"The special property."

"Sanctuary?"

"Exactly."

He glanced toward Hestia.

"Would you mind standing a little farther away?"

Hestia blinked.

"Me?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because I need another person to observe the effect."

Hestia stood.

She moved several steps away.

Bell looked toward Ein.

"Try extending the same sensation you felt earlier."

Ein closed her eyes.

She concentrated.

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then a faint warmth spread around her.

Bell watched the readings.

The magical field expanded.

Not particularly far.

Perhaps a few steps at most.

Hestia tilted her head.

"...Oh?"

She raised her hand.

A faint sensation had appeared around her body.

It wasn't a barrier.

Nor was it a particularly strong defensive effect.

It felt more like the surrounding magical energy had become slightly more stable.

Hestia looked toward Ein.

"That's rather comfortable."

Ein opened her eyes.

"Really?"

"Yes!"

Bell examined the instruments.

The effect was measurable.

But the moment Ein attempted to extend it farther, the readings changed.

Her expression tightened.

The field flickered.

Then disappeared.

"Ah..."

She lowered her hand.

Bell immediately approached.

"Stop there."

Ein looked at him.

"Sorry."

"There's nothing to apologize for."

He examined her condition.

"How are you feeling?"

"A little tired."

"Mentally?"

She nodded.

Bell made another note.

There it was.

The cost.

Extending the protection required Mind, and increasing the affected area placed additional strain on her concentration.

He looked toward Hestia.

"Did you notice anything?"

Hestia nodded.

"It wasn't particularly powerful, but I could feel something protecting me."

"Did it interfere with your own magic?"

"No."

"Did you feel any unusual pressure?"

"Not really."

Bell nodded.

That was enough for a preliminary observation.

The skill could extend a protective effect to nearby allies.

However, its effectiveness diminished with distance and increased Mind consumption.

He closed his notebook.

"That's enough for today."

Ein blinked.

"Already?"

"Yes."

She smiled.

"But we haven't tested everything."

"And we won't."

Bell smiled.

"There's no advantage in exhausting you simply because we're curious."

Hestia nodded.

"Listen to him. He's right."

Ein looked between them.

Then smiled.

"...All right."

Bell returned the equipment to its original position.

He had learned considerably more than he expected.

But one question remained.

Lux Aeterna.

The third skill.

And perhaps the most interesting one from a research perspective.

## VI. Light That Reveals
*Bell's POV*

The final experiment required a different arrangement.

Bell prepared a small magical construct.

It was a simple device designed to conceal the structure of its internal formation.

Nothing particularly dangerous.

Its purpose was to test whether Lux Aeterna could identify hidden magical structures and interfere with hostile magical effects.

He activated the construct.

The surrounding air remained unchanged.

To an ordinary observer, there was nothing unusual about the device.

Bell, however, knew precisely what it was doing.

Several magical inscriptions were concealed beneath its outer structure.

A second layer interfered with the readings produced by conventional detection methods.

He looked toward Ein.

"Can you see anything unusual?"

She examined the construct.

"No."

"Good. That's what I expected."

He smiled.

"Now, try using Lux Aeterna."

Ein hesitated.

"How?"

Bell considered the question.

"Don't force it. Focus on the magic itself. Try to understand what you're attempting to do rather than simply producing more energy."

She nodded.

Her eyes closed.

A faint glow appeared around her hand.

It was different from the warmth associated with Felicitas.

This light was clearer.

More concentrated.

And strangely, it seemed to interact with the magical energy surrounding her rather than simply illuminating the room.

Ein opened her eyes.

"...I can see something."

Bell immediately looked at the construct.

"What?"

"Lines."

She raised her hand.

"There are lines inside it. I can see where the magic is connected."

Bell's expression changed.

He checked the instrument.

The concealed inscriptions had become visible.

Not physically.

Rather, their magical structure had been exposed.

He leaned forward.

"Can you identify the interference?"

Ein nodded.

"It's connected to the outer formation."

"Can you remove it?"

She hesitated.

"I think so."

"Don't attempt anything yet."

Bell examined the readings.

"Let's establish what you're seeing first."

He pointed toward the device.

"Describe the structure."

Ein began explaining.

Her observations were surprisingly accurate.

She could identify the concealed inscriptions, distinguish their connections, and even recognize the point where the interference originated.

Bell compared her description with his own records.

The correspondence was remarkable.

He slowly smiled.

"That's excellent."

Ein looked at him.

"Really?"

"Yes. But there's something else I'd like to test."

He adjusted the construct.

A small amount of hostile magical interference began circulating through its outer formation.

Not enough to cause damage.

Just enough to establish a controlled target.

"Try neutralizing that portion."

Ein concentrated.

The light around her hand intensified.

For a moment, the construct resisted.

Then the interference weakened.

The concealed formation became unstable.

And finally, the hostile magical structure collapsed.

The remaining magical energy dispersed harmlessly.

Bell stared at the readings.

"...There it is."

Hestia leaned forward.

"What happened?"

"She removed the hostile magical interference without dismantling the entire construct."

He examined the readings again.

"That's considerably more precise than I expected."

Ein looked at her hand.

The light gradually faded.

Perhaps that was why she had never entirely accepted Dionysus's plans.

She knew what it meant to be treated as something worth protecting.

And she knew what it meant to be treated as a tool.

Filvis closed her eyes.

"Please..."

Her voice was barely audible.

"Let her have the life she deserves."

Then she stood.

There was still a role to play.

And she intended to play it until the opportunity to change the situation finally presented itself.

## XV. Something Worth Protecting
*Ein's POV*

The night was peaceful.

Ein sat beside Bell in the living area.

A cup of warm tea rested between her hands.

She had spent the past several minutes listening to Bell explain some of his research notes, although she suspected he was deliberately simplifying certain parts of the explanation.

She didn't mind.

It was nice listening to him talk about something he enjoyed.

Eventually, he stopped.

"You're not paying attention."

Ein blinked.

"I am!"

"Really?"

"Of course."

Bell raised an eyebrow.

"What was I explaining?"

She looked at him.

Then smiled.

"...Something about magical efficiency."

He laughed.

"That's technically correct."

Ein giggled.

"See?"

"You're impossible."

He leaned back.

For a while, they simply enjoyed the quiet.

Then Ein noticed something.

A faint warmth had appeared around them.

It wasn't particularly strong.

But she recognized it.

Felicitas.

The warmth spread gently through the space between them.

Bell noticed the change.

He glanced toward the nearby instrument he had brought from the laboratory.

"...Wait."

The readings were fluctuating.

He examined them.

The effect wasn't coming from Ein alone.

There was a second resonance.

His own presence appeared to be contributing to the fluctuation.

Then the surrounding magical energy stabilized.

A faint protective field formed around them.

Bell looked toward Ein.

"Sancta Natura?"

She nodded.

"I think so."

The field expanded slightly.

Then a soft radiance appeared.

Lux Aeterna.

For a brief moment, the three effects overlapped.

The surrounding atmosphere became noticeably warmer.

Bell felt some of his accumulated fatigue ease.

Not disappear.

Just lessen.

Ein looked at him.

"Are you feeling better?"

He blinked.

"...A little."

She smiled.

"Good."

Bell examined the readings again.

The phenomenon was extremely limited.

It wasn't a fully developed domain.

It hadn't restored injuries or replenished enormous quantities of Mind.

It was simply an incomplete interaction between the three skills.

But it was enough to confirm that their effects could overlap.

He slowly smiled.

"Well..."

Ein tilted her head.

"What?"

"Nothing."

He put the instrument aside.

"Just something worth remembering."

She leaned against his shoulder.

Bell gently wrapped an arm around her.

Outside, the evening breeze moved through the trees surrounding the manor.

The city continued its ordinary activities.

Somewhere within it, another individual was preparing to challenge the circumstances that had defined her life.

And here, in a quiet room, someone else was discovering that she could finally live without being defined by hers.

Ein closed her eyes.

"Bell?"

"Yes?"

"Do you think I'll be able to help other people someday?"

He looked down at her.

"Of course."

"Even if I'm not particularly strong?"

Bell smiled.

"Strength isn't the only thing that matters."

He gently brushed her hair aside.

"And you don't need to become the strongest person in the world to make a difference."

Ein considered that.

Then smiled.

"...I would like that."

Bell looked toward the notebook resting on the table.

Three skills.

Three spells.

And a possibility that remained far beyond their current understanding.

He thought about the word he had written earlier.

Elysium.

Perhaps one day.

But not today.

Today, Ein had learned to create a barrier.

She had produced her first lightning spear.

She had briefly manifested another version of herself.

And, more importantly, she had laughed without being afraid of what might happen afterward.

Bell closed his eyes.

For now, that was enough.

And somewhere beyond the walls of the manor, the faint connection between two individuals remained.

Not a chain.

Not a command.

Not an obligation.

Simply a reminder that they had once shared something.

And that they were now free to decide what it would mean.
