# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

# Chapter Seventy-Eight — The Fool

*Some people spend centuries searching for the answer to a question they have already forgotten.*

### I. A Present for Grandfather

A week had passed since Bell Cranel had asked Uranus for permission.

Seven days.

It wasn't particularly long by ordinary standards, especially considering the age of the person he intended to visit. However, Bell had made a promise, and he didn't particularly enjoy leaving promises unattended.

Besides, there was something he wanted to discuss with his grandfather.

And perhaps, more importantly, he wanted to visit him.

Bell sat at his desk, staring at a small collection of objects arranged before him.

There were several possibilities.

A book?

Probably unnecessary. Uranus had existed for longer than most civilizations. The chances of finding something he hadn't already read were rather low.

A weapon?

That was even worse.

Giving a weapon to a primordial being sounded less like presenting a gift and more like handing a sword to someone who had already witnessed the invention of swords.

Food?

Bell considered it.

Then dismissed the idea.

He wasn't entirely certain whether Uranus even needed to eat.

He leaned back in his chair.

"That's surprisingly difficult."

Across the room, Ein tilted her head.

"What is?"

Bell pointed toward the desk.

"Finding a present for my grandfather."

Ein looked at the objects.

Then at him.

"Why?"

"Because I'm visiting him."

"Does that mean you have to bring something?"

"No, but..."

Bell scratched his cheek.

"It's a courtesy, isn't it?"

Ein considered his answer.

Then smiled.

"You're thinking too much."

"Am I?"

"Yes."

She approached his desk and picked up one of the objects.

It was a small decorative ornament Bell had considered giving Uranus.

"Perhaps he would appreciate this."

"Perhaps."

Bell looked at it.

Then shook his head.

"Although I doubt he's particularly interested in material possessions."

"Then give him something else."

"Like what?"

Ein returned the ornament to the desk.

"Something that matters."

Bell fell silent.

Something that matters.

He considered the suggestion.

His grandfather wasn't particularly interested in material possessions. He already possessed knowledge, authority, and experiences that Bell couldn't begin to comprehend.

There was, however, another person who might appreciate a gift.

Bell's eyes suddenly brightened.

"Oh."

Ein looked at him.

"What?"

"Fels."

"The Fool?"

"Yes!"

Bell stood.

"Now that's an idea!"

Ein blinked.

Bell began pacing around the room.

"Grandfather doesn't really need anything, but Fels..."

He stopped.

A rather peculiar smile appeared on his face.

"Fels might."

The Fool.

A being who had once been a human.

An alchemist.

A researcher.

And, most importantly, the creator of the Philosopher's Stone.

An achievement that should have been celebrated throughout history.

Instead, their god had punished them for it.

Their flesh had been stripped away, leaving behind a skeletal existence that resembled neither a living human nor an ordinary undead creature.

Eight centuries.

Eight hundred years of existence without the body they once possessed.

Bell's smile gradually widened.

"Yes. I think I've found the perfect present."

Ein stared at him.

She already had a suspicion that whatever Bell had just decided would probably be rather unusual.

"Bell?"

"Hm?"

"What exactly are you planning to give Fels?"

He looked at her.

Then answered with complete sincerity.

"Their body."

Ein paused.

"...Their body?"

"Well, technically, a restoration."

Bell raised one finger.

"Not a replacement. Not an artificial construct. An actual restoration."

He sounded increasingly excited.

"I've been thinking about it ever since the experiments with you."

Ein watched him carefully.

Bell continued.

"The body isn't simply a collection of bones and organs. Every component has to function together. Muscles, connective tissues, nerves, blood vessels, organs..."

He began counting with his fingers.

"And bones, obviously. We can't forget those."

Ein smiled.

"You're excited."

"Of course I am!"

Bell laughed.

"Imagine it. Eight hundred years without a body, and suddenly—"

He spread his arms.

"Welcome back!"

Ein couldn't help giggling.

"You're making it sound rather simple."

"It isn't."

His expression became thoughtful.

"Actually, that's the interesting part."

He returned to his desk and retrieved his notebook.

There were several pages filled with observations from his recent experiments.

His work on Ein had provided him with an extraordinary amount of information concerning biological structures and the relationship between physical integrity and magical interference.

The previous experiments had forced him to consider something important.

Restoration wasn't necessarily the same as healing.

Healing repaired damage.

Restoration returned something to its intended state.

And if a body had been deliberately deprived of its physical components, the problem became considerably more complicated.

He would need to reconstruct everything.

Not merely the visible parts.

The muscles.

The tissues.

The internal organs.

The nervous system.

The circulatory system.

And the skeletal structure.

Fortunately, he had already begun developing the necessary method.

He looked toward Ein.

"I think I can do it."

She approached him.

"Are you certain?"

Bell smiled.

"Not completely."

"Bell."

"But I'm confident."

He gently took her hand.

"And besides, I have someone who can help me."

Ein raised an eyebrow.

"Who?"

"You."

She blinked.

Then smiled.

"That's hardly fair."

"Why?"

"You're making it difficult for me to refuse."

Bell laughed.

"Then I suppose I'll have to take responsibility."

He picked up his notebook.

"Come on. Let's visit my grandfather."

### II. The Road to the Heavens

Their preparations didn't take particularly long.

Bell had already arranged the necessary materials, while Ein required little beyond making herself comfortable.

Before leaving, Bell checked the contents of his small bag.

A few instruments.

Several prepared magical components.

His notebook.

And one additional item.

A small mirror.

Ein noticed it.

"What's that for?"

Bell glanced at it.

"Just in case."

"In case of what?"

He smiled.

"You'll see."

She shook her head.

Bell extended his hand.

Ein accepted it.

A moment later, the surrounding space began to distort.

Their bodies disappeared from the laboratory.

***

The air was different.

Bell noticed it immediately.

The surroundings possessed a peculiar stillness, one that couldn't be found in ordinary places.

They stood before an enormous structure.

The Prayer Altar of Uranus.

Or, more precisely, the entrance leading toward it.

Bell glanced around.

"Hm?"

Ein looked at him.

"What is it?"

"We're outside."

She examined the surroundings.

"Is that unusual?"

"Well, I expected to arrive inside."

Bell approached the entrance.

There was something peculiar about the space surrounding the structure.

A faint distortion.

Not particularly noticeable unless someone knew what to look for.

He extended his hand.

A subtle magical fluctuation passed through his fingers.

"A barrier."

Ein looked toward the entrance.

"Do you think Fels placed it?"

"Probably."

Bell withdrew his hand.

"Although I suppose it's reasonable. This isn't exactly a place where anyone should be able to teleport freely."

He examined the enormous doors.

They were metallic.

Massive.

Their surfaces possessed a peculiar sheen, resembling polished Mithril.

Bell approached them.

Then raised his hand.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

The sound echoed through the surrounding structure.

He waited.

Nothing happened.

Bell tilted his head.

Then knocked again.

A moment later, something shifted beyond the doors.

A faint mechanical sound reverberated through the entrance.

The massive doors slowly opened.

Bell immediately smiled.

"Good afternoon!"

And stepped inside.

***

The interior was precisely as he remembered it.

The enormous chamber.

The solemn atmosphere.

The stone chair positioned at its center.

And the ancient being seated upon it.

Uranus.

Bell's grandfather.

"Grandfather!"

The boy's voice carried through the chamber.

Uranus slowly raised his gaze.

For a moment, the ancient being simply regarded his great-grandson.

Then his expression softened.

"Bell."

The boy approached with considerably more enthusiasm than the surroundings seemed to warrant.

"It's been a while!"

"Only seven days."

"Exactly!"

Uranus chuckled.

"An extraordinary length of time."

Bell scratched his cheek.

"Well, I didn't want to keep you waiting."

"Did you come here for another discussion?"

"Partly."

Bell glanced toward Ein.

"But I also brought someone."

Uranus followed his gaze.

Ein stood slightly behind Bell.

She inclined her head respectfully.

"Greetings, Lord Uranus."

Bell immediately turned toward his grandfather.

"Oh! Right. Grandfather, this is Ein."

He reached for her hand.

"My girlfriend."

There was a brief silence.

Uranus looked at the two young individuals.

Then returned his gaze to Bell.

"I see."

Bell smiled.

"She's the person I told you about."

"Indeed. The one whose condition required the use of Arcanum."

Ein lowered her head slightly.

"I owe you my gratitude. Your permission allowed Bell to save me."

Uranus regarded her for several moments.

Then spoke gently.

"You need not thank me for a decision that was ultimately his to make."

His eyes returned to Bell.

"Although I must admit, you have accomplished something rather extraordinary."

Bell scratched his cheek.

"I had help."

"Naturally."

Uranus's expression softened.

"One should never underestimate the importance of assistance."

Bell nodded.

"That's true."

And so their conversation began.

***

Hours passed.

Bell spoke about his recent work.

He explained the results of the purification process, the strange properties of the corruption, and the conclusions he had reached concerning the Dungeon.

Uranus listened attentively.

Occasionally, he offered observations of his own.

Ein participated whenever she could, although much of the conversation concerned subjects she hadn't personally studied.

At one point, Bell began discussing the relationship between biological structures and magical interference.

Uranus listened.

Then asked a question.

"Do you believe the body is merely a vessel?"

Bell paused.

He considered the question.

"Not exactly."

"Explain."

"Well..."

Bell leaned back slightly.

"I think it's more accurate to consider the body a system. The organs, tissues, nerves, and everything else aren't independent components. They interact with one another."

He gestured toward himself.

"If you remove one part, the others might continue functioning for a while. But eventually, the entire system becomes unstable."

"And what of the soul?"

Bell considered it.

"That's a different question."

Uranus smiled faintly.

"Indeed."

Bell continued.

"I don't think restoring the body automatically means restoring everything that belongs to a person. There's still the question of memory, identity, and whatever relationship exists between the soul and the physical structure."

Ein looked at him.

Bell shrugged.

"I'm still investigating."

Uranus nodded.

"An appropriate answer."

The conversation continued.

At some point, Bell even managed to make a joke concerning his grandfather's age.

Uranus found it amusing.

Ein found Bell considerably more amusing.

And for a while, the ancient altar felt less like a sacred place and more like an ordinary family gathering.

Until—

A door opened somewhere behind them.

### III. The Fool

The sound was subtle.

A mechanical click.

Then the slow movement of a door.

Bell turned his head.

Someone was approaching.

Their footsteps were measured.

Unhurried.

There was no particular urgency in their movements.

A dark robe concealed most of their skeletal frame.

Their face was hidden beneath the familiar hood.

Fels.

The Fool.

They entered the chamber without haste.

"Lord Uranus."

Their voice was quiet and somewhat hollow.

Then their gaze shifted toward the visitors.

"Bell Cranel."

Bell smiled.

"Hello, Fels!"

The skeletal figure inclined their head.

"Lady Ein."

Ein returned the greeting.

"Good afternoon."

Fels approached.

Their presence was peculiar.

Even after encountering them previously, Bell couldn't help finding their existence fascinating.

A skeleton.

Yet not entirely.

There was something almost spectral about their appearance, as though the absence of flesh had transformed them into something that existed between life and death.

Bell watched them for several seconds.

Then tilted his head.

"Fels?"

"Yes?"

"Can I ask you something?"

"Of course."

Bell's expression became unusually serious.

"If you were given the opportunity to become whole again..."

He paused.

"...Would you still want immortality?"

The chamber became quiet.

Fels didn't answer immediately.

Bell continued.

"Or would you rather return to being human? To having an actual body again, even if it meant giving up whatever existence you have now?"

Ein glanced toward Bell.

Uranus remained silent.

Fels stood motionless.

For someone who had existed for eight hundred years, it was a rather unusual question.

They had experienced countless things.

The rise and fall of civilizations.

The advancement of knowledge.

The discovery of secrets that should have remained hidden.

And yet, somewhere along the way, they had forgotten something.

Something important.

Their own identity.

Fels slowly lowered their head.

A minute passed.

Then another.

Finally, they spoke.

"If I could..."

Their hollow voice echoed through the chamber.

"...I would have done that long ago."

Bell looked at them.

Fels continued.

"I have spent centuries in this condition. I have pursued knowledge, conducted experiments, and sought answers to questions that others would consider impossible."

Their skeletal fingers moved slightly.

"Yet there are certain things that knowledge alone cannot restore."

They looked toward their hands.

"Time has passed. My former existence has become increasingly distant."

A pause.

"I cannot even remember what it felt like."

The Fool lowered their head.

"Perhaps I once considered this immortality a blessing."

Their voice became quieter.

"But I no longer know."

Bell smiled.

Not mockingly.

Not triumphantly.

Just genuinely.

"Well."

He raised his right hand.

"Your wish is granted."

Fels tilted their head.

"...Pardon?"

Bell's smile widened.

"Your present."

For a moment, nobody moved.

Then Bell spoke a few unfamiliar words.

The air around him changed.

A faint magical fluctuation emerged.

But nothing happened.

Fels looked toward him.

"Bell Cranel, what are you—"

"Chant Delay."

The boy smiled.

"I've already completed the incantation. All that's left is activating it."

Uranus's expression changed slightly.

He recognized the technique.

A high-tier mage technique.

The ability to separate the completion of an incantation from its activation, allowing the prepared magic to remain dormant until the appropriate moment.

Bell had already prepared everything.

The spell was waiting.

And now—

He released it.

### IV. A Gift for the Fool

Light engulfed the chamber.

It began around Fels.

A soft radiance surrounded their skeletal frame, gradually intensifying until their entire body disappeared behind it.

Ein instinctively stepped closer to Bell.

Uranus watched.

The inscriptions within the magical formation illuminated simultaneously.

Bell raised his hand.

He was concentrating.

The process wasn't particularly simple.

He had to reconstruct the physical structures without introducing unnecessary abnormalities.

The bones came first.

The skeletal framework began forming.

One after another, the missing components returned.

Then came the connective tissues.

Muscles.

Blood vessels.

Nerves.

Internal organs.

The restoration continued.

Every missing structure was being reconstructed.

Not replaced with an artificial imitation.

Not covered by an illusion.

Restored.

Bell's expression became increasingly focused.

He monitored the magical flow.

The spell was functioning.

However, he wasn't going to celebrate prematurely.

There were too many things that could go wrong.

He adjusted the output.

Then maintained the flow.

The light intensified.

For a brief moment, the chamber was almost completely obscured.

And then—

It began to fade.

Slowly.

Gradually.

Until the light finally disappeared.

Silence followed.

Where the skeletal Fool had once stood, there was now a person.

A man.

He wore a tattered robe that had somehow remained around his restored body.

His hair was spiky and dirty-blonde, with distinctive green-tipped strands.

His eyes were a deep red.

And two diagonal scar-like marks extended across his forehead and eyebrows.

He stood motionless.

For several seconds, he didn't appear to understand what had happened.

Then—

He looked down.

His hands.

His arms.

His chest.

He slowly raised one hand toward his face.

Fingers touched his cheek.

Warmth.

Texture.

A sensation he hadn't experienced for eight centuries.

He inhaled.

The air entered his lungs.

His chest expanded.

His heart beat.

Once.

Twice.

He staggered slightly.

Bell immediately stepped forward.

"Are you all right?"

The man didn't answer.

His eyes were moving rapidly.

He looked around.

Then toward the nearest reflective surface.

A mirror.

He approached it.

Slowly.

Almost cautiously.

As though afraid that the image might disappear if he moved too quickly.

He stared.

A man stared back.

Dirty-blonde hair.

Green-tipped strands.

Red eyes.

Two distinctive marks.

His face.

His own face.

For eight hundred years, he hadn't remembered what he looked like.

He hadn't even remembered whether he had once possessed a face worth remembering.

And now—

Something clicked.

A small fragment.

Then another.

A chain reaction.

Memories.

Not all at once.

Not perfectly.

But enough.

His fingers tightened against the mirror.

His eyes widened.

"...Ah."

A faint sound escaped his throat.

He looked at his reflection again.

Then—

His expression changed.

The confusion disappeared.

The unfamiliarity vanished.

And something buried beneath centuries of forgotten memories finally resurfaced.

His name.

His identity.

His purpose.

His dream.

"...Senku."

The man whispered.

He stared at his reflection.

Then repeated it.

"Senku Ishigami."

His voice trembled.

He raised a hand toward his forehead.

Those distinctive marks.

His hair.

His eyes.

He remembered.

He remembered who he was.

A genius.

An inventor.

A scientist.

Someone who had dedicated his existence to understanding the world.

Someone who had pursued knowledge not merely for the sake of possessing it, but because there were things worth saving.

And suddenly—

Another memory surfaced.

A particular desire.

A particular objective.

His eyes widened.

"The cure..."

He spoke almost inaudibly.

"The petrification..."

His fingers trembled.

"Medusa."

The name struck him with extraordinary clarity.

That was it.

That was what he had been searching for.

Not immortality.

Not eternal existence.

A cure.

A means of reversing the petrification caused by that monstrous creature.

A way to save those who had been turned to stone.

His mind raced.

The memories were incomplete, but the underlying purpose was unmistakable.

He had wanted to create something.

Something capable of reversing petrification.

And he had been punished.

His flesh.

His body.

His memories.

Everything had been taken.

As though someone had deliberately removed the very knowledge that had driven him to pursue his research.

Osiris.

His god.

The one who had stripped him of his flesh.

For eight hundred years, he had remained the Fool.

A being who possessed extraordinary knowledge but had forgotten the reason he had sought it.

And now—

He remembered.

### V. The First Tear

Bell watched quietly.

He hadn't expected the restoration to produce such a reaction.

He had expected surprise.

Perhaps disbelief.

Maybe even excitement.

But this—

This was different.

The newly restored man stood before the mirror, staring at his reflection.

His shoulders began trembling.

His eyes became moist.

And then—

A tear fell.

A real tear.

It travelled down his cheek.

For a moment, he simply stood there.

Then another followed.

And another.

He raised his hand.

Touched his face.

Looked at the moisture on his fingertips.

He laughed.

A small, broken sound.

"...I can cry."

His voice trembled.

He looked toward Bell.

The boy stood a short distance away.

Still smiling.

Still watching.

And suddenly, the man understood.

This wasn't merely a restoration.

It was a second beginning.

He looked at Bell.

His deep red eyes were filled with something that had been absent for centuries.

Joy.

Pure, overwhelming happiness.

"Thank you."

The words came out quietly.

Then he laughed again.

"Thank you..."

His voice broke.

For thirty seconds, perhaps a little longer, he allowed himself to cry.

Not because he was grieving.

Not because he was remembering the suffering.

But because, for the first time in eight hundred years, he could feel himself.

He could breathe.

He could cry.

He could smile.

He could exist as the person he had once been.

And that was enough.

Bell scratched his cheek.

"You're welcome."

Ein smiled softly.

Uranus watched the scene without interrupting.

There was something rather extraordinary about witnessing such a moment.

A being who had existed for centuries as a reminder of punishment had finally regained something that should never have been taken from them.

A body.

A face.

And perhaps, most importantly—

A reason.

The man wiped his eyes.

Then looked toward Bell.

His expression changed.

A peculiar spark appeared in his eyes.

"Hey."

Bell tilted his head.

"Yes?"

The man approached.

"How exactly did you do that?"

Bell blinked.

"...Huh?"

"Your restoration magic."

His eyes were already moving.

Examining Bell.

Then the surrounding magical formation.

Then the remaining traces of energy.

"That wasn't ordinary healing."

Bell smiled.

"Well, technically—"

"Wait."

The man raised a hand.

"Did you reconstruct the entire biological structure? Including the nervous system?"

Bell nodded.

"Yes."

"How?"

"That's a rather long explanation."

"Good."

The man smiled.

"Then let's start with the fundamentals."

Bell stared at him.

Ein covered her mouth.

Uranus chuckled.

It seemed that the tears had finally stopped.

And the Fool had found something else to do.

### VI. The Seventieth Floor

"Before you leave," Uranus spoke.

The newly restored man turned.

"Lord Uranus."

The ancient being regarded him.

"There is something I believe you should know."

The man paused.

Uranus looked toward Bell.

Then back toward the restored individual.

"Your existence as the Fool was not without historical consequence."

Bell tilted his head.

"Historical consequence?"

Uranus nodded.

"Eight centuries ago, Fels descended into the Dungeon."

The man's expression changed.

"How far?"

"To the seventieth floor."

Bell's eyes widened.

"Seventieth?"

He looked toward Fels.

"Wait, you went that deep?"

Uranus continued.

"It was an undertaking that few would have considered reasonable. The expedition encountered circumstances that threatened to become a disaster."

His voice became solemn.

"At the time, the Dungeon had become unusually active. The expedition suffered substantial losses, and the survivors were forced to retreat under conditions that should have made their return impossible."

Bell listened carefully.

Uranus continued.

"Fels was among those who reached the deepest point of that expedition. Their knowledge and preparations allowed the surviving members to return."

The restored man looked down.

A faint expression of concentration appeared on his face.

Something was familiar.

A dark corridor.

The sound of footsteps.

The sensation of descending.

And a distant memory of something enormous moving in the darkness.

But the images were incomplete.

"Seventy..."

He repeated the number.

Uranus nodded.

"That expedition became known as the Seventieth-Floor Retreat. Its records were eventually preserved as a warning against pursuing the Dungeon without sufficient preparation."

Bell looked at Fels.

"So you were part of a historical expedition?"

The man gave a small smile.

"Apparently."

Uranus's expression softened.

"You were more than a participant."

He paused.

"You were one of the reasons anyone returned."

For a moment, the restored man remained silent.

Then he looked toward his hands.

"Interesting."

His expression gradually changed.

A familiar excitement was returning.

"Very interesting."

Bell smiled.

It seemed that his present had come with an additional benefit.

Fels was no longer simply a mysterious skeletal attendant.

They were someone with a history.

A history that had been waiting to be remembered.

### VII. The Scientist

The conversation didn't last much longer.

At least, not in the way Bell had expected.

The newly restored man suddenly turned toward the door.

"Where's the laboratory?"

Bell blinked.

"Your laboratory?"

"Yes."

He looked at Uranus.

"Is it still there?"

Uranus nodded.

"Your former facilities have been preserved."

"Excellent."

The man smiled.

Then looked toward Bell.

"I'll need access to several things."

Bell raised an eyebrow.

"Such as?"

"Writing materials. Measuring instruments. Chemical apparatus. A place to work."

He paused.

"And a mirror."

Bell smiled.

"You already have one."

"Good."

The man adjusted his tattered robe.

"Then I suppose there's no reason to waste time."

He began walking.

His steps were considerably more energetic than before.

Bell watched him go.

Then looked toward Uranus.

"Grandfather?"

"Yes?"

"Is he always like that?"

Uranus smiled.

"Not quite."

Bell tilted his head.

"What's that supposed to mean?"

"It means you have given someone back their reason to pursue the world."

The ancient being looked toward the departing figure.

"Whether that is a blessing or a troublesome development remains to be seen."

Bell laughed.

"Well, I suppose we'll find out."

Ein gently took his hand.

"Are you happy?"

Bell looked at her.

"Of course."

He smiled.

"Although I think I might have accidentally created another problem."

Ein giggled.

"Another?"

"Yes."

He glanced toward the laboratory entrance.

"An eight-hundred-year-old scientist with a renewed interest in research."

Uranus chuckled.

"Then perhaps you should prepare yourself."

Bell sighed dramatically.

"Grandfather, please don't say that."

For a moment, the chamber returned to its earlier atmosphere.

A quiet conversation between family members.

Nothing particularly extraordinary.

And yet, something extraordinary had happened.

The Fool had been restored.

And the man who had once existed behind that title had finally returned.

### VIII. A Promise Kept

Bell eventually turned toward Uranus.

"Grandfather."

The ancient being raised his eyes.

"Yes, Bell?"

"I should probably get going."

"Already?"

Bell nodded.

"There's still some work waiting for me."

He glanced toward Ein.

"And I promised I'd take her home."

Ein smiled.

Uranus regarded the two of them.

Then nodded.

"Very well."

Bell approached.

"Thank you for letting us visit."

"You need not thank me for that."

Uranus's expression softened.

"You are always welcome here."

Bell smiled.

"Then I'll visit again."

"I shall look forward to it."

Bell inclined his head.

Then, after a moment, added:

"And I'll bring another present next time."

Uranus chuckled.

"I believe you have already given me quite enough trouble."

"That's not a no."

"Indeed."

Bell laughed.

Ein gave Uranus a respectful farewell.

Then the two departed.

***

The journey back was quiet.

Ein walked beside Bell, holding his hand.

For a while, neither spoke.

Eventually, she looked toward him.

"Bell?"

"Hm?"

"Do you think Fels will be all right?"

Bell considered the question.

Then smiled.

"I think they'll be fine."

He glanced toward the distant surroundings.

"Although I suspect they'll be rather busy."

Ein giggled.

"With research?"

"Probably."

Bell looked down at their joined hands.

"Eight hundred years is a long time to lose."

He paused.

"But perhaps that's exactly why they won't waste any more of it."

Ein gently squeezed his hand.

Bell returned the gesture.

For a brief moment, he remembered the man standing before the mirror.

The tears.

The smile.

The words.

*I can cry.*

Bell smiled faintly.

He hadn't expected the restoration to be quite so emotional.

But perhaps that was the point.

A body wasn't simply a collection of organs and tissues.

It was the means through which someone experienced the world.

And sometimes, restoring it meant giving someone back the ability to experience things they had long forgotten.

He looked toward Ein.

"Well, shall we go home?"

She smiled.

"Yes."

Bell extended his hand.

The surrounding space began to distort.

And the two disappeared.

***

Hearth Manor.

The familiar surroundings welcomed them.

Bell stepped forward, followed by Ein.

He glanced around.

Then exhaled.

"Home."

Ein smiled.

"Home."

She moved closer and gently rested her head against his shoulder.

Bell didn't object.

Instead, he placed an arm around her.

For the moment, there were no experiments to conduct.

No complicated magical formations to prepare.

No mysteries demanding immediate answers.

Only the quiet satisfaction of having fulfilled a promise.

He had visited his grandfather.

He had introduced Ein.

And he had given someone back something they had lost eight centuries ago.

Bell smiled.

Not a bad day.

Not bad at all.

Somewhere beneath the ancient altar, a laboratory had once again become occupied.

And within it, a scientist was preparing to begin his work.

His name was Senku Ishigami.

And he had approximately eight hundred years of lost research to catch up on.

**End of Chapter Seventy-Eight.**
