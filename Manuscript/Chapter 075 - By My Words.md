### DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

### Chapter Seventy-Five — By My Words

Some things could be understood through observation. Others required
experimentation. And there were things that could only be accomplished by giving one's
words meaning.

### I. The Root of the Problem

Bell had always believed that identifying a problem was considerably more important
than immediately attempting to solve it.

It was an approach that had saved him from unnecessary complications more times
than he cared to admit.

After all, a solution was only as good as one's understanding of the problem.

And if the problem itself had been misunderstood, then even the most sophisticated
solution could become another source of trouble.

Unfortunately, his current situation was considerably more complicated than he would
have preferred.

Bell stood before the examination chamber.

Behind the transparent barrier, Ein remained motionless.

The suspended animation formation continued operating, maintaining her bodily
functions while preventing further deterioration. Several layers of magical formations
surrounded the chamber, each performing a particular function.

Everything was stable.

At least, according to the instruments.

Bell glanced at the readings before returning his attention to the documents.

"All right..."

He pulled a chair closer.

"Let's narrow this down."

He had already received permission from Uranus.

That particular matter was settled.

The remaining question was how to employ the authorization without making an
irreversible mistake.

He opened his notebook.

The first problem concerned the nature of the corruption.

During Test One, he had successfully extracted a substantial portion of the foreign
material from Ein's body.

However, the results had revealed something considerably more troubling.

The corruption wasn't simply an external contaminant.

It behaved like an artificial network.

It possessed connections, distributed itself throughout its host, and demonstrated an
extraordinary capacity to sustain its own structure.

Even after being separated from Ein, the extracted material remained active.

That alone was unusual.

Ordinary magical contamination didn't necessarily behave that way.

But there was something else.

Bell retrieved one of the crystalline containers.

Inside it, a dark substance shifted slightly.

He observed it for several seconds.

Then he adjusted the mana circulating around the container.

The substance responded.

Not aggressively.

Not defensively.

It simply reacted to the presence of the surrounding energy.

Bell frowned.

"You're not independent."

He examined the readings.

The corruption appeared self-sustaining, but that description wasn't entirely accurate.

There was a difference between something that could sustain itself and something that
possessed an independent source of energy.

The distinction was important.

He increased the observation interval.

Then compared the results against the previous measurements.

A pattern began to emerge.

The material consumed energy continuously.

Not an extraordinary quantity at every individual moment, but enough to become
significant over an extended period.

And the larger the structure became, the greater its requirements.

Bell leaned forward.

So that's how it works.

He opened another document.

The notes concerning Ein's condition were considerably more extensive.

Her body had been transformed.

Her magical pathways had been altered.

And the corruption had established connections throughout her biological structure.

Yet, despite the apparent complexity, the network was surprisingly inefficient.

It required constant maintenance.

Bell tapped his pen against the paper.

Why would something with this level of sophistication possess such an enormous
maintenance requirement?

He considered several possibilities.

A naturally occurring parasite?

Unlikely.

An artificial magical construct?

Possible, although its properties were inconsistent with ordinary magical engineering.

A curse?

That would explain certain aspects, but not its behavior after extraction.

He returned to the original observations.

The corruption had not simply appeared inside Ein.

It had developed around something that already existed.

Something that had been altered.

Something that had undergone a fundamental change.

Bell stopped writing.

His eyes gradually narrowed.

"Wait."

He looked toward the examination chamber.

Then toward the crystalline container.

And finally, toward the documents concerning the Dungeon.

For several seconds, he remained silent.

A possibility had emerged.

One that he hadn't properly considered before.

What if the corruption isn't the original problem?

He slowly turned another page.

What if it's the consequence?

### II. The Dungeon's Territory

Bell retrieved several older observations.

He had encountered numerous unusual phenomena during his previous investigations.

The Dungeon's behavior.

Its monsters.

The irregularities associated with its environment.

And, most importantly, the relationship between the Dungeon and the existence of living
creatures within its territory.

He had initially approached Ein's condition as a medical problem.

Understandable, considering that she was the person he wanted to save.

But the more he examined the corruption, the less it resembled an ordinary biological
condition.

There was an external influence.

Something that had changed the original state of its host.

Bell began arranging the documents across the table.

One by one, he compared the observations.

The corrupted material.

Its energy requirements.

Its reaction to magical interference.

Its unusual persistence.

And the most important characteristic of all.

Its relationship with death.

Bell's expression gradually changed.

Of course.

He leaned back.

The Dungeon wasn't merely a place where monsters lived.

It was an environment possessing its own peculiar characteristics, its own ecosystem,
and its own relationship with the creatures that existed within it.

But there was a distinction he had overlooked.

What happened to those who died there?

Not adventurers who escaped with injuries.

Not monsters that dissolved into magic stones.

But individuals whose lives ended while they remained within the Dungeon's territory.

Bell stared at the documents.

The possibility was disturbing.

And the more he examined it, the more difficult it became to dismiss.

The Dungeon could corrupt those who died within its domain.

Not merely consume their remains.

Not merely leave behind some lingering magical contamination.

It could alter what remained of a deceased individual and turn that existence into
something fundamentally different.

His eyes moved toward the examination chamber.

Ein.

The corruption.

The artificial network.

The strange relationship between her current condition and the remnants of what she
had once been.

A cold realization settled over him.

So that's what happened.

The Dungeon wasn't necessarily creating an entirely new creature from nothing.

It could take something that had already existed, something that had already belonged
to the world, and corrupt it.

The implications were horrifying.

Death within the Dungeon didn't necessarily mean that the deceased would simply
become part of the past.

There was another possibility.

Their remains could become the foundation of something else.

Something that no longer followed the rules of ordinary life.

Bell closed his eyes briefly.

He could imagine the consequences.

Adventurers died in the Dungeon every day.

Some were recovered.

Others weren't.

And if his interpretation was correct, the Dungeon possessed a means of exploiting
those deaths.

It was an unpleasant thought.

But there was another observation worth considering.

He examined the energy readings again.

The maintenance requirements.

The continuous consumption.

The amount of energy necessary to sustain a single corrupted individual.

Bell began making calculations.

Then stopped.

His expression became considerably more relaxed.

"...Ah."

He leaned against the chair.

"So that's the limitation."

The Dungeon couldn't simply produce an unlimited number of these creatures.

At least, not without consequences.

Maintaining even one corrupted existence appeared to demand an extraordinary
amount of energy.

The process was expensive.

Not necessarily in terms of the initial transformation, but in terms of sustaining the
resulting structure.

If the Dungeon attempted to maintain several such creatures simultaneously, the
cumulative expenditure would become enormous.

It wasn't impossible.

But it certainly wasn't something that could be repeated carelessly.

Bell wrote a few additional observations.

Dungeon Corruption — Preliminary Findings

* The Dungeon possesses the ability to corrupt individuals who die within its territory.

* The resulting corruption can fundamentally alter the deceased's original condition.

* The process appears to require substantial energy to maintain.

* Sustaining even one corrupted individual places a considerable demand on the
Dungeon.

* The ability is therefore unlikely to be something that can be deployed indiscriminately
or continuously.

He examined the list.

There were still unanswered questions.

How exactly did the Dungeon accomplish this?

Was there a particular mechanism responsible?

Could the corruption spread independently?

Were there different degrees of transformation?

And did every deceased individual possess the same susceptibility?

He didn't know.

Not yet.

He wasn't going to pretend that a handful of observations constituted a complete
explanation.

Nevertheless, he had identified something important.

The Dungeon was involved.

And that was enough for now.

Bell closed the notebook.

He looked toward Ein.

"...Well."

A faint sigh escaped his lips.

"That's a rather unpleasant discovery."

He stood.

But then he shook his head.

No.

Not now.

Whatever the Dungeon was doing, whatever its motivations or limitations might be,
none of that changed the immediate objective.

He still had someone to save.

And he had already spent enough time investigating the problem.

Bell approached the examination chamber.

"Sorry, Ein."

He placed a hand against the transparent surface.

"I'll have to investigate the rest later."

His expression softened.

"For now, let's get you out of there."

### III. Preparations

Bell returned to the center of the laboratory.

The next procedure would be different from the previous experiments.

Test One had involved extracting the corruption through conventional methods.

Test Two would employ something considerably more fundamental.

Arcanum.

He looked toward the ceiling.

"Grandfather, I hope you don't regret giving me permission."

He chuckled quietly.

Then began preparing the room.

The first step was establishing the necessary boundaries.

He couldn't afford to allow the purification effect to spread beyond its intended target.

Ein's body was already in a precarious condition.

The corruption had become integrated with her biological structures, particularly her
heart and brain.

A careless application could damage the very things he was attempting to preserve.

He retrieved several prepared materials.

Then began inscribing the first symbols onto the floor.

They were unfamiliar characters.

Not the conventional magical notation used in ordinary spell construction.

Their shapes were irregular, composed of intersecting lines and symbols that seemed to
follow a different system entirely.

Bell continued working.

One inscription became two.

Two became four.

And gradually, the entire chamber began acquiring an intricate arrangement of
markings.

He extended the formation toward the walls.

The symbols connected.

Their individual structures formed a larger pattern.

It wasn't simply a magic circle.

It was a framework.

A means of defining the boundaries of the intended effect.

Bell stepped backward.

He examined the arrangement.

The inscriptions were strange.

Even to someone accustomed to dealing with unusual magical phenomena, their
structure was difficult to associate with conventional magic.

But that was precisely the point.

This wasn't a spell that relied on ordinary magical construction.

It was something else.

Something that required the user to establish the intended effect through a different
medium.

Words.

Meaning.

And authority.

Bell examined the final inscription.

Then returned to the workbench.

He checked the monitoring instruments.

The suspended animation formation remained stable.

The protective formations were operational.

The surrounding mana circulation was within acceptable parameters.

He examined the readings again.

Then once more.

Satisfied, he approached the examination chamber.

"All right."

He placed his hand against the surface.

"Let's begin."

### IV. By My Words

Bell closed his eyes.

For a moment, nothing happened.

The laboratory remained quiet.

The magical instruments continued their steady operation.

The inscriptions on the walls and floor remained dormant.

Then something changed.

Bell's breathing slowed.

His posture became unnaturally still.

The atmosphere around him shifted.

It wasn't the familiar sensation of mana gathering.

Nor was it the pressure produced by an ordinary spell.

Something considerably more profound had begun to emerge.

His hair gradually changed color.

The familiar white strands lost their brightness, becoming pale.

Then lighter.

Until they settled into a soft, almost luminous shade of grey.

Bell opened his mouth.

And spoke.

The language that emerged was not one commonly heard in the mortal world.

It possessed an ancient rhythm.

Its sounds were unfamiliar, its structure unlike the languages used by the people of
Orario.

Yet the words carried a strange sense of authority.

They were not merely spoken.

They were declared.

The first line echoed throughout the laboratory.

" 𒂊 𒅗 𒋾𒆷𒀀𒈾 𒂷𒂊 𒈨𒌍"

The inscriptions began glowing.

Bell continued.

"𒂗 𒌓 𒌋 𒆬𒌓 𒌀 𒅆𒁉𒋫 𒅇𒌅𒀀 𒉆𒆬𒌓 𒈬𒌦𒁺𒀀 𒉆𒂟 𒈬𒌦𒁶𒈠 𒌋 𒄯𒊏𒀀                  𒆳 𒆲𒈾"

The light spread.

It traveled across the floor, following the inscriptions and connecting each symbol to the
next.

The walls responded.

The entire room had become part of a single formation.

Bell's voice remained steady.

"𒅗𒂷𒋫 𒇽 𒌀𒆷 𒉌𒄭𒀀 𒉡𒈬𒌦𒋢𒌒 𒉡𒈬𒌦𒄩𒇴"

The air trembled.

The crystalline containers began vibrating.

The dark material within them reacted violently.

For the first time since its extraction, the corruption displayed something resembling
resistance.

Bell continued speaking.

His expression remained unchanged.

"𒉆𒈨𒌍 𒆬𒂵"

The final words echoed.

Then silence.

For one brief instant, the entire laboratory became completely still.

And then—

Light.

An overwhelming brilliance erupted throughout the chamber.

The inscriptions illuminated simultaneously, their combined radiance becoming so
intense that the walls, floor, and examination chamber disappeared behind a blinding
white glow.

The instruments registered an extraordinary fluctuation.

The crystalline containers shook.

The dark material inside them began disintegrating.

Not exploding.

Not burning.

Simply losing the structure that had allowed it to exist.

The remaining corruption within Ein responded in the same manner.

Its connections began disappearing.

One after another.

The artificial network that had established itself throughout her body was being
dismantled.

And yet, the surrounding biological structures remained untouched.

The corruption was being separated.

Not by physically removing it.

Not by destroying the body that sustained it.

But by returning the foreign material to a state in which it could no longer maintain its
existence.

The light intensified.

Then, gradually—

It faded.

The inscriptions dimmed.

The trembling stopped.

And the laboratory returned to silence.

Bell remained standing.

His hair was still pale grey.

His eyes remained closed.

For several seconds, he did not move.

Then he exhaled.

The strange state gradually receded.

His hair returned to its familiar white.

He opened his eyes.

And immediately looked toward the examination chamber.

"Readings."

He approached the instruments.

His eyes moved rapidly across the displayed information.

The first set.

Stable.

The second.

Stable.

The third—

Bell stopped.

He leaned closer.

"...No way."

He checked again.

Then another instrument.

Then another.

The corruption readings had disappeared.

Not diminished.

Not reduced.

Gone.

Every remaining concentration had been neutralized.

Bell stared at the results.

For several seconds, he simply stood there.

Then a smile slowly appeared on his face.

"It worked."

He looked toward Ein.

"It actually worked."

His expression softened.

But he didn't celebrate just yet.

There was still one thing to do.

He approached the control formation.

The suspended animation was no longer necessary.

He examined the readings one final time.

Her bodily functions were stable.

The corruption had been eliminated.

The surrounding magical structures remained intact.

And the support formations were no longer required to preserve her condition.

Bell placed his hand against the control inscription.

"All right, Ein."

He smiled.

"Welcome back."

He deactivated the suspended animation.

### V. The First Breath

The formation gradually disappeared.

The faint magical glow surrounding the examination chamber faded.

The monitoring instruments continued operating, recording the gradual return of normal
bodily functions.

Bell watched carefully.

There was no immediate movement.

No sudden reaction.

Only silence.

Then—

A finger moved.

Bell straightened.

Another movement followed.

Ein's hand shifted slightly against the surface.

Her breathing changed.

It was subtle at first.

Then more noticeable.

Bell approached.

"Ein?"

Her eyelids trembled.

For a moment, she seemed unable to open them.

Then, slowly, they lifted.

Light entered her vision.

Blurred shapes.

Unfamiliar surroundings.

And a familiar face.

She blinked.

Once.

Twice.

Her eyes gradually adjusted.

"...Bell?"

His expression brightened.

"Hey."

He leaned closer.

"How are you feeling?"

Ein stared at him.

She seemed confused.

Her eyes moved around the room.

Then returned to him.

"...What happened?"

Bell chuckled softly.

"That's a rather long explanation."

He examined her readings.

"But the short version is that the purification worked."

Ein looked at him.

"Purification?"

"Yes."

He smiled.

"The corruption is gone."

For several seconds, she remained silent.

Then she slowly raised her hand.

Her fingers moved.

She looked at them as though she were seeing them for the first time.

Then she touched her face.

Her cheeks.

Her forehead.

Her hair.

She drew a breath.

And another.

Her expression gradually changed.

Something was different.

Something that she had almost forgotten.

There was no constant pressure.

No invisible restraint.

No unfamiliar presence lurking beneath her thoughts.

No sensation of being held together by something that did not belong to her.

She could feel herself.

Completely.

Her body.

Her breathing.

Her heartbeat.

Her thoughts.

Everything.

Ein's eyes widened.

"...Ah."

Bell tilted his head.

"What's wrong?"

She didn't answer immediately.

Instead, she slowly sat up.

Bell moved closer, ready to support her if necessary.

But she didn't collapse.

Her movements were steady.

She looked at her hands again.

Then toward the surrounding room.

And finally at Bell.

A small smile appeared.

"Bell..."

"Yes?"

"I can move."

He blinked.

"Of course you can."

"No."

Her voice trembled.

"I mean..."

She looked down.

Then back at him.

"I can move without feeling something holding me back."

Bell's expression softened.

Ein slowly brought a hand to her chest.

Her heartbeat was steady.

Normal.

And for the first time in what felt like an eternity, she could feel it without the strange
interference that had accompanied her existence.

She inhaled.

The air entered her lungs.

It was such an ordinary sensation.

And yet, it felt extraordinary.

She closed her eyes.

A breath escaped her.

Then another.

"...I'm free."

The words were barely audible.

Bell didn't immediately respond.

He understood.

Or at least, he believed he understood enough.

For someone who had spent so long trapped within an existence that was no longer
entirely her own, the absence of that restraint must have been overwhelming.

He offered her a hand.

"Can you stand?"

Ein looked at it.

Then took it.

"Let's find out."

He helped her rise.

Her legs trembled slightly.

Bell immediately steadied her.

"Easy."

"I'm fine."

"That's not particularly convincing."

She looked at him.

Then smiled.

"...You always say that."

"Because you keep saying you're fine when you're clearly not."

Ein laughed.

It was quiet.

A little unsteady.

But genuine.

Bell found himself smiling.

It was a sound he hadn't realized he had been waiting to hear.

### VI. Something Worth Returning To

The two remained standing beside the examination chamber.

Bell had already checked the readings several times.

Ein was stable.

There were no indications of the previous corruption.

Her biological functions were operating normally, and the remaining traces of foreign
magical interference had disappeared.

There would still be some recovery required.

He wasn't going to pretend that a single purification procedure could erase every
possible consequence of what she had endured.

But the immediate danger was over.

And that was enough.

For now.

Bell pulled a chair closer.

"You should sit down for a while."

Ein looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because you've been suspended for a considerable amount of time."

She blinked.

"...How long?"

"Long enough."

"That's not an answer."

"I know."

He smiled.

"Unfortunately, I don't think you'll appreciate the exact number."

Ein gave him a small, disapproving look.

Bell laughed.

"All right. I'll explain everything."

He sat down.

Then began recounting what had happened.

The first examination.

The discovery that the corruption was integrated into her body.

The extraction procedure.

The difficulties involving her heart and brain.

The alternative methods he had considered.

And finally, his journey to seek Uranus's permission to use Arcanum.

Ein listened quietly.

Occasionally, she asked questions.

Sometimes, she simply stared at him.

When he finished explaining the purification itself, she remained silent for a while.

Then she looked down.

"...You did all of that?"

Bell tilted his head.

"Well, I had some help from my own research."

"But you were the one who did it."

He smiled.

"Yes."

Ein lowered her eyes.

Her fingers slowly tightened around one another.

"I don't know what to say."

"You don't have to say anything."

"But—"

"Ein."

She looked up.

Bell's expression was gentle.

"I'm glad you're awake."

That was all.

No elaborate explanation.

No request for gratitude.

No expectation that she should repay him.

Just a simple statement.

And somehow, it made her chest tighten.

She looked at him.

Really looked at him.

At the person who had spent so much time trying to understand something that even
she could barely explain.

Someone who had treated her as a person even when her condition had made that
difficult.

Someone who had refused to abandon her.

Someone who had given her the opportunity to return.

"...Bell."

"Hm?"

"Thank you."

He smiled.

"You're welcome."

She looked away.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Ein slowly shifted closer.

Bell noticed.

But he didn't move away.

She looked at his face.

Her expression was difficult to read.

There was gratitude.

Relief.

Affection.

And something else that she had been unable to properly acknowledge while the
corruption remained.

Something that had existed beneath the fear and uncertainty.

Something that had gradually become impossible to ignore.

"Bell?"

"Yes?"

"Can I ask you something?"

"Of course."

She hesitated.

Then smiled faintly.

"Would you mind if I did something?"

Bell blinked.

"That depends."

"On what?"

"On what you're planning to do."

Ein laughed.

A small sound.

Then she leaned forward.

And kissed him.

Bell's eyes widened slightly.

For an instant, he remained perfectly still.

He hadn't expected it.

Not from her.

Not at that particular moment.

But he didn't pull away.

Instead, after the briefest hesitation, he returned the kiss.

His hand moved gently toward her shoulder.

Ein's fingers rested against his chest.

There was no urgency in the gesture.

No desperation.

It was gentle.

Almost hesitant.

As though both of them were trying to understand the meaning of something that had
been waiting for them to acknowledge it.

When they finally separated, Ein remained close.

Her eyes were lowered.

Her cheeks had acquired a faint color.

Bell looked at her.

Then smiled.

"...Well."

Ein blinked.

"What?"

"I wasn't expecting that."

She looked away.

"I'm sorry."

"Why?"

"Because I—"

"Ein."

He gently interrupted her.

She looked back.

Bell's smile softened.

"You don't have to apologize."

For several seconds, she simply stared at him.

Then her expression changed.

"...You didn't refuse."

Bell scratched his cheek.

"Would you have preferred that?"

"No!"

Her response came much faster than expected.

Bell laughed.

Ein covered her face with both hands.

"Oh..."

"What's wrong?"

"Nothing."

"You're hiding your face."

"I'm aware."

He chuckled.

For a moment, they simply sat together.

Then Ein slowly lowered her hands.

"Bell."

"Hm?"

"Can I ask another question?"

"Go ahead."

She looked at him.

"Was that..."

She hesitated.

"...all right?"

Bell considered the question.

Then gently took her hand.

"Yes."

Her eyes widened slightly.

He smiled.

"More than all right."

Ein lowered her gaze.

Her fingers slowly intertwined with his.

There was something almost unfamiliar about the gesture.

Not because she had never experienced affection.

But because this time, there was no uncertainty about whether she was acting
according to her own wishes.

No foreign influence.

No invisible restraint.

No voice telling her what she should feel.

It was her decision.

Her feelings.

Her choice.

She looked at Bell again.

And smiled.

A genuine, unrestrained smile.

Bell returned it.

This is for the best, I guess.

He glanced toward the now-empty examination chamber.

The experiment had succeeded.

The corruption had been eliminated.

And Ein had finally awakened.

There were still questions to investigate.

The Dungeon.

Its ability to corrupt the deceased.

The nature of the artificial network.

And the implications of everything he had discovered.

But those matters could wait.

For the moment, Bell allowed himself to enjoy the quiet.

Ein leaned against his shoulder.

He didn't object.

Instead, he gently rested his head against hers.

And for once, he didn't feel the need to calculate what came next.

Some things could wait.

Some things deserved to be experienced rather than immediately understood.

And some words, once spoken, could become promises.

Bell closed his eyes.

The laboratory was quiet.

But it no longer felt empty.

Perhaps that was enough.



### VII. A Promise Without Restraint

The laboratory gradually returned to its ordinary atmosphere.

Bell had already begun putting away the instruments.

There was no longer any reason to keep the examination chamber operational,
although he intended to preserve the records for future reference.

Ein sat nearby.

She had been watching him for several minutes.

Not because there was anything particularly interesting about his work.

Rather, she simply wanted to look at him.

Bell eventually noticed.

"Something on my face?"

Ein shook her head.

"No."

"Then why are you staring?"

"Am I not allowed to?"

He paused.

Then smiled.

"I didn't say that."

She giggled.

It was an unfamiliar sensation, being able to laugh without immediately wondering
whether something inside her would interfere.

She looked at her hands.

Then at Bell.

"Can I tell you something?"

"Of course."

"I was afraid."

Bell stopped arranging the instruments.

Ein continued.

"Even before you began the purification, I was afraid. I knew you were trying to help me,
and I trusted you, but..."

Her expression softened.

"I was afraid that when I woke up, something would still be wrong."

Bell approached her.

"And now?"

She looked at him.

"Now I don't feel anything holding me back."

He smiled.

"That's good."

"Very good."

She reached for his hand.

Bell allowed her fingers to intertwine with his.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Ein leaned closer.

"Bell."

"Hm?"

"I missed you."

His expression softened.

"I was right here."

"I know."

She smiled.

"But I couldn't answer you."

Bell remembered the occasions when he had spoken to her while she remained
suspended.

The explanations.

The promises.

The occasional conversations he knew she couldn't hear.

He hadn't expected an answer.

But he had spoken anyway.

"I know," he replied.

Ein looked down at their joined hands.

"Thank you for coming back to me."

Bell gently squeezed her hand.

"You don't have to thank me for that."

"But I want to."

She raised her eyes.

"And I want to thank you properly."

Before he could ask what she meant, she leaned forward and kissed him.

This time, there was no hesitation.

Bell returned the kiss naturally, his hand moving gently to her cheek.

It was familiar.

Yet somehow different.

There was no longer a hidden burden between them, no uncertainty about whether the
corruption might interfere with her feelings or actions.

It was simply Ein.

His girlfriend.

The person he had wanted to save.

When they separated, she remained close.

Her cheeks were slightly flushed.

Bell smiled.

"Feeling better?"

Ein gave him an amused look.

"Much better."

"Good."

She rested her forehead against his.

"You're smiling."

"Am I not allowed to?"

She laughed.

"You're using my words against me."

"Perhaps."

Ein shook her head.

Then, after a moment, she wrapped her arms around him.

Bell returned the embrace.

They remained that way for a while.

There were no experiments to conduct.

No calculations to complete.

No immediate problems demanding his attention.

Just two people holding each other.

And perhaps that was precisely what they needed.

Eventually, Ein spoke.

"Bell?"

"Yes?"

"Can we stay like this for a little longer?"

He glanced at the laboratory.

Then at her.

"Of course."

She smiled.

"Thank you."

Bell gently rested his chin against her head.

This is for the best, I guess.

He looked toward the examination chamber.

The place that had held her suspended for so long was now empty.

The corruption was gone.

The experiment was complete.

And the person he had been trying to save was finally standing beside him.

There were still countless things he needed to investigate.

The Dungeon.

Its strange ability to corrupt the deceased.

The nature of the artificial network.

And the possibility that other individuals had suffered the same fate.

But those matters could wait.

For now, Bell allowed himself to enjoy the quiet.

Ein leaned against him.

He didn't object.

Instead, he gently held her closer.

And for once, he didn't feel the need to calculate what came next.

Some things could wait.

Some things deserved to be experienced rather than immediately understood.

And some words, once spoken, could become promises.

Bell closed his eyes.

The laboratory was quiet.
