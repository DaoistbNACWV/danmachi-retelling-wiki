# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

## Chapter Eighty-Five — Information

The break, it seemed, had become considerably more crowded.

Bell didn't particularly mind.

If anything, he found the sudden company rather amusing. Three members of the Loki Familia had stumbled upon their little campsite, and judging by their expressions, none of them had expected to find him in Melen.

Especially not with Filvis.

The elf, meanwhile, had gradually recovered from the initial surprise. She remained beside Bell, though she had adjusted her position enough to sit comfortably rather than hide against his chest.

Alicia had already made herself comfortable.

Ais was sitting quietly, occasionally glancing between Bell and Filvis.

Lefiya, on the other hand, was still processing the situation.

It wasn't that she disliked Filvis.

At least, that wasn't the problem.

The problem was that Bell appeared entirely comfortable with her.

And that was considerably more difficult to accept than Lefiya would have liked.

Bell placed a cup on the table.

"Here."

"Thank you," Alicia replied.

He handed another to Ais.

Then another to Lefiya.

"Thank you, Bell."

"You're welcome."

Filvis watched the exchange before looking at the remaining refreshments.

"Would you like something as well, Bell?"

"I'm fine. I'll have some later."

"All right."

The conversation settled into a brief silence.

Outside, the waves continued their steady rhythm against the shoreline. The afternoon breeze passed through the surrounding vegetation, carrying the faint smell of saltwater into the campsite.

It was peaceful.

Almost suspiciously so.

Bell looked at the three visitors.

"By the way, what brings you here?"

Ais raised her head.

"Hm?"

"To Melen. I wasn't expecting to encounter the Loki Familia here."

"Oh."

Alicia glanced at Ais, then at Lefiya.

It was a reasonable question.

They hadn't exactly announced their arrival to him, and their presence in Melen wasn't something particularly secret.

Still, the three exchanged a look.

Ais eventually answered.

"We came with the expedition."

"The expedition?"

"Yes. We were investigating something."

"Something?"

Lefiya placed her cup down.

"Well, it's not exactly something we can explain in a single sentence."

"Then use two."

"That's not what I meant!"

Alicia laughed.

"Bell, you're making her nervous."

"Am I?"

"Yes."

He looked at Lefiya.

"Sorry."

Lefiya looked away.

It was difficult to remain annoyed when he apologized so casually.

Ais, however, had already begun explaining.

"We came to inspect the Leviathan seal."

Bell's expression changed slightly.

Not dramatically.

Just enough for Filvis to notice.

"The Leviathan seal?"

Ais nodded.

"It was a precautionary inspection. We wanted to make sure nothing had happened to it."

"Was something wrong?"

"No. Riveria said it was intact."

"That's good."

Bell nodded.

He didn't ask immediately why the Loki Familia had considered the inspection necessary. There was probably a reason behind it, and he wasn't particularly interested in questioning something that had already been explained.

Instead, he looked toward the sea.

"How long are you staying?"

"Until we leave," Alicia answered.

"That sounds pleasant."

"It would be," Lefiya muttered.

Bell glanced at her.

"Would be?"

"Nothing."

Alicia smiled.

"She's just a little tired."

"I see."

Bell accepted the explanation without further questioning.

Then he looked at the three of them again.

"Well, since we're exchanging information, I suppose I should tell you something."

Filvis turned toward him.

She already knew where this was going.

Bell reached for the cup beside him.

"Filvis and I are investigating something ourselves."

Ais blinked.

"Investigating?"

"Yes."

"What?"

"A suspicious cargo shipment."

The three girls looked at him.

Bell continued.

"More specifically, we're looking into reports of illegal monster transportation around Melen."

Silence.

A particularly noticeable silence.

Alicia slowly lowered her cup.

Lefiya stopped moving.

Even Ais, whose expression rarely changed significantly, appeared to become more attentive.

"...Monster transportation?" Alicia repeated.

Bell nodded.

"That's right."

"Illegal transportation?"

"Yes."

"Of monsters?"

Bell looked at her.

"That's generally what the word means."

"Bell!"

He smiled faintly.

"Sorry."

But the smile didn't last particularly long.

He placed his cup on the table.

"There's reason to believe that someone is moving monsters through Melen. The circumstances are suspicious, and the cargo itself may be connected to something larger."

Lefiya leaned forward.

"Wait. Are you saying someone is transporting monsters outside the Dungeon?"

"That's what we're investigating."

"Why would anyone do that?"

"That's a good question."

Bell didn't immediately elaborate.

He allowed the question to remain unanswered for a moment.

Not because he was trying to create suspense.

Rather, he wanted to see how they would react.

Ais looked at Filvis.

"Do you know about this?"

Filvis nodded.

"Yes. Bell and I have been looking into it."

"And you haven't found the people responsible?"

"Not yet."

Alicia frowned.

"That sounds rather serious."

"It could be," Bell replied.

"Could be?"

"We have suspicions. We don't have confirmation of everything yet."

That distinction mattered.

An investigation was not the same as a conclusion.

He wasn't going to tell them that someone was definitely transporting monsters for a particular purpose when he hadn't established the actual circumstances.

Lefiya looked between Bell and Filvis.

"How long have you been investigating?"

"Long enough to know that the situation isn't particularly straightforward."

"That's vague."

"Because I don't have a complete answer."

Bell shrugged.

"I'd rather tell you that than pretend I know something I don't."

Alicia nodded thoughtfully.

It was an answer she could appreciate.

But Ais was still looking at him.

"Bell."

"Hm?"

"Can you help us?"

Bell paused.

He looked at her.

Then at Alicia.

Then at Lefiya.

"...With what?"

Lefiya opened her mouth.

Alicia spoke first.

"Perhaps we should explain our situation before asking him to help."

"That's probably a good idea," Bell agreed.

And so the conversation took a different direction.

---

## An Unsuccessful Investigation

Alicia placed her cup on the table.

"To be honest, we've been having some difficulties."

Bell listened.

"The inspection of the Leviathan seal went smoothly. That part was relatively simple. However, there was another matter that we were supposed to investigate."

"The monsters?"

Alicia nodded.

"Yes."

Lefiya crossed her arms.

"We've been looking into the insect-type monsters appearing around the area. Unfortunately, we haven't made much progress."

"How much?"

"Almost none."

Bell raised an eyebrow.

"Almost?"

"That's practically the same as none!"

Ais looked toward Lefiya.

"She's right."

"Thank you, Ais."

Lefiya sighed.

"We've searched the surrounding areas. We've questioned people where appropriate, checked the places we could access, and tried to determine whether there was any connection between the incidents."

"And?"

"Nothing."

Alicia continued.

"We haven't found a reliable trail. There are no clear indications of where the monsters are coming from, where they're being kept, or who might be responsible."

Bell rested his elbow against the table.

"How many locations have you checked?"

"Several," Alicia replied.

"Several?"

"Enough to make the lack of results frustrating."

He nodded.

That was understandable.

Melen wasn't a small settlement.

Its surrounding areas, coastline, settlements, roads, and less frequently visited locations offered plenty of places for someone to conceal something.

Searching without a reliable starting point was a terrible way to spend time.

Especially when the target was something that could be transported.

"Have you found anything unusual?"

Lefiya shook her head.

"Nothing substantial."

"Even traces?"

"Not enough to establish anything."

Bell looked at Filvis.

She had been listening quietly.

There was a reason she hadn't interrupted.

She knew that Bell was already considering the implications.

He turned back toward the three girls.

"Then I suppose we're looking at the same problem from different directions."

Ais tilted her head.

"Different directions?"

"You're investigating the monsters. We're investigating suspicious cargo."

He tapped the table lightly.

"If the reports we're following are connected, then there's a reasonable possibility that our investigations overlap."

Alicia's expression brightened.

"That's what we were thinking."

Lefiya nodded.

"Actually, that's why we wanted to ask you."

Bell looked at her.

"Me?"

"Yes."

She hesitated.

Then continued.

"We've been trying to find a proper lead, but we haven't been successful. If you're already investigating suspicious cargo, perhaps you could help us identify where the monsters are coming from."

Bell remained silent for a moment.

He looked at the three of them.

Then at Filvis.

There was something rather amusing about the situation.

They had spent time searching for information without finding anything substantial.

Meanwhile, he and Filvis had been investigating a different matter that might connect to theirs.

The coincidence was convenient.

Perhaps too convenient.

But convenience wasn't necessarily suspicious.

"Let me understand something," Bell said.

The girls turned toward him.

"You've been investigating the insect-type monsters, but you haven't found their origin, their transportation route, or the people involved."

"That's correct," Alicia answered.

"And you haven't found a reliable explanation for why they're appearing?"

Ais shook her head.

"No."

Bell nodded.

"All right."

He leaned back.

"Then tell me what you know about them."

Filvis finally spoke.

"I can explain that."

Bell turned toward her.

"Please."

She nodded.

---

## The Insect-Type Monsters

Filvis adjusted her posture.

Unlike Bell, who was content to approach a problem through questions and possibilities, she preferred to establish the available information first.

It was a habit Bell appreciated.

"One of the monsters we're concerned about is called a Viola."

Lefiya nodded.

"That's one of them."

Filvis continued.

"There are also Poison Vargas and two other insect-type monsters involved in the reports."

Bell's eyes narrowed slightly.

"Four types?"

"Yes."

"Are they appearing together?"

"Not consistently. The reports aren't sufficiently detailed to establish a reliable pattern."

That was already useful.

Bell glanced at Alicia.

"Are these sightings confirmed?"

"Some are based on reports we've received. Others are things we've personally encountered or verified."

"So there's a difference in reliability."

"Yes."

Bell nodded.

"Good. Keep those separate."

Lefiya looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because an unverified report shouldn't carry the same weight as something you've actually witnessed."

He rested his hands on the table.

"Otherwise, we might end up building an entire investigation around something that never happened."

Lefiya considered that.

Then nodded reluctantly.

Filvis continued.

"The Violas are the primary concern among the reports we've been examining. The Poison Vargas are another matter, although their presence is also significant."

Bell listened without interrupting.

He didn't need to know every detail immediately.

What mattered first was establishing the scope.

Four different monster types.

A number of reports.

An investigation that had produced almost nothing.

And a separate suspicion involving illegal transportation.

There were several possibilities.

They could be unrelated incidents.

Someone might be deliberately releasing monsters.

The monsters could be moving naturally from somewhere else.

Or there could be an organized operation transporting them.

The problem was that none of those possibilities had been established.

He didn't particularly enjoy working with so many unknown variables.

But that was precisely why he needed more information.

"How many sightings?"

Alicia shook her head.

"We don't have an accurate number."

"Locations?"

"Several scattered locations."

"Times?"

"Not consistent enough to establish a schedule."

Bell slowly nodded.

"Then we're missing the three things that matter most."

Lefiya frowned.

"Which are?"

"Where they appear, when they appear, and how they get there."

He raised three fingers.

"If we can establish those, we might be able to determine whether there's a pattern."

Ais looked at him.

"Can you find them?"

Bell glanced toward the surrounding area.

"Possibly."

"How?"

He smiled.

"That's the interesting part."

Filvis gave him a sideways glance.

She knew that expression.

It was the expression Bell wore whenever he had several ideas and was deciding which one would be the most entertaining to pursue.

Unfortunately, that didn't always mean it was the most practical.

"Bell," she said.

"Hm?"

"Don't make this unnecessarily complicated."

He looked at her.

"I haven't done anything yet."

"That's precisely why I'm saying it now."

Alicia laughed.

Lefiya, however, was beginning to look impatient.

"Can we please focus?"

Bell raised his hands slightly.

"Of course."

He turned back toward the girls.

"There's one thing I'd like to know first. How large is the area you've already searched?"

Alicia considered the question.

"Quite large."

"That's not a measurement."

"I know."

Bell smiled.

"Then we're going to need a better one."

---

## A Proposal

The afternoon continued with questions.

Bell asked about the locations they had inspected, the nature of the sightings, and the information they had gathered from their investigation.

He didn't expect them to remember every detail.

In fact, he was more interested in discovering what they hadn't considered important enough to record.

Sometimes the most useful information wasn't the thing someone remembered.

It was the thing they had forgotten.

Filvis occasionally added details when the conversation reached something she knew.

Ais was straightforward with her answers.

Alicia was more careful, distinguishing what they knew from what they merely suspected.

Lefiya, despite her occasional frustration, contributed several observations of her own.

It was a surprisingly productive conversation.

Eventually, Bell leaned back.

"I think I understand the situation."

Alicia looked at him expectantly.

"Do you have an idea?"

"Several."

"Really?"

"Yes."

Lefiya immediately leaned forward.

"Then what are they?"

Bell smiled.

"First, I need to explain something."

He looked toward the coastline.

"Melen is large."

Ais nodded.

"Yes."

"And the surrounding areas aren't exactly easy to search. If we continue investigating the way you have been, we're going to spend a considerable amount of time checking places without knowing whether they're relevant."

"That's what we've been doing," Lefiya muttered.

"I know."

Bell tapped the table.

"Which is why we need to change our approach."

Alicia tilted her head.

"How?"

"Numbers."

The answer was simple.

Perhaps too simple.

Lefiya blinked.

"Numbers?"

"More people."

Bell gestured toward the surrounding area.

"We have a large area to investigate. Even if each person only covers a small section, having more people working simultaneously would make a considerable difference."

Ais looked at him.

"You're suggesting that we divide the search?"

"Exactly."

Bell nodded.

"Your group already has experience investigating the monsters. Filvis and I have been following the suspicious cargo. We can combine our information, establish which areas deserve attention, and divide the work accordingly."

Alicia smiled.

"That sounds reasonable."

"It is."

Bell continued.

"But there's something important."

The three girls waited.

"We shouldn't assume that every reported sighting is connected to the cargo investigation."

He raised a finger.

"Nor should we assume that every suspicious shipment contains monsters."

Another finger.

"We need evidence before connecting the two."

Lefiya nodded.

"That makes sense."

"Good."

Bell looked at Filvis.

"What do you think?"

She considered the proposal.

"It would certainly improve the investigation. We would have more people available to check locations and compare information."

"Exactly."

"But we should be careful about how we distribute the information."

Bell smiled.

"Agreed."

Ais looked between them.

"Then we can work together?"

"That's what I'm proposing."

Alicia seemed relieved.

"That would be wonderful."

Lefiya nodded.

"Finally, we might actually get somewhere."

Bell chuckled.

"Let's not celebrate before we've found anything."

"You're always so pessimistic!"

"I'm being realistic."

"That's not much better!"

Alicia laughed.

Filvis looked at Bell.

He was smiling again.

There was something about him that made even an investigation seem like an interesting activity.

She couldn't entirely blame the girls for wanting to spend time with him.

Unfortunately, she also couldn't pretend she was particularly enthusiastic about sharing all of it.

Especially when three pairs of eyes kept wandering in her direction.

---

## The Question of Cooperation

The agreement itself was surprisingly easy to reach.

There was no reason to make things complicated.

Both groups wanted answers, and their investigations had enough similarities to justify cooperating. More importantly, neither side had a particularly convincing reason to refuse.

Bell wasn't interested in competing over who would discover the truth first.

That would be a waste of time.

Alicia seemed to share his sentiment.

"Then it's settled. We'll cooperate."

"Works for me."

Ais nodded.

"Bell will help us?"

"I'll help with the investigation. I can't promise we'll find everything immediately."

"That's fine."

Lefiya seemed considerably more enthusiastic now.

"Then we should report this to Riveria as soon as possible!"

"Eventually," Bell replied.

Lefiya stopped.

"Eventually?"

"There's no need to rush into reporting something before we've properly organized what we're going to do."

"But—"

"Think about it. If we report that we're investigating illegal monster transportation, what exactly are we going to tell them?"

Lefiya opened her mouth.

Then closed it.

Bell continued.

"We have suspicious cargo, reports of insect-type monsters, and a possible connection between the two. That's enough to justify an investigation, but not enough to make a conclusion."

Alicia nodded.

"He's right. We should organize our findings first."

Ais looked at Bell.

"Then what should we do now?"

"Start with the information we already have."

He reached for a small notebook.

It wasn't particularly large, but it was enough for the preliminary notes he intended to make.

"Locations. Sightings. Cargo reports. Anything that might establish a connection."

He opened it.

"We can compare them and see whether anything overlaps."

Lefiya leaned forward.

"You're going to write everything down?"

"Would you prefer that I memorize it?"

"No, but..."

Bell smiled.

"Then let me work."

He began writing.

Filvis watched him for a moment before moving closer.

"Do you need me to organize the monster reports?"

"That would be helpful."

She nodded.

"All right."

Alicia observed the exchange.

Then her eyes shifted toward Bell.

Then Filvis.

Then back to Bell.

There was a small smile on her face.

It wasn't particularly difficult to understand why the two of them worked so comfortably together.

They seemed accustomed to sharing space.

Even their silences were natural.

And that, perhaps, was the most frustrating part.

Ais was still watching them.

"Bell."

"Hm?"

Ais looked at him.

"Can I help?"

"Of course."

"What should I do?"

"Could you tell me the locations you've already checked? Including the ones where you didn't find anything."

Ais nodded.

"Okay."

Lefiya immediately joined in.

"I can help with that too!"

"Great."

Alicia smiled.

"Then I'll organize the reports."

Bell looked around the table.

"Perfect. We have a proper investigation team now."

For a moment, everyone seemed satisfied.

Everyone except Lefiya.

She glanced at Filvis.

The elf was sitting close to Bell, occasionally looking at his notebook and offering suggestions.

There was nothing particularly provocative about it.

That was the problem.

She wasn't doing anything wrong.

She was simply there.

And Bell seemed perfectly happy with that arrangement.

Lefiya quietly lowered her gaze.

*This is going to be difficult.*

Ais, meanwhile, had a different approach.

She simply watched.

Then, after a moment, she moved her chair a little closer to Bell.

Filvis noticed.

So did Alicia.

Neither commented.

Bell, however, was too occupied with the notebook to notice the subtle competition developing around him.

Or perhaps he noticed and decided that it wasn't worth acknowledging.

With Bell, it was sometimes difficult to tell.

---

## An Unnecessary Advantage

The conversation gradually became more technical.

Bell wanted to establish the area they needed to cover before deciding how many people should be assigned to each section.

He studied the rough geographical information Alicia provided.

Melen.

A coastal settlement with surrounding terrain, roads, and several areas that were less frequently visited.

The problem wasn't simply finding monsters.

It was finding the people moving them.

Those were two different objectives.

A monster could leave traces.

A person transporting one could deliberately conceal them.

And if someone had gone to the trouble of arranging illegal transportation, it was reasonable to assume that they understood the importance of avoiding attention.

Bell tapped his pencil against the notebook.

*We need to find the route, not merely the destination.*

That was the important distinction.

If he found a monster, it wouldn't necessarily tell him where it had come from.

But if he found the place where it had been transported, the evidence might lead him toward the people responsible.

He could approach the problem differently.

Much differently.

The surrounding area contained ambient mana.

He could potentially use it to trace disturbances, identify irregularities, and narrow down locations where something unusual had happened.

It wasn't an impossible method.

In fact, it might save them a considerable amount of time.

Bell briefly considered it.

Then dismissed the idea.

*No.*

He closed his notebook halfway.

*Not yet.*

There was a certain satisfaction in solving a problem through observation and deduction.

He didn't want to immediately rely on an ability simply because it was convenient.

Besides, there were other considerations.

The area was large.

The information was incomplete.

And even if he successfully detected something unusual through ambient mana, that wouldn't necessarily tell him what had caused it.

He might find traces.

But traces weren't answers.

*If I use it now, I'll only be replacing one uncertainty with another.*

He preferred to establish the groundwork first.

Let the investigation develop naturally.

If they reached a point where ordinary methods became insufficient, he could reconsider.

There was no reason to reveal every advantage he possessed.

Bell looked at the others.

"All right. I think we should divide the investigation into stages."

Alicia turned toward him.

"Stages?"

"Yes. First, we organize the information. Second, we identify locations that deserve further inspection. Third, we assign people to investigate those locations."

He drew a rough line across the page.

"We shouldn't send everyone everywhere. That's inefficient."

Lefiya nodded.

"That makes sense."

"And we should keep a record of what each person finds, even if they find nothing."

Ais looked at the notebook.

"Why?"

"Because knowing where something isn't can be useful too."

He looked up.

"Especially when we're trying to identify a route."

Filvis smiled faintly.

"You're already thinking several steps ahead."

"Someone has to."

"You're enjoying this, aren't you?"

Bell paused.

Then smiled.

"A little."

She shook her head.

Alicia watched the exchange with growing amusement.

"Bell, you seem rather different when you're investigating something."

"Different?"

"You're more focused."

"Is that a bad thing?"

"No. I think it's rather interesting."

Lefiya crossed her arms.

"He's always like that!"

Bell looked at her.

"Am I?"

"Yes!"

"Then I suppose you know me rather well."

Lefiya froze.

Alicia covered her mouth.

Ais blinked.

Filvis lowered her eyes.

And Bell returned to his notebook as though he hadn't just caused another minor disturbance.

*Honestly...*

Lefiya turned away.

*He's impossible.*

---

## A Little Jealousy

It took another few minutes before the subject of cooperation was completely settled.

Alicia had gathered the available reports, Ais had provided the locations they had inspected, and Lefiya had begun arranging the information into a more coherent order.

Filvis was assisting Bell with the preliminary categorization.

The arrangement worked.

Perhaps a little too well.

"Bell."

"Hm?"

Ais looked at him.

"Can I sit there?"

She pointed toward the space beside him.

Bell glanced at the chair.

"Of course."

He moved his notebook slightly to make room.

Ais sat down.

Filvis looked at her.

Ais looked at Filvis.

Neither said anything.

Alicia noticed.

Then smiled.

*Oh, this is going to be interesting.*

Lefiya noticed it too.

Her expression immediately changed.

"A-Ais!"

"What?"

"Nothing!"

Ais tilted her head.

Then looked at Bell.

"You're going to stay in Melen for a while?"

"Probably."

"How long?"

"Until we've made sufficient progress."

Ais nodded.

"Then I'll see you tomorrow."

Bell smiled.

"Sure."

Filvis quietly turned a page in the notebook.

"Bell, shouldn't we discuss where we're going to begin?"

"Right."

He looked back at the information.

"Tomorrow, we should start by checking the locations with the most reliable reports. There's no point wasting time on something that hasn't been verified."

Alicia agreed.

"That would be the sensible approach."

Lefiya nodded.

"We can help arrange the necessary personnel."

"That would be useful."

Ais looked at Filvis again.

"Will you be going with Bell?"

Filvis paused.

"Yes."

"All right."

It was a simple exchange.

Nothing particularly hostile.

Yet the atmosphere around the table changed slightly.

Bell looked up.

"Is there a problem?"

"No," Ais replied.

"All right."

He returned to his notes.

Filvis glanced at him.

Then at Ais.

A small smile appeared on her face.

She understood.

The girls were jealous.

Not necessarily of her as a person, but of the closeness she shared with Bell.

It was understandable.

She wasn't entirely unfamiliar with that feeling herself.

But she had no intention of surrendering her position simply because the others had discovered it.

She leaned slightly toward Bell.

"You're forgetting something."

Bell looked at her.

"What?"

"Your drink."

He glanced toward the cup beside him.

"Oh."

He picked it up.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

Alicia smiled.

Lefiya looked away.

Ais remained quiet.

And Bell, once again, appeared blissfully unaware of the small emotional battlefield developing around his table.

*Perhaps I should leave them alone for a while,* Alicia thought.

It was probably the kindest thing to do.

After all, they had an investigation to conduct.

And a rather important one.

She wasn't about to let a little jealousy interfere with that.

*There will be time for Bell later.*

She smiled to herself.

*For now, we need to find those monsters.*

---

## The First Step

By the time the afternoon began to fade, the preliminary arrangements had been completed.

It wasn't a particularly sophisticated plan.

There were still too many unanswered questions, and the available information wasn't sufficient to establish a definitive direction.

But it was a beginning.

And beginnings mattered.

Bell closed his notebook.

"All right. I think that's enough for today."

Lefiya looked surprised.

"Already?"

"We've been discussing this for quite some time."

"But we haven't actually started searching!"

"Which is precisely why we should prepare before doing so."

He stood and stretched his arms.

"There's no advantage in exhausting ourselves today only to discover tomorrow that we overlooked something important."

Alicia nodded.

"That's reasonable."

Ais stood as well.

"Then tomorrow?"

"Tomorrow."

Bell smiled.

"We'll begin properly."

Filvis gathered the remaining notes.

"I'll organize the information we discussed tonight."

"I'll help."

Alicia offered immediately.

Filvis looked at her.

"Thank you."

Lefiya raised her hand.

"Me too!"

Bell looked at them.

"You're all very enthusiastic."

"Of course!" Lefiya replied. "We can't afford to waste another day!"

"That's true."

He looked at Ais.

"What about you?"

Ais nodded.

"I'll help."

"Then I suppose we're settled."

Alicia smiled.

"Bell, there's something else."

"Hm?"

"Since we're going to be working together, perhaps we should establish where we'll meet tomorrow."

"That's a good idea."

They discussed a suitable meeting arrangement, keeping it simple enough that neither group would have difficulty finding the others.

There was no need to overcomplicate things.

Once the details were settled, the three girls began preparing to leave.

Ais was the last to move.

She looked at Bell.

"See you tomorrow."

"See you."

Then she looked at Filvis.

"Goodbye."

Filvis returned her greeting.

"Goodbye, Ais."

Lefiya followed.

"Thank you for the refreshments!"

"You're welcome."

Alicia was the last.

She looked at the two of them and smiled.

"Take care of each other."

Filvis nodded.

Bell raised a hand.

"See you tomorrow."

The three walked away.

For a moment, the campsite became quiet again.

The sound of the waves returned to prominence.

Bell watched them disappear beyond the vegetation.

Then he looked toward Filvis.

"...Well."

Filvis glanced at him.

"What?"

"That was unexpected."

She smiled.

"Very."

Bell sat down again.

He looked at the notebook on the table.

"Still, it worked out rather nicely."

"You're not bothered?"

"About what?"

"Having three unexpected visitors interrupt our afternoon."

Bell considered the question.

Then shook his head.

"Not particularly."

Filvis looked at him.

"You're rather accommodating."

"Should I not be?"

She smiled.

"No. I suppose not."

Bell leaned back.

"Besides, we needed more people."

"That's true."

He looked toward the direction the girls had gone.

"They seemed rather frustrated with their investigation."

"They were."

"Then perhaps this will help."

Filvis nodded.

She understood that Bell was serious.

For all his occasional teasing, he wasn't treating the matter lightly.

There was something unusual happening around Melen.

And if the reports were connected, then the problem might be considerably larger than either group initially expected.

She looked at the notebook.

"Do you think the cargo and the monsters are connected?"

Bell didn't answer immediately.

He picked up the notebook and examined the information again.

"I think it's worth investigating."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the only honest one I have."

Filvis smiled faintly.

"Fair enough."

Bell closed the notebook.

"Let's not jump to conclusions."

---

## Something Beneath the Surface

The sun continued its descent.

Melen was gradually transitioning into evening.

The streets were becoming busier as merchants prepared to close their stalls and residents returned to their homes. Somewhere in the distance, the sound of a carriage could be heard passing along a road.

It was an ordinary evening.

Nothing about the settlement suggested that something particularly unusual was happening.

And that was precisely what made the situation interesting.

Bell stood near the edge of the campsite.

He looked toward the settlement.

*Illegal transportation.*

The phrase had been bothering him.

Not because it was particularly difficult to understand.

But because of what it implied.

Someone had to arrange the transportation.

Someone had to provide the monsters.

Someone had to move them.

And someone had to ensure that nobody noticed.

That meant there were several possible points of failure.

A shipment could be discovered.

A route could be exposed.

A person could talk.

An arrangement could go wrong.

Yet the Loki Familia had found practically nothing.

That didn't necessarily mean the operation was particularly sophisticated.

It could simply mean they were looking in the wrong places.

Bell looked at the surrounding terrain.

*Or perhaps the monsters aren't being transported in the way we expect.*

He considered that possibility.

A cargo shipment didn't necessarily have to resemble a conventional shipment.

There were different ways of concealing something.

Different methods of movement.

Different explanations for why a particular location might appear suspicious.

He couldn't establish any of them yet.

And he wasn't going to invent an answer simply because he wanted one.

Behind him, Filvis approached.

"Bell?"

He turned.

"Hm?"

"You're thinking again."

"Am I that obvious?"

"Rather."

He smiled.

"Sorry."

She shook her head.

"Don't apologize."

Filvis stood beside him.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then she looked toward Melen.

"Do you think we'll find something tomorrow?"

Bell followed her gaze.

"I hope so."

"You're not certain?"

"No."

He slipped his hands into his pockets.

"That's why it's called an investigation."

Filvis laughed quietly.

"You're impossible."

"That's what I've been told."

She leaned against him.

Bell looked down.

Then gently rested his hand against hers.

The evening breeze passed through the campsite.

For the moment, there was no reason to hurry.

They had a plan.

They had additional people.

And tomorrow, they would begin searching properly.

But somewhere in Melen, there was something they hadn't found.

Something that had escaped the attention of the Loki Familia.

Something that might have been moving through the settlement while everyone else was looking in the wrong direction.

Bell didn't know what it was.

Not yet.

And perhaps that was the most interesting part.

He glanced at the notebook once more.

Four insect-type monsters.

Suspicious cargo.

Missing information.

A city that had provided no reliable answers.

*Well...*

A faint smile appeared on his face.

*Let's see what we're missing.*

**End of Chapter Eighty-Five.**
