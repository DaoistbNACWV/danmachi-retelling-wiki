# Chapter Eighty-Seven: We Meet Again

*Somewhere beyond the boundaries of existence, where neither time nor space held meaning, something that should have ceased to exist continued to drift.*

---

There was nothing.

No light. No darkness. No sound. No sensation of falling, despite the endless sensation of movement. There was not even a distinction between one direction and another. Everything that could have been perceived had long since disappeared, leaving behind an incomprehensible expanse of absolute nothingness.

Bell drifted.

He could not remember how long he had been there. Perhaps a few seconds. Perhaps several centuries. The concept of duration had become meaningless in a place where time itself did not appear to exist.

He tried moving his arms.

Nothing happened.

Or perhaps they had moved, but there was no way for him to distinguish the action from stillness.

His body felt strangely distant. It was as though he were observing himself from somewhere far away, separated from his physical existence by an immeasurable distance.

*What happened?*

His thoughts were sluggish.

He remembered something.

A flash of light. An overwhelming sensation. The world disappearing around him.

Then nothing.

Bell attempted to recall the events preceding his current predicament, but his memories seemed scattered. He could remember fragments, impressions, and certain conversations, but putting them together was proving increasingly difficult.

*Am I dead?*

The question lingered.

It was a reasonable conclusion, considering his circumstances. However, the absence of a heartbeat or breathing did not necessarily mean anything here.

He had experienced stranger things.

Far stranger.

"Bell."

A familiar voice reached him.

His attention immediately shifted.

It was faint at first, almost indistinguishable from the silence surrounding him. Yet there was no mistaking it. The voice possessed a particular quality that he had grown accustomed to hearing.

"Bell. Can you hear me?"

He recognized it.

"Astra?"

"Finally. I've been attempting to establish a connection for quite some time."

The voice became clearer.

A familiar presence emerged somewhere within his consciousness, although Bell could not see her. It was the sensation of someone standing beside him, except there was no physical presence to accompany it.

His personal assistant.

The one who had accompanied him through countless experiences, provided information when he needed it, and occasionally reminded him of things he would have preferred to ignore.

For the first time since arriving in this place, Bell felt something resembling relief.

"Where are you?" he asked.

"Maintaining a mental connection with you."

"That doesn't answer my question."

"It is the most accurate answer I can provide."

Bell sighed internally.

*Some things never change.*

"Then tell me where I am."

There was a brief pause.

"An intermediate space. Or, more accurately, a region between established worlds. It is difficult to describe using conventional terminology because the underlying principles governing this location do not correspond to those of your previous environment."

"Am I still in our world?"

"No."

"Can I return?"

Another pause.

This time, it was considerably longer.

"Not at present."

Bell's expression, had he possessed one, would have become considerably less pleasant.

"Explain."

"The world has imposed restrictions upon your existence. These restrictions are not ordinary physical limitations, nor are they comparable to the laws governing magic, divinity, or the abilities you have previously encountered."

"Then what are they?"

"Fundamental restrictions."

Astra's voice remained composed, but Bell noticed something unusual.

She sounded cautious.

"Your existence has become incompatible with certain aspects of the world. Consequently, the world itself is rejecting your return."

Bell remained silent.

He understood the implication.

It was not that he lacked the ability to return. Rather, the destination itself would not permit his existence to re-enter.

A rather inconvenient distinction.

"Can you remove those restrictions?"

"Not with our current capabilities."

"Can we force our way through?"

"That would be inadvisable."

"Why?"

"Because the restrictions are not simply barriers. They are closer to an imposed condition upon the relationship between your existence and that world. Attempting to overcome them without sufficient power could result in your existence being rejected entirely."

Bell considered that.

"So I need to become stronger."

"Precisely."

"How much stronger?"

"I cannot provide an exact measurement. The restrictions appear to respond to the nature of your existence rather than a quantifiable level of power. You would need to acquire sufficient authority to challenge the conditions imposed upon you."

"Authority, huh?"

"Yes. Power alone might not be sufficient. You would need the ability to impose your own conditions upon reality, rather than merely resist those already established."

Bell found himself contemplating the explanation.

It was not an entirely unfamiliar concept.

Throughout his previous experiences, he had encountered numerous beings whose power extended beyond conventional measurements. Some possessed authority over particular aspects of existence. Others could manipulate the principles governing their surroundings.

And then there were those who stood above the systems themselves.

If the world was rejecting him because of what he represented, then attempting to return through brute force would be equivalent to trying to break a rule by punching it.

Not impossible, perhaps.

Just incredibly stupid.

"How do we accomplish that?"

"I have prepared several possible approaches."

"Of course you have."

"I would appreciate it if you allowed me to finish before making assumptions."

"Go ahead."

Astra began explaining.

Her proposal was relatively straightforward. Bell would need to accumulate strength, acquire additional abilities, and gradually expand the scope of his authority. Rather than attempting to challenge the restrictions immediately, they would establish a foundation capable of supporting such an undertaking.

It was a long-term plan.

An exceptionally long-term plan.

There were numerous uncertainties, but the general direction was sound.

Bell listened carefully.

He asked questions whenever something appeared unclear, challenged several assumptions, and requested additional explanations regarding the relationship between authority and existence.

Astra answered each question as thoroughly as her current information allowed.

Eventually, Bell reached a conclusion.

"That sounds feasible."

"I agree. Although I would strongly recommend avoiding unnecessary risks during the initial stages."

"You're assuming I have a choice."

"You generally have one. You simply dislike choosing the safer option."

"That's a rather unfair assessment."

"Would you like me to provide supporting examples?"

"No."

"Understood."

Bell almost laughed.

Almost.

For a moment, the situation felt strangely ordinary. It was as though they were discussing another adventure, another problem that needed solving, another obstacle standing between him and whatever objective he had decided to pursue.

There was comfort in that familiarity.

Then something happened.

Astra stopped speaking.

"Bell?"

He noticed it too.

Something had changed.

It was subtle at first, a sensation that could easily have been dismissed as an illusion. The nothingness surrounding him seemed to possess a direction.

Not a physical direction.

A destination.

Bell felt himself drifting toward something.

Or perhaps he had already crossed into it.

"Astra?"

"Yes?"

"Are we moving?"

There was a brief silence.

"According to my observations, your position has remained relatively consistent. However..."

"However?"

"I'm detecting an unusual distortion ahead."

Bell turned his attention toward the direction of the disturbance.

He could not see anything.

Yet something was there.

It felt like a boundary.

An invisible division separating one region of existence from another.

"Can you identify it?"

"Negative. My current perception is insufficient to determine its nature."

Bell considered stopping.

He attempted to slow his movement, but the sensation of drifting continued. Whatever was drawing him forward did not seem particularly interested in his cooperation.

"Can you pull me back?"

"I can attempt to—"

The connection distorted.

Astra's voice suddenly became fragmented.

"Bell, wait. Something is interfering with the connection."

"What?"

"The interference is originating from beyond the boundary. I cannot establish its—"

Her voice disappeared for a fraction of a second.

Then returned.

"Bell, do not cross—"

The connection snapped.

Silence.

"Astra?"

No response.

"Astra."

Nothing.

He attempted to establish the connection himself.

Nothing.

For the first time since entering this place, Bell was genuinely alone.

He continued drifting.

The invisible boundary approached.

Or rather, he approached it.

There was no sensation of impact when he crossed it. No explosion, no flash of light, no sudden alteration in his surroundings.

Instead, everything simply changed.

The nothingness vanished.

And something else took its place.

---

The first thing Bell noticed was the presence of something incomprehensibly vast.

It was not merely large.

It was enormous in a manner that made the concept of size itself seem inadequate.

The space surrounding him had acquired depth, distance, and structure. There were things that resembled stars, although their positions and arrangements did not correspond to any celestial formation he recognized.

Some appeared to be suspended in the distance.

Others seemed to exist in layers, overlapping without occupying the same physical location.

Bell could not determine whether he was looking at a place, a dimension, or something that existed beyond the conventional understanding of both.

He felt small.

Not physically.

His existence itself seemed insignificant against the magnitude of what surrounded him.

And then he saw it.

A figure.

No.

Calling it a figure was already an understatement.

It was a man.

Or something that had chosen to resemble one.

Its body was gilded in gold, radiating an almost oppressive brilliance. The material covering its enormous frame resembled polished metal, yet Bell instinctively understood that it was not ordinary gold.

There was an unnatural quality to its appearance.

Its proportions were humanoid, but its scale was so extraordinary that Bell struggled to comprehend where its body ended.

And above its shoulders floated three enormous heads.

Each possessed a distinct expression.

One appeared calm.

Another wore an expression of mild curiosity.

The third seemed almost amused.

They were arranged around the same central body, suspended independently, as though they were separate entities sharing a single existence.

Bell stared.

For several moments, neither party spoke.

Then the central head tilted slightly.

"Oh?"

The voice was enormous.

It did not merely travel through the surrounding space. It seemed to emerge from every direction simultaneously, resonating through the very foundation of the place.

"Here you are again, Chronos."

Bell's attention sharpened.

*Chronos?*

The name was familiar.

Far too familiar.

He had heard it before. He knew what it represented, what it implied, and why someone might address him using that particular name.

But he had never encountered this being.

At least, not in his current memories.

The second head leaned forward.

"How many times are you going to visit our macrocosm?"

The third head laughed softly.

"Didn't I erase you from existence?"

A brief silence followed.

"How curious."

The enormous being examined Bell.

Its three heads appeared to be observing him from different perspectives, scrutinizing every aspect of his existence.

"How are you still alive?"

Bell did not answer.

He was too busy attempting to understand what he was looking at.

*Our macrocosm?*

The terminology alone was troublesome.

He had encountered beings who referred to their respective worlds as universes, dimensions, realms, and even higher planes of existence.

But this was different.

The manner in which the creature spoke suggested that the entire structure surrounding them was something it considered its domain.

And apparently, it had encountered him before.

Or rather, someone who looked like him.

The first head narrowed its eyes.

"Wait."

Its expression changed.

"Oh."

The second head followed.

"I see."

The third head chuckled.

"It appears you've acquired a new body."

Bell's thoughts briefly stopped.

*New body?*

He looked down at himself.

He could not properly inspect his physical condition, but he understood what the creature was implying.

It was not addressing him as though he were an unfamiliar visitor.

It was addressing him as someone it recognized.

Someone who had apparently been erased from existence before.

Someone named Chronos.

And yet...

"Never mind," the enormous being continued.

"Whatever the circumstances, it appears I cannot send you back to your respective realm."

The first head looked disappointed.

The second seemed entertained.

The third smiled.

"How unfortunate."

Bell finally found his voice.

"Who are you?"

The three heads turned toward him.

For a moment, the enormous figure appeared genuinely amused.

"Who am I?"

The first head repeated the question.

The second laughed.

The third tilted its head.

"Have you really forgotten?"

Bell remained silent.

The being regarded him for another moment before speaking.

"How interesting. Perhaps the new body has brought about some peculiar changes."

It gestured toward Bell.

"Regardless, I have no particular interest in discussing your circumstances."

Its enormous hand began to move.

The movement was slow, almost casual.

Yet the surrounding space trembled.

Bell immediately became aware of something.

A pressure.

Not the pressure of physical weight, nor the sensation of an overwhelming magical presence.

It was something far more fundamental.

The feeling of being observed by an entity that did not need to acknowledge the laws governing ordinary existence.

Bell attempted to move.

For the first time, his body responded.

Unfortunately, it did not respond quickly enough.

The enormous being continued speaking.

"It is rather amusing to meet you again, Chronos."

One of its heads smiled.

"But as briefly as this reunion may be..."

The second head raised an enormous finger.

"I suppose I shall simply send you somewhere else."

The third head closed its eyes.

"Goodbye."

And then—

The world disappeared.

---

There was no resistance.

No opportunity to negotiate.

No final exchange of words.

The enormous being had simply decided that Bell no longer belonged in its presence, and the surrounding reality had responded accordingly.

Bell felt himself being pulled apart.

Not physically.

Something was happening to the very structure of his existence.

His consciousness stretched across an immeasurable distance, passing through layers of reality that he could neither perceive nor comprehend.

For a fleeting moment, he felt the presence of Astra.

A distant, almost inaudible connection.

Then even that disappeared.

He fell.

For the first time since arriving in the endless nothingness, he experienced something resembling actual movement.

He was falling through darkness.

Except it was not empty.

There was something inside it.

Something unpleasant.

Something that made his instincts recoil.

Bell felt countless impressions brushing against his consciousness.

Anger.

Fear.

Grief.

Hatred.

Jealousy.

Regret.

Despair.

They came in fragments, like countless voices speaking simultaneously.

Some were distant.

Others were disturbingly close.

He felt an overwhelming accumulation of negative emotions, each one seemingly independent yet connected to something much larger.

It was as though the entire world had been saturated with them.

*What is this?*

He attempted to establish his connection with Astra again.

Nothing.

He tried reaching for the familiar abilities that had once been available to him.

The response was inconsistent.

Some answered.

Others did not.

It was difficult to determine whether the problem originated from his condition or the world he was entering.

Then he felt something else.

A presence.

Not the enormous golden being.

Not Astra.

Something else.

It was faint, almost imperceptible, but there was no mistaking its nature.

It was alive.

And it was surrounded by an extraordinary amount of negativity.

Bell's descent accelerated.

The darkness began to change.

He could feel something approaching.

A world.

A living world.

One filled with countless individuals, each possessing their own thoughts, desires, and emotions.

And beneath all of them—

Something else.

Something that did not belong to ordinary humanity.

---

*Somewhere in another world.*

The morning sun had barely risen.

The streets were beginning to fill with people, and the ordinary rhythm of daily life continued uninterrupted.

Cars passed through intersections.

Pedestrians crossed sidewalks.

Shopkeepers prepared their businesses.

Children hurried toward their respective schools.

It was an entirely ordinary morning.

At least, to those who could not see beyond the surface.

Because beneath the ordinary appearance of the world existed something far more sinister.

A hidden ecosystem.

A world inhabited by things born from the darker aspects of human existence.

Fear gave birth to them.

Hatred strengthened them.

Grief and resentment nourished them.

The more humanity suffered, the more plentiful they became.

Most people would never see them.

They would never understand why certain places felt uncomfortable, why particular buildings were avoided, or why inexplicable tragedies occurred in places where nobody could provide a reasonable explanation.

They would simply attribute such incidents to bad luck.

Or unfortunate circumstances.

But those who possessed the necessary perception understood the truth.

They were not alone.

And they never had been.

---

A small disturbance occurred somewhere beyond the ordinary boundaries of perception.

It was subtle.

Almost nonexistent.

A brief fluctuation in the surrounding atmosphere, accompanied by a strange distortion that lasted less than a fraction of a second.

A few birds suddenly changed direction.

A stray dog stopped walking and stared toward an empty alley.

Then everything returned to normal.

Except something had changed.

Something had entered the world.

And the world, in turn, had begun to acknowledge its presence.

---

Bell opened his eyes.

Or at least, he thought he did.

His consciousness was returning in fragments.

The first thing he noticed was warmth.

Then pressure.

A strange sensation of confinement surrounded him, accompanied by a rhythmic sound that he initially struggled to identify.

*Thump.*

*Thump.*

*Thump.*

A heartbeat.

His heartbeat?

No.

It was not his.

He attempted to move.

His limbs responded, but something felt wrong.

The movements were weak, uncoordinated, and considerably smaller than what he remembered.

He tried opening his eyes again.

Light.

Blurry shapes.

An indistinct ceiling.

The sensation of being held.

Someone was speaking nearby.

"Congratulations. He's healthy."

A woman's voice.

It sounded tired but relieved.

Another voice answered.

"Thank goodness."

A man.

There was a noticeable tremor in his words.

"He's really here..."

Bell attempted to focus.

*What?*

He tried turning his head.

He could barely move it.

Something was terribly wrong.

His vision remained unfocused, and the surrounding sounds were muffled. He could distinguish voices, but the words seemed to reach him through several layers of distance.

He attempted to speak.

Nothing.

Instead, a small noise escaped his throat.

A cry.

He stopped.

*...What?*

The realization came slowly.

Too slowly.

He attempted to raise his hand.

Something moved in front of his face.

Small fingers.

Tiny fingers.

He stared.

Or tried to.

His vision was still developing, and the details remained indistinct, but there was no mistaking the proportions.

His body was different.

Much smaller.

Considerably smaller.

He was...

"He's crying."

The woman spoke again.

"That's normal. He's still adjusting to the outside world."

The man let out a nervous laugh.

"Right. Of course."

There was a brief pause.

"Can I hold him?"

"Carefully."

A pair of arms approached.

Bell felt himself being lifted.

His body instinctively reacted to the movement, and he found himself staring toward a face.

A human face.

An unfamiliar one.

The man holding him appeared young, perhaps in his twenties. His expression was filled with an overwhelming mixture of exhaustion, relief, and happiness.

"Hello there."

The man smiled.

"Welcome to the world, little one."

Bell stared.

He wanted to ask where he was.

He wanted to ask what had happened.

He wanted to know why his body had changed and what had become of Astra.

But none of those questions could escape his mouth.

Instead, he made another small sound.

The man chuckled.

"Already trying to talk, huh?"

The woman laughed.

"He's a newborn. Give him some time."

Bell wanted to object.

*I'm not trying to talk.*

*I'm trying to understand what the hell is happening.*

Unfortunately, his current vocal capabilities were insufficient to communicate that sentiment.

He closed his eyes.

Perhaps he needed time.

Perhaps this was another temporary condition.

Perhaps there was some explanation that would become apparent once he had regained control of his body.

He had been through stranger circumstances.

He could endure this.

That was when he felt it.

A faint sensation.

Something crawling along the edges of his perception.

Bell's eyes immediately opened.

There it was again.

A peculiar presence.

It was not coming from the people around him.

It was somewhere else.

Beyond the walls.

Beyond the building.

Something was observing the newborn.

And it was not alone.

He could feel numerous similar presences scattered throughout the surrounding area. Some were weak, almost insignificant. Others possessed a much more substantial presence.

They were not human.

Bell's instincts recognized that much.

He attempted to examine them.

A peculiar sensation answered his attempt.

It was as though he were looking at something through an unfamiliar set of senses.

The negative emotions he had encountered during his descent were everywhere.

Not merely in the people around him.

They had accumulated into something tangible.

Something that could move, grow, and apparently exist independently.

*What kind of world is this?*

For a brief moment, Bell recalled the enormous golden being.

Its three heads.

Its words.

*I'll simply send you somewhere else.*

He felt a headache beginning to form.

Of all the possible destinations, he had apparently been sent to a world where the collective negativity of humanity had become something capable of taking physical form.

Wonderful.

Absolutely wonderful.

He tried reaching for Astra once more.

Nothing.

Not even a faint response.

His expression remained unchanged, but internally, he was becoming increasingly concerned.

He was alone.

In an unfamiliar world.

Inside an unfamiliar body.

Without a reliable explanation for his circumstances.

And whatever had happened to him before arriving here appeared to have affected more than just his physical condition.

He would need to start from the beginning.

Again.

*Fine.*

He could work with that.

He had no intention of allowing his current circumstances to dictate the remainder of his existence.

If this world had rules, he would learn them.

If it had restrictions, he would understand them.

And if something stood between him and his eventual return, he would find a way to overcome it.

That was the plan.

A rather ambitious plan for someone who could barely move his fingers.

But a plan nonetheless.

---

"He's surprisingly quiet."

The woman adjusted the blanket around the newborn.

The man looked down at him.

"Maybe he's just tired."

"Perhaps."

She smiled.

"What's his name again?"

The man hesitated.

Then he looked toward the small child in his arms.

"We haven't decided yet."

"Really?"

"I was thinking we should wait until we could see him properly."

The woman chuckled.

"You're the father. You should have at least one idea."

"I do."

He looked at the newborn.

There was something strangely reassuring about holding him.

Perhaps it was the weight of the small body against his arms. Perhaps it was the realization that, despite everything, the child was finally here.

Whatever the reason, his expression softened.

"I've been thinking about it for months."

"Then?"

The man smiled.

"I think I've found one."

He lowered his head.

"How about..."

Before he could finish, a sudden chill passed through the room.

It was so brief that neither adult paid it any attention.

The newborn, however, noticed.

Something had entered his immediate surroundings.

A faint, unpleasant presence.

It lingered for a moment before disappearing.

Bell's attention followed it.

*So they can enter places like this too.*

He would need to investigate.

But first, he needed to understand his own condition.

His vision was still blurry, his body was practically useless, and his thoughts were becoming increasingly difficult to maintain.

He closed his eyes.

For now, rest was the only reasonable option.

---

Far away from the hospital, somewhere beyond the ordinary perception of humanity, a peculiar disturbance occurred.

A small, malformed creature crouched upon the ceiling of an abandoned building.

Its body was twisted.

Its limbs were unnaturally elongated, and its face resembled a grotesque imitation of a human being.

It was a curse.

A relatively weak one.

Normally, it would have continued wandering without purpose, feeding upon the negative emotions accumulated within its surroundings.

But something had caught its attention.

It stopped moving.

Its head slowly turned.

For a moment, it stared toward the distant hospital.

Then its entire body began to tremble.

It was afraid.

The creature did not understand why.

There was no visible threat.

No overwhelming spiritual pressure.

No approaching sorcerer.

Nothing that could reasonably explain the sudden instinctive terror overwhelming its existence.

Yet something had arrived.

Something that did not belong.

Something that its primitive instincts could not comprehend.

The curse slowly lowered its head.

Then it fled.

---

Back inside the hospital, the newborn slept peacefully.

His small chest rose and fell with each breath.

The adults continued talking, unaware of the peculiar events occurring beyond their immediate surroundings.

And somewhere within the depths of his consciousness, Bell continued to drift.

He was not dreaming.

He was simply resting.

Yet, for a fleeting instant, something stirred.

A faint sensation of recognition.

An unfamiliar world.

An unfamiliar body.

An unfamiliar existence.

And beneath it all, a name.

Not Bell Cranel.

Not Chronos.

Something else.

Something that belonged to the life he had just inherited.

A name that had not yet been spoken aloud.

A name that would eventually become known to those who inhabited this world.

Because the child who had entered this world was not merely another newborn.

He was an existence that had already lived through circumstances beyond the comprehension of ordinary beings.

An existence that had once been erased.

An existence that had somehow returned.

And now, he would have to begin again.

---

**Somewhere else, beyond the boundaries of the world.**

The enormous golden being watched the place where Bell had disappeared.

Its three heads remained silent for a moment.

Then the second one spoke.

"That was rather unexpected."

The first head nodded.

"Indeed. I was certain the previous erasure had been sufficient."

The third head laughed.

"Perhaps we should have checked."

"Perhaps."

The first head turned slightly.

"But it no longer matters. He is no longer within our macrocosm."

The second head examined the space where Bell had once stood.

"Do you think he will remember?"

The third head smiled.

"Does it matter?"

The first head remained silent.

Then, after a considerable pause, it spoke.

"Chronos..."

Its voice carried a peculiar quality.

Something between amusement and apprehension.

"Let us hope you do not become troublesome again."

The three heads slowly turned away.

And the macrocosm continued its existence.

Unaware that the being it had once erased had returned.

Unaware that its latest decision might have consequences extending far beyond its understanding.

And unaware that, in a distant world filled with curses, fear, and malice, something ancient had just begun its second life.

**A new beginning.**

**A different world.**

**And a name that had yet to be revealed.**

---

*End of Chapter Eighty-Seven.*
