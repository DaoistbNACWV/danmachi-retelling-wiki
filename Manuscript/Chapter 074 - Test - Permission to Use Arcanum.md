# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

# Chapter Seventy-Four — Test: Permission to Use Arcanum

*There were several ways to repair a broken thing. Some required patience, others demanded extraordinary power. And occasionally, the solution required asking someone who possessed far more power than oneself.*

---

### I. An Answer That Solved Nothing

The laboratory had become unusually quiet.

Several crystalline containers occupied the far end of the workbench. Their contents were unmistakable: dark, corrupted material extracted from Ein's body during the previous experiment.

Even separated from its original host, the substance remained active.

It moved occasionally, responding to the mana circulating around it. The movements were subtle, almost imperceptible, but Bell had already established that the material possessed an extraordinary capacity to sustain itself.

He had spent the better part of the morning examining the results of Test One.

The outcome was neither a complete success nor a failure.

He had successfully extracted a substantial portion of the corruption. His healing magic had restored the damaged tissue, and the suspended animation formation continued maintaining Ein's bodily functions.

But the most important concentrations remained.

Her heart.

Her brain.

Two locations where the corruption had become so deeply integrated with her biological structures that attempting conventional extraction would risk killing her.

Bell sat before his notes.

His right hand supported his chin while his left occasionally turned a page.

There were several diagrams illustrating the distribution of the remaining corruption. Each contained annotations regarding its connections, magical properties, and observed reactions.

He had already considered the obvious solutions.

More precise runic separation.

Improved mana regulation.

A different arrangement of healing formations.

Even the possibility of developing a specialized purification process.

Unfortunately, none of those approaches addressed the fundamental problem.

The corruption wasn't merely attached to Ein.

It had become part of the system keeping her alive.

Removing it required distinguishing between two things that had become practically inseparable.

Bell stared at the diagram.

*There has to be another way.*

He leaned back.

His eyes gradually moved toward one of the empty sections of his notebook.

A thought entered his mind.

It was an unpleasantly familiar one.

*Cloning.*

His expression became thoughtful.

The concept itself wasn't particularly difficult to understand.

If the original body was too compromised to undergo the necessary treatment, perhaps he could produce another one.

A biological copy.

A vessel constructed from Ein's original biological information, free from the corruption that had invaded her current body.

He could theoretically transfer her consciousness or soul into the new vessel after establishing the necessary conditions.

It was an interesting possibility.

More importantly, it offered a potential way around the problem of her heart and brain.

Bell began writing.

**Alternative: Biological reconstruction through duplication.**

He stopped.

His pen remained suspended above the paper.

Then he slowly lowered it.

*Wait.*

He examined the sentence.

The idea sounded considerably more attractive when considered as an isolated technical problem.

But that wasn't the problem he was trying to solve.

He wasn't attempting to manufacture another Ein.

He was trying to save the one already lying in his laboratory.

A clone would introduce an entirely different set of complications.

Would the resulting body possess the same memories?

Would it develop the same personality?

Would the soul transfer successfully?

Would the original Ein's consciousness remain intact?

And, perhaps most importantly, what would happen to the person currently suspended inside the examination chamber?

He rubbed his forehead.

*I'm getting ahead of myself.*

Even if he succeeded in producing an identical body, he would still need to solve the problem of transferring Ein into it.

That wasn't a cure.

It was an entirely different procedure with its own uncertainties.

Worse, it risked treating her original body as something disposable.

Bell looked toward the examination chamber.

Ein remained suspended within the protective formations.

Her expression was peaceful, untouched by the complications that had occupied his attention for the past several hours.

He had promised her that he would find a way to save her.

Not manufacture a replacement.

Not create something that resembled her.

Save her.

Bell closed the notebook.

*No. That's not the answer.*

He wasn't entirely dismissing the possibility of biological reconstruction. It might prove useful for other problems someday.

But using it here would mean abandoning the actual objective before exhausting the available alternatives.

And he wasn't prepared to do that.

He stood and approached the examination chamber.

The formations continued their steady operation.

He checked the readings.

Stable.

Mana circulation remained within the established parameters. The remaining corruption showed no significant change in activity.

Everything was functioning as intended.

For now.

Bell rested one hand against the edge of the examination table.

"Still sleeping, huh?"

There was no response.

He smiled faintly.

"Well, I haven't forgotten about you."

He turned toward his workbench.

The problem remained.

But perhaps he had been approaching it from the wrong direction.

---

### II. Two Possibilities

Bell returned to his desk and began arranging his research into separate categories.

He needed to distinguish between what he could accomplish through his own abilities and what required an external source of power.

The distinction was important.

There were two possibilities worth investigating.

The first was considerably more straightforward.

High-tier cleansing magic.

If he could develop or employ a sufficiently powerful purification effect, perhaps he could neutralize the corruption without physically extracting every remaining fragment.

Theoretically, the process would be simpler.

Instead of separating the artificial network from the biological structures it had invaded, he would target the corruption itself.

Neutralize it.

Render it harmless.

Then allow the remaining foreign material to be removed through a controlled procedure.

There was an obvious advantage.

He wouldn't necessarily need to interfere with the heart or brain in the same manner as before.

However, the method possessed a rather significant limitation.

Mana consumption.

A spell powerful enough to affect the entire corruption network would require an extraordinary amount of magical energy.

Bell examined his calculations.

The artificial network wasn't a small contamination occupying a limited region.

It extended throughout Ein's body, with major concentrations embedded in particularly sensitive areas.

A localized cleansing spell might be insufficient.

He needed something capable of reaching the entire structure.

And that meant considerably increasing the spell's output.

*The surrounding environment could provide the mana.*

His eyes moved toward another page.

He had already experimented with manipulating ambient mana through different magical formations.

Rather than supplying the entire cost himself, he could establish a controlled area and draw the necessary energy from the environment.

It was a reasonable solution.

But not a particularly comfortable one.

The amount of mana required could be enormous.

He would need to establish a sufficiently large formation, regulate the incoming energy, and maintain the spell throughout the purification process.

A mistake in the calculations could destabilize the formation.

And even if everything worked, there was another consequence.

His own reserves.

The process would demand substantial concentration and potentially consume much of the mana he needed to maintain the surrounding systems.

Bell glanced toward the examination chamber.

The suspended animation formation required continuous support.

The protective formations required maintenance.

The laboratory itself contained several interconnected magical structures.

He couldn't simply expend everything and hope that the remaining systems would continue operating.

If his reserves became depleted, he would have to prioritize.

And the possibility of leaving Ein's condition unstable because he had exhausted himself was unacceptable.

He wrote another note.

**Method One — High-tier cleansing magic.**

- Requires substantial mana.
- Can potentially target the corruption directly.
- Environmental mana may provide the necessary energy.
- Requires careful regulation and extensive preparation.
- Risk of severe personal exhaustion.

He placed the pen down.

It was a viable option.

Expensive, but viable.

The second possibility, however, was considerably more complicated.

And considerably more dangerous.

Bell reached for another collection of documents.

The heading contained a word he had encountered numerous times throughout his research.

**Fluctuation.**

He examined the notes.

There was a particular property of Fluctuation that he had been considering for some time.

It wasn't simply ordinary magical energy.

Its nature was connected to something considerably more fundamental.

Arcanum.

Or, more precisely, the residue of arcanum.

Bell leaned forward.

*If I'm understanding this correctly...*

He began drawing a diagram.

A simple comparison emerged in his mind.

Cooking oil.

Used cooking oil.

They weren't entirely different substances.

The latter had undergone changes through its previous use. Its original condition had been altered, and its properties were no longer identical to those of fresh oil.

But it hadn't ceased being oil.

It was still recognizable as the same underlying substance, albeit in a degraded state.

That was how Bell understood the relationship between arcanum and Fluctuation.

Fluctuation was not equivalent to pure divine arcanum.

It was a residual manifestation.

Something that had already undergone a change in state.

Weaker.

Less refined.

And considerably different in its original potency.

But not entirely disconnected from its source.

Bell tapped his pen against the paper.

*That's the interesting part.*

If Fluctuation was indeed a residual form of arcanum, then it might explain why it remained capable of producing effects that ordinary magical energy couldn't easily replicate.

Its potency was diminished.

But diminished didn't mean harmless.

Especially when dealing with beings from the lower realm.

A quantity of power that might be insignificant to a deity could still be overwhelming to a mortal.

And Ein's condition was precisely the kind of problem where that distinction mattered.

Bell began writing again.

**Method Two — Controlled use of divine arcanum.**

He paused.

Then added another line.

**Requires divine authorization.**

His expression became thoughtful.

Unlike the first method, this approach might allow him to employ a much more direct purification effect.

He wouldn't necessarily need to compensate for the enormous mana requirements through his own reserves.

But there was a problem.

Arcanum wasn't something mortals could casually manipulate.

It belonged to the gods.

Even if Fluctuation contained traces of that power, the underlying source remained divine.

And the spell he intended to employ wasn't something he could simply activate through conventional magical means.

It required arcanum.

Not an ordinary quantity of mana.

Not an elaborate chant.

Not a conventional spell structure.

Something fundamentally different.

Bell looked at the ceiling.

"Well..."

A small smile appeared on his face.

"That's going to require a conversation."

---

### III. A Spell Without a Name

Bell moved toward the larger workbench.

He cleared a space and retrieved a collection of notes concerning the purification effect he had been studying.

The spell was unusual.

In fact, calling it a conventional spell was probably misleading.

It possessed no established chant.

No elaborate sequence of words.

No conventional name.

There was simply the intended effect.

A direct application of divine power toward the restoration of a target's original condition.

At least, that was how Bell understood its operation.

He examined his notes again.

*If I can direct it properly, the corruption should be the only thing affected.*

That was the objective.

Not indiscriminate destruction.

Not an overwhelming release of divine energy.

Purification.

The removal of something that didn't belong.

However, the absence of a chant didn't mean the absence of requirements.

Quite the opposite.

The spell demanded an extraordinary degree of control.

Without a conventional chant or established magical structure to regulate its activation, the user would have to understand the intended effect and its limitations.

The power itself wasn't the difficult part.

Directing it correctly was.

Bell began writing several precautions.

- Establish the precise target.
- Prevent interference with the host's original biological structures.
- Restrict the effect to the artificial corruption.
- Maintain a stable connection throughout the process.
- Avoid unnecessary expansion of the affected area.

He stopped writing.

There was another consideration.

The spell could theoretically heal anyone from the lower realm.

That was an extraordinary capability.

But it didn't mean the effect was automatically safe under every circumstance.

A divine power capable of restoring an injured mortal could also overwhelm one if applied carelessly.

The difference between healing and damaging something was not always determined by the nature of the power itself.

Sometimes, it depended entirely on how that power was used.

Bell looked at his notes.

*Which brings me back to the same problem.*

He needed permission.

Not because he was incapable of understanding the spell.

Not because he was asking someone to perform it for him.

He needed authorization to use something that belonged to the divine realm.

And there was only one person he considered appropriate to approach.

Uranus.

Bell's expression softened.

His grandfather.

The ancient Being who had guided him during his earlier development, answered questions when he could, and repeatedly reminded him that possessing knowledge did not mean possessing every answer.

Their relationship was somewhat unusual.

But Bell had no intention of treating it casually.

If he wanted to use divine arcanum, he would ask properly.

He closed the notebook.

"All right."

He glanced toward Ein.

"Let's see what Grandfather has to say."

---

### IV. A Short Journey

Bell stood in the middle of the laboratory.

He had already prepared everything he needed.

The research documents were arranged neatly on the desk. The examination chamber was functioning normally, and the protective formations remained stable.

He checked the readings one final time.

There was no reason to expect any immediate deterioration.

Still, he wasn't particularly comfortable leaving the laboratory unattended.

He adjusted the surrounding formations and reinforced the monitoring structures.

The mana supply remained stable.

The suspended animation formation continued operating.

Everything was ready.

Bell stepped away from the table.

Then he concentrated.

Teleportation was one of the abilities he had developed and incorporated into his repertoire.

However, it wasn't an unrestricted method of movement.

There were conditions.

First, he needed to have visited the destination previously.

Second, he needed to know its location.

And finally, the distance mattered.

The farther away the destination was, the greater the difficulty and the more demanding the process became.

It wasn't an ability that allowed him to travel anywhere simply by imagining a place.

He needed a genuine spatial reference.

Fortunately, he already possessed one.

The prayer room of Uranus.

It was located in Orario.

He had visited the place before.

He knew its location.

And compared to travelling across the continent, the distance was hardly troublesome.

Bell raised his hand.

The surrounding space began to distort.

A faint magical vibration passed through the laboratory.

The edges of the room seemed to shift, as though the air itself had briefly forgotten its original arrangement.

Then—

The distortion expanded.

A thin boundary formed around Bell's body.

And the next instant, he disappeared.

---

The prayer room remained as quiet as ever.

The familiar atmosphere of the Guild's deepest sanctuary greeted him.

The architecture was simple, yet there was something unmistakably ancient about the place.

Its stone walls carried the weight of countless years.

The enormous statue of Uranus stood in its usual position, overlooking the chamber with an expression that seemed to possess an understanding far beyond ordinary comprehension.

Bell appeared several steps away.

He looked around.

Everything was as he remembered.

He adjusted his clothing and approached the statue.

Then he noticed something.

A familiar presence.

"Well, this is unexpected."

The voice came from behind him.

Bell turned.

Uranus was there.

The ancient Being regarded him with a gentle expression.

For a moment, Bell simply stared.

Then he smiled.

"Good afternoon, Grandfather."

Uranus's expression brightened.

"Bell."

There was genuine pleasure in his voice.

He approached his great-grandson.

"I must admit, I wasn't expecting a visit today."

"Is that a problem?"

"Not at all."

Uranus chuckled.

"On the contrary. It is rather pleasant to see you arrive without being accompanied by an impending catastrophe."

Bell scratched his cheek.

"That's a rather specific way of describing my previous visits."

"Have I said something inaccurate?"

"...No."

Uranus laughed.

Bell couldn't help but smile.

For a moment, the atmosphere became considerably lighter.

The two stood facing one another, separated by the considerable difference in their existence but connected through a rather unusual family relationship.

Uranus examined him.

"You appear well."

"I'm doing fine."

"That is reassuring."

He paused.

"Although, judging by your expression, I suspect you haven't come merely to exchange greetings."

Bell gave a small laugh.

"You're right."

"Then I shall spare you the formalities. What brings you here?"

Bell's expression became serious.

"I need your permission to use arcanum."

The change in atmosphere was immediate.

Uranus regarded him silently.

Not with suspicion.

Rather, with the attention of someone who had suddenly recognized the importance of the subject being discussed.

"Arcanum?"

Bell nodded.

"Yes."

"That is not a request I hear from mortals very often."

"I imagine not."

Uranus folded his hands behind his back.

"Then I presume you have a reason."

"I do."

Bell looked directly at him.

"And I'd like to explain it properly before asking you to decide."

Uranus smiled.

"Very well. Let us hear it."

---

### V. The Researcher's Report

Bell began by explaining the circumstances surrounding Ein.

He didn't immediately discuss the spell.

Instead, he described the results of his previous experiment.

The corruption.

Its distribution.

The artificial network.

The extraction procedure.

And the limitations he had encountered.

Uranus listened without interrupting.

Bell continued.

"I've managed to remove a substantial portion of the corruption."

"Successfully?"

"Yes. The extracted material remains active even after separation, but the procedure itself works."

"Then what is preventing you from completing the process?"

"The heart and brain."

Bell retrieved his notes.

He handed several pages to Uranus.

"The remaining concentrations are deeply integrated with the biological structures responsible for maintaining her life. Removing them through conventional extraction would risk fatal damage."

Uranus examined the documents.

His expression became thoughtful.

"Then you have reached the limits of your current method."

"More or less."

Bell pointed toward one of the diagrams.

"I've also confirmed that high-tier healing magic can reconstruct the damaged tissue after extraction. That part works. But it doesn't solve the problem of removing the corruption from structures that cannot safely withstand the procedure."

"I see."

Uranus returned his attention to the documents.

"And what do you intend to do?"

"I have two possibilities."

Bell explained the first.

High-tier cleansing magic.

The spell would target the corruption directly, potentially neutralizing it without requiring the same degree of physical extraction.

However, the mana consumption would be considerable.

He explained how he intended to draw mana from the surrounding environment, establish a controlled formation, and regulate the energy supply.

Uranus listened carefully.

"An ambitious undertaking."

"That's one way to describe it."

"And the second possibility?"

Bell hesitated briefly.

Then he turned another page.

"Fluctuation."

Uranus's eyes narrowed slightly.

"Continue."

"I've been studying its properties. From what I've observed, Fluctuation isn't entirely disconnected from arcanum. I believe it's a residual manifestation of it."

He drew a simple diagram.

"Think of it like cooking oil and used cooking oil. The latter has already undergone changes, but it hasn't stopped being oil. Its original properties have been altered, but the underlying substance remains recognizable."

Uranus examined the diagram.

Bell continued.

"Fluctuation is considerably weaker than divine arcanum in its original state. But it still retains properties associated with its source."

He tapped the page.

"And even a diminished quantity of divine power can be extraordinarily potent when applied to beings from the lower realm."

Uranus remained silent.

Bell took that as an invitation to continue.

"I don't intend to use it indiscriminately. The spell I've been researching is a direct purification effect. It doesn't require a conventional chant or an established spell name."

He paused.

"There's no elaborate activation sequence. The effect is direct, and the power comes from arcanum."

Uranus raised his gaze.

"You are describing a rather unusual application."

"I know."

"Do you understand the implications?"

"Not entirely. That's partly why I'm here."

Bell smiled faintly.

"I've studied the theoretical side, but I don't want to pretend that understanding the mechanism automatically means I can use it safely."

Uranus regarded him for several moments.

Then he looked toward the documents again.

"What exactly do you intend to purify?"

"The artificial corruption inside Ein."

"And nothing else?"

"That's the objective."

Bell's expression became firm.

"I don't intend to erase her transformation indiscriminately or interfere with her original identity. The target is the foreign structure that has invaded her body."

Uranus nodded slowly.

"Then your distinction is an important one."

Bell folded his arms.

"That's why I need to be careful."

---

### VI. The Weight of Divine Authority

Uranus placed the documents on a nearby surface.

For a moment, he remained silent.

Then he spoke.

"Bell, there is something you should understand about arcanum."

Bell straightened.

"Yes?"

"It is not merely another form of magical energy."

Uranus looked directly at him.

"Divine arcanum belongs to the gods. Its existence is fundamentally different from the power ordinarily employed by mortals."

Bell nodded.

"I understand that much."

"Do you?"

Uranus's voice remained gentle.

"Then consider what you are asking."

He raised one hand.

A faint presence emerged.

It wasn't an overwhelming manifestation.

There was no violent surge of energy, nor did the room tremble beneath the weight of his existence.

Yet Bell could perceive the difference.

The power was unmistakable.

Arcanum.

"Even a restrained expression of divine authority possesses consequences beyond what ordinary mortals can easily comprehend."

Uranus lowered his hand.

"The gods are capable of restricting their power, but that does not mean every application is harmless. The manner in which it is employed matters."

Bell listened.

Uranus continued.

"Your proposed spell may be direct, but its simplicity does not make it uncomplicated. You intend to apply divine power to an individual whose internal structures have already been compromised."

"Yes."

"And if your understanding of the corruption is incomplete?"

"Then I might affect something I didn't intend to."

"Precisely."

Uranus studied him.

"Your research suggests that the corruption has become integrated with her heart and brain. You must consider whether the purification effect can distinguish between the foreign structure and the living system supporting it."

Bell nodded.

"That's one of my primary concerns."

"Then why do you believe arcanum is the appropriate solution?"

Bell took a moment to answer.

"Because the problem isn't necessarily a lack of power."

He glanced at his notes.

"It's a problem of compatibility and precision."

Uranus waited.

"The corruption is artificial. It behaves like an independent network, but it has established itself inside a living organism. Conventional methods can separate it, but they can't safely remove everything."

Bell looked at his grandfather.

"If I can employ an effect that directly targets the foreign structure, I might be able to neutralize the remaining corruption without physically extracting it."

"And if it fails?"

"Then I'll stop."

Uranus raised an eyebrow.

Bell continued.

"I'm not going to keep forcing the spell simply because I want the experiment to succeed. I'll establish the necessary restrictions beforehand, monitor the process, and make sure the existing support formations remain operational."

He paused.

"That's also why I haven't attempted it yet."

Uranus regarded him.

Then, unexpectedly, he smiled.

"Good."

Bell blinked.

"Good?"

"Indeed."

Uranus approached him.

"At least you understand that possessing the ability to attempt something does not automatically grant you the wisdom to proceed."

Bell scratched his cheek.

"I've learned that the hard way."

"Have you?"

"Several times."

Uranus chuckled.

"Then perhaps the lessons are finally beginning to take effect."

Bell sighed.

"Grandfather..."

"Yes?"

"You're enjoying this."

"Immensely."

Bell shook his head.

For a moment, they both smiled.

But Uranus's expression gradually returned to its previous seriousness.

"Now, regarding your request."

Bell straightened.

"Yes?"

---

### VII. A Request Between Gods

Uranus folded his hands behind his back.

"You said you require my permission."

"That's correct."

"Not assistance?"

Bell shook his head.

"Not necessarily. I have already developed the theoretical framework and the necessary preparations. What I need is authorization to employ the arcanum."

Uranus considered his words.

"Then your request is rather straightforward."

"More or less."

Bell paused.

"Think of it as a god requesting permission to use another god's arcanum."

Uranus's expression softened.

"An interesting comparison."

He looked toward the distant ceiling.

"In the divine realm, such matters are not entirely unfamiliar. The authority to employ divine power is not something one should assume merely because the opportunity exists."

Bell nodded.

"That's what I thought."

Uranus returned his attention to him.

"And you believe that my approval is sufficient?"

"Yes."

There was no hesitation in Bell's response.

He had come here for a reason.

Uranus was the person he trusted to make that decision.

Not because he expected unconditional approval.

Rather, because he knew the ancient Being would consider the consequences before answering.

Uranus studied his great-grandson.

"You have already considered an alternative that would not require divine authorization?"

"Yes. The cleansing magic."

"And yet you came here."

"Because the other method might be more appropriate."

Bell smiled faintly.

"Besides, I'd rather ask than make assumptions about something that belongs to the gods."

Uranus remained silent.

Then he laughed softly.

"Bell."

"Yes?"

"You are asking for permission to employ a power that could overwhelm most mortals, and yet you speak as though you are requesting permission to borrow a book."

Bell looked slightly embarrassed.

"Well, I did say I wanted to ask properly."

"Indeed."

Uranus approached him.

His expression became warm.

"You have my approval."

Bell blinked.

"...Really?"

"Yes."

The answer was immediate.

But Uranus raised one finger before Bell could respond.

"However, I have conditions."

Bell straightened.

"Of course."

"First, you will not employ the arcanum carelessly. The fact that the intended effect is beneficial does not excuse an improper application."

"I understand."

"Second, you will ensure that the target is properly isolated. I do not want the surrounding environment or unrelated individuals affected by your experiment."

Bell nodded.

"Agreed."

"Third, you will not allow the procedure to proceed if you determine that Ein's life is in immediate danger."

"I won't."

Uranus looked at him for another moment.

"And finally..."

He paused.

"Do not mistake my permission for a guarantee of success."

Bell smiled.

"I wouldn't expect one."

Uranus chuckled.

"Good."

He placed a hand on Bell's shoulder.

"Then proceed with the necessary preparations. You have my authorization to use the arcanum for this purpose."

Bell lowered his head.

"Thank you, Grandfather."

Uranus smiled.

"You're welcome."

For a moment, the two remained silent.

Then Bell looked up.

"There's something else."

"Another request?"

"Not exactly."

He scratched his cheek.

"I'd like to clarify something about the Fluctuation."

Uranus raised an eyebrow.

"Go on."

"If it's a residual manifestation of arcanum, then does that mean its properties are still connected to the original divine power?"

"That is a reasonable interpretation."

"Even after its potency has diminished?"

"Yes. A change in state does not necessarily erase the nature of its origin."

Bell nodded.

"Then my theory isn't entirely wrong."

"Not entirely."

Uranus smiled.

"Although I would advise against assuming that every property of divine arcanum survives its transition into another state."

Bell immediately began taking notes.

"Understood."

"Bell."

"Yes?"

"You came here to request permission, not to conduct an entire research seminar."

Bell stopped writing.

"...Right."

Uranus laughed.

"Some things never change."

---

### VIII. A Grandfather's Warning

Their conversation continued for several minutes.

Bell explained some of the remaining details concerning his preparations, while Uranus offered observations about the nature of divine authority.

There were still many things neither of them could establish with certainty.

The exact interaction between Fluctuation and the original arcanum remained a subject requiring further investigation.

The precise behavior of the purification effect against an artificial magical network was also uncertain.

And, most importantly, neither of them could guarantee that the corruption would respond exactly as Bell expected.

But that was acceptable.

Bell hadn't come looking for a promise.

He had come looking for permission.

And he had received it.

Eventually, Uranus looked at him with a more thoughtful expression.

"How is Ein?"

Bell's expression softened.

"She's stable."

"Still suspended?"

"Yes. The formation is functioning properly. Her bodily processes are being maintained, and the extracted portions haven't begun regenerating."

"Then you have managed to preserve her condition."

"For now."

Bell glanced toward the documents.

"The remaining corruption is still there, though."

Uranus nodded.

"And you intend to remove it."

"That's the plan."

He paused.

"Although I suppose I should stop saying that until I've actually succeeded."

Uranus smiled.

"A sensible correction."

Bell laughed softly.

Then his expression became more serious.

"I don't want to keep her suspended indefinitely."

"I understand."

"I know it's necessary for now, but she deserves to wake up."

Uranus looked at him.

There was something unmistakably familiar in Bell's expression.

A mixture of determination and concern.

The same expression he had seen during their previous conversations.

The young man had always possessed an unusual tendency to take responsibility for problems that extended beyond his immediate capabilities.

Sometimes that quality was admirable.

Sometimes it was dangerous.

And occasionally, it was both.

"Bell."

"Yes?"

"Remember what I told you before."

Bell waited.

"Your abilities do not determine the value of your decisions. Nor does the existence of a possible solution mean you must pursue it regardless of the consequences."

Bell nodded.

"I remember."

"Then do not forget it simply because you have obtained permission to use a greater power."

Uranus's expression softened.

"Power can make difficult things possible. It cannot decide what should be done."

Bell considered the words.

Then he smiled.

"That's why I came to you."

Uranus chuckled.

"Flattery?"

"An observation."

"An increasingly dangerous habit."

Bell laughed.

The ancient Being shook his head.

For a moment, the atmosphere returned to its earlier warmth.

Then Bell stepped backward.

"I should get going. I have preparations to finish."

"Already?"

"Unfortunately."

Uranus sighed dramatically.

"How unfortunate. I was beginning to enjoy the company."

Bell smiled.

"We can talk again later."

"See that you do."

He paused.

"And Bell?"

"Yes, Grandfather?"

"Take care of yourself."

Bell's expression softened.

"I will."

Uranus nodded.

"Then go."

---

### IX. The Next Experiment

The laboratory returned to view.

Bell appeared in the same position he had occupied before leaving.

The teleportation process had been completed without difficulty.

He glanced around.

Everything remained undisturbed.

The protective formations continued functioning.

The instruments displayed stable readings.

And Ein remained suspended within the examination chamber.

Bell released a breath.

"Well..."

He stretched his arms.

"That went better than expected."

He approached the workbench and retrieved his notebook.

There was something satisfying about having the necessary authorization.

Not because it guaranteed success.

But because it removed one of the uncertainties surrounding the experiment.

He could now proceed with the preparations.

Bell turned to a fresh page.

**Test Two — Controlled Arcanum Purification.**

He began outlining the procedure.

First, he needed to establish the exact region he intended to affect.

Second, he would need to isolate the remaining corruption from the surrounding biological structures.

Third, he would establish the necessary restrictions to prevent the purification effect from spreading beyond its intended target.

Finally, he would introduce the arcanum.

Not all at once.

Not without measurement.

And certainly not without a means of terminating the process if something went wrong.

Bell paused.

There was another consideration.

The spell itself.

He examined the notes.

No chant.

No name.

No conventional magical construction.

Just the direct application of arcanum.

He tapped his pen against the paper.

*I should probably give the procedure a proper designation.*

He considered several possibilities.

Then shook his head.

*No. Naming it can wait.*

There were more important things to accomplish.

He turned toward Ein.

The artificial network remained embedded within her body.

The corruption was still there, hidden beneath the surface, maintaining its connections to the very structures that kept her alive.

Bell approached the examination chamber.

He examined the readings.

Stable.

He placed one hand against the table.

"Ein."

He knew she couldn't hear him.

Still, he spoke.

"I got permission."

A faint smile appeared on his face.

"From Grandfather, no less."

He looked at the formations.

"I think I've found another way to help you."

There was no response.

The laboratory remained quiet.

Bell lowered his hand.

"Don't worry. I'm not going to rush it."

He turned toward the workbench.

There was still a considerable amount of preparation ahead.

The cleansing method remained an alternative.

The arcanum-based spell required careful handling.

And the relationship between Fluctuation and divine power deserved further examination before he attempted anything particularly ambitious.

He had permission.

He had a theoretical solution.

And he had a reason to proceed.

But one thing remained unchanged.

The person he was trying to save was still lying in that chamber.

Bell looked at the diagrams one final time.

*All right.*

*Let's see if this works.*

He picked up his pen.

The next experiment would determine whether the power of the divine realm could accomplish what ordinary magic had failed to achieve.

Not by overwhelming the corruption.

Not by destroying the body that sustained it.

But by separating the two without sacrificing the person trapped between them.

For the first time since beginning his investigation, Bell possessed an option that might finally address the problem at its source.

And he intended to use it carefully.

Because this time, he wasn't merely testing a new magical technique.

He was attempting to bring someone back.
