# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

## Chapter Eighty — Melen

The sea was quiet.

Perhaps a little too quiet.

Bell stood near the edge of the vessel, watching the coastline gradually approach. The sunlight reflected against the water, creating a beautiful spectacle of blue and gold. The wind was gentle, the waves were calm, and there wasn't a cloud in sight.

It was picturesque.

Almost suspiciously so.

"Beautiful, isn't it?"

Ein stood beside him, her white hair moving gently with the breeze.

Bell glanced at her.

"It is."

"You're not looking at it."

"I'm looking at it."

"You're looking for something."

He smiled faintly.

"Is there a difference?"

"Quite a considerable one, actually."

Ein folded her arms, amused by his response.

Bell returned his attention to the coastline.

Melen.

A port city with a reputation for its beautiful beaches and maritime trade. It was an attractive destination, particularly during the warmer seasons.

Yet something bothered him.

There were ships.

There were buildings.

There were people moving along the distant harbor.

But the sea itself seemed strangely vacant.

No visible activity beyond the occasional fishing vessel. No disturbances beneath the surface. No unusual movement along the coastline.

Even the birds seemed unusually scarce.

Perhaps he was overthinking.

That possibility wasn't particularly comforting.

"Ein."

"Hm?"

"Once we arrive, I want you to survey the surrounding beach."

She raised an eyebrow.

"Looking for something specific?"

"Anything that doesn't belong."

"That's rather broad."

"I know."

Bell smiled.

"That's why I'm asking you."

Ein shook her head.

"You're becoming increasingly troublesome."

"That's unfortunate."

"Very."

Despite her complaint, she nodded.

The vessel eventually reached the harbor.

Bell and Ein disembarked without attracting much attention. They collected their belongings and made their way toward the outskirts of the city.

He wasn't particularly interested in finding accommodation.

A proper investigation required a degree of independence, and the city's establishments were hardly ideal for that purpose.

Instead, Bell searched for an elevated position overlooking the surrounding coastline.

It took some time.

Eventually, he found a suitable location.

A raised section of land overlooking the beach, with enough vegetation to provide concealment and sufficient visibility to monitor the surrounding area.

"Here."

Ein looked around.

"You're planning to stay here?"

"For the time being."

"How practical."

"I thought so."

He placed his belongings down and began preparing the campsite.

The work was relatively straightforward.

He established the sleeping arrangements, organized their supplies, checked the surrounding terrain, and arranged a small cooking area.

He also made certain that their equipment remained accessible.

A campsite was more than somewhere to sleep.

It was a temporary base.

And temporary bases needed to be functional.

By the time he finished, the afternoon sun had begun descending.

Bell looked toward the beach.

"Ein?"

No response.

He turned around.

She was nowhere nearby.

He wasn't particularly concerned.

She had already mentioned that she intended to survey the surrounding area.

Still, he waited.

Several minutes later, Ein returned.

"Nothing."

Bell looked at her.

"Nothing?"

"Nothing suspicious along the beach. No unusual tracks, no strange magical traces, and no apparent disturbances."

She approached the campsite.

"The surrounding area appears peaceful."

Bell nodded.

"Interesting."

Ein tilted her head.

"You don't sound convinced."

"I'm not."

"Why?"

He glanced toward the sea.

"Because peaceful doesn't necessarily mean safe."

A faint breeze passed through the vegetation.

Ein followed his gaze.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then she looked back at him.

"Well, what do you intend to do?"

Bell smiled.

"Enjoy the scenery."

She stared at him.

"...You're joking."

"Of course."

He returned to arranging their supplies.

Ein sighed.

Sometimes, she wondered whether he deliberately made things difficult for himself.

And sometimes, she wondered whether he simply couldn't help it.

Either way, she had already learned one thing.

A peaceful vacation with Bell Cranel was probably too much to ask.

**End of Chapter Eighty.**
