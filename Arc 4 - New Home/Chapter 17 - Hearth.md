# DANMACHI: RETELLING OF THE LEGEND

## Chapter Seventeen — Hearth

**[AUTHOR-REQUESTED REPOSITORY DRAFT — NOT FINALIZED / LOCKED]**

### I. A Familia of Two

Bell stood before the small goddess with his hands clasped behind his back.

Hestia stared at him.

Bell stared back.

Neither spoke.

The silence continued for several seconds.

“...So,” Hestia finally said, pointing at herself. “You want to join *my* Familia?”

“Yes.”

“You do realize I don't have a Familia, right?”

“Yes.”

“You realize I don't have a single member?”

“Yes.”

“I don't even have money.”

“I noticed.”

Hestia's eyebrow twitched.

“Then why are you asking me?!”

Bell tilted his head.

“Because you accepted me.”

That answer completely disarmed her.

Hestia blinked.

Bell continued.

“I don't need a powerful Familia. I don't need a famous one. I just need somewhere I can belong.”

Hestia stared at him for a long moment.

Then her expression softened.

“You really are strange, Bell Cranel.”

“I've been told that before.”

She smiled.

“Fine.”

Bell's eyes widened slightly.

“You'll join?”

“Of course!”

Hestia puffed out her chest proudly.

“From this moment onward, you are the first member of the Hestia Familia!”

Bell looked at her.

“...The first?”

“The first!”

“So technically, this Familia didn't exist until now.”

“Don't ruin the moment!”

Bell smiled faintly.

“Understood.”

Hestia placed both hands on her hips.

“And don't think you're getting away from the formal ceremony. I still need to give you my Falna.”

Bell nodded.

“That is actually the main reason I came.”

“Then what are we waiting for?”

### II. The Old Church

The journey to Hestia's residence was considerably less impressive than Bell had expected.

They eventually arrived at an old abandoned church.

Bell stopped.

He looked up.

The building was worn down, with cracked stone, broken windows, and weeds growing through portions of the structure.

“This is it?”

Hestia scratched the back of her head.

“Technically... yes.”

Bell walked through the entrance.

Dust covered nearly everything.

Old furniture was scattered around the interior, and some of the wooden structures had begun to rot.

Yet Bell didn't complain.

Instead, something about the place made him strangely nostalgic.

It reminded him of his old hideout.

Not because it looked the same.

Because it was *small.*

Quiet.

Hidden.

A place where nobody expected anything from him.

Bell walked deeper inside.

Hestia followed.

“There is a basement underneath,” she explained. “It's not very big, but there's enough room for three people.”

Bell inspected it.

The basement was crude, but structurally sound.

A few broken shelves.

An old table.

Two usable beds.

A storage area.

A small cooking space.

Bell looked around.

“We can work with this.”

Hestia stared at him.

“*We?*”

Bell picked up a broken wooden board.

“Yes.”

He examined it.

“This can be repaired.”

He pointed toward the shelves.

“These can be cleaned.”

Then toward the wall.

“That leak can be sealed.”

Hestia's eyes sparkled.

“You're going to help fix the church?”

“It's our home now.”

Hestia froze.

“Our... home?”

Bell nodded.

“Yes.”

For some reason, those two words hit Hestia harder than any compliment could have.

She turned her face away.

“...You're pretty good at saying things like that without realizing it.”

Bell didn't understand.

“What?”

“Nothing!”

### III. Making a Home

It took several hours.

Bell repaired what he could.

He cleaned the basement.

He reorganized the storage area.

He fixed the broken table and even managed to reinforce one of the damaged wooden supports.

Hestia watched him work.

“You've done this before.”

Bell paused.

“Yes.”

“Where?”

Bell looked toward the old wooden wall.

“Somewhere far away.”

Hestia noticed the change in his voice.

She didn't press.

Instead, she quietly handed him another piece of cloth.

“Then let's make this place better than wherever you came from.”

Bell looked at her.

A small smile appeared.

“I'd like that.”

Eventually, the work was finished.

Hestia stood in the center of the basement and looked around.

It wasn't beautiful.

It wasn't luxurious.

But it was clean.

It was warm.

It was livable.

For the first time in a long while, Bell felt that he had somewhere to return to.

Hestia turned around.

“Bell.”

“Yes?”

“It's time.”

Bell nodded.

“The Falna.”

Hestia's expression became serious.

“Lie down.”

Bell did so.

Hestia sat behind him.

“Don't move.”

“I won't.”

“Ready?”

“Yes.”

Hestia closed her eyes.

Divine power began to flow.

### IV. The Blessing

Something changed.

The moment Hestia's Falna touched Bell's body, her expression vanished.

Her eyes widened.

“What...?”

The divine light spread across his back.

And then—

Hestia saw it.

Not merely his body.

Not merely his potential.

She saw **his soul.**

Her breath stopped.

It was enormous.

Not physically.

Something deeper.

The soul before her seemed to contain layers upon layers of experience that should not have existed within a fourteen-year-old boy.

Magic.

Combat.

Knowledge.

Instinct.

Alchemy.

Countless disciplines.

Countless memories.

And beneath all of it—

Something frighteningly calm.

Hestia's hands trembled.

“Bell...”

“Yes?”

“...What exactly are you?”

Bell turned his head slightly.

“A human.”

Hestia swallowed.

“Are you sure?”

Bell thought about it.

“Mostly.”

“*Mostly?!*”

The Falna continued.

Numbers began forming.

At first they flickered.

Then they changed.

Then changed again.

Hestia stared at the status appearing before her.

Her divine eyes struggled to interpret it.

Finally, the Falna stabilized.

### VI. A Goddess's Questions

Hestia stared.

“Five skills...”

Bell remained silent.

She continued reading.

When she reached the magic section, she slowly lowered the parchment.

“Bell.”

“Yes?”

“Where did you learn all of this?”

Bell stared at the ceiling.

“Practice.”

“...That's not an answer.”

“It is technically an answer.”

Hestia sighed.

“You're impossible.”

But then Hestia noticed another section.

Her expression became blank.

“There's more.”

Bell looked at her.

“More?”

“Developmental Abilities.”

She stared at the list.

And stared.

And stared.

Finally, Hestia slowly lowered the parchment.

“...Bell.”

“Yes?”

“This isn't a Falna.”

Bell blinked.

“What is it?”

Hestia stared at him.

“It's a résumé.”

Bell couldn't help himself.

He laughed.

A genuine laugh.

Hestia stared for a moment.

Then she smiled.

“Fine.”

She placed the parchment down.

“Whatever you are...”

She gently placed her hand on Bell's head.

“Welcome to the Hestia Familia.”

Bell's smile faded slightly.

His eyes softened.

“Thank you.”

Hestia smiled.

“And Bell?”

“Yes?”

“You said you wanted a place to belong.”

“Yes.”

She pointed toward the basement.

“Then stop calling it *the church*.”

Bell looked around.

Hestia grinned.

“It's our home now.”

Bell looked around once more.

The old stone.

The repaired table.

The small beds.

The dim light.

Nothing about the place was impressive.

Yet somehow—

It felt different.

For the first time since arriving in Orario, Bell didn't feel like he was merely passing through.

He had a goddess.

He had a Familia.

And now—

He had a **hearth**.

Bell quietly nodded.

“...Our home.”

Above them, the old church remained silent.

But beneath its broken roof, something new had begun.

The smallest Familia in Orario had just gained its first adventurer.

And neither Bell nor Hestia yet understood what that meant.

Because the status written upon Bell's back wasn't merely powerful.

It was something the gods of Orario had never seen before.
